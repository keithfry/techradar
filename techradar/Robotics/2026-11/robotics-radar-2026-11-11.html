PRISM humanoid control module improves simulated walking

A neural network input module named PRISM improved simulated humanoid walking by enabling models to complete 92.5% of 24-second trials without falling. PRISM achieved this by learning multiplicative relationships among observations, past actions, and commands, offering a more explicit representation of physical interactions. This architectural improvement resulted in superior performance compared to conventional multilayer perceptrons, even when friction, mass, and payload were altered. The module's effectiveness was shown across various physical conditions without requiring retraining. PRISM's learned representations were found to make quantities like joint power and contact forces more predictable.

How Much Work Do Robots Really Deliver? The Thinking Behind Worken

AGIBOT proposes Worken, a framework to measure the useful work robots actually complete, moving beyond simple task completion. Worken calculates productivity using the formula: Active Robots $\times$ Autonomous Working Hours $\times$ Output Efficiency Ratio. This framework accounts for how independently robots operate and the quality of the output against set benchmarks. It aims to evaluate the practical contribution of physical AI in real-world settings by focusing on effective work delivered. Worken seeks to provide a clearer measure of robotic productivity, acknowledging that operating time alone is not equivalent to productive output.

Ep#116: SimEX: Simulation-Integrated Robotics AutoResearch

SimEX is a simulation-based auto-research framework designed to help coding agents acquire physical capabilities for controlling real robots. It operates in two stages: first, the agent performs open-ended probe-and-optimize iterations in simulation to develop a robot skill toolbox. Second, the agent adapts this toolbox by performing a few physical trials, using simulation to correct the simulator and diagnose failures. This method allows agents to efficiently learn complex skills for tasks like towel folding and plate manipulation with minimal real-world interaction. The research suggests that simulation is a critical component for achieving physical intelligence by enabling agents to develop knowledge and procedures.

Maze Runner: Autonomous Maze Navigation with Line-Based Localization and Control Barrier Functions

Maze Runner presents an end-to-end autonomy stack for navigating mazes using line-based localization and control barrier functions. Perception extracts wall lines from LiDAR scans using RANSAC and total least squares to estimate per-line uncertainty. Two Extended Kalman Filters are used for localization, one tracking a line map and another performing online SLAM. Planning uses an inflated occupancy grid with A* or Hybrid A* for path generation, while a Nonlinear Model Predictive Controller tracks the path with CBFs ensuring safety. Simulation results show high accuracy and fast planning/control execution times.

CAFE: Closed-Loop Autonomous Task Formulation and Execution without Instance-Specific Instructions

CAFE addresses scene-driven robot autonomy by formulating and executing tasks without instance-specific instructions. It maintains an online memory of objects, functional zones, and execution states to guide the process. A rule-guided Trigger-LLM facilitates task formulation from accumulated evidence, with Draft, Complete, and Audit modules proposing goals and checking dependencies. Outcomes update the memory, enabling correction and subsequent discovery. Evaluations on 12 scenes show that the Tri-LLM significantly outperformed baselines in task complexity and execution metrics.

Robo-MLT: Dual-System Robotic Manipulation for Long-Horizon Medical Laboratory Tasks

Robo-MLT is a dual-system framework designed for reliable, long-horizon robotic manipulation in medical laboratory tasks. It combines explicit workflow monitoring with learned action generation to handle dynamic changes during execution. A vision-language planner generates skill sequences from protocols and observations, while a monitor tracks skill completion and triggers replanning. A skill-conditioned policy generates manipulation trajectories coordinated by an asynchronous runtime. Evaluation showed Robo-MLT achieved high task success rates by leveraging explicit workflow monitoring for enhanced reliability.

DOBB: Dynamic Oriented Bounding Boxes for Multi-Agent Collision Avoidance under State Uncertainty and Heterogeneous Dynamics

Learning-based collision avoidance methods must handle state uncertainty and heterogeneous agent dynamics reliably. The Dynamic Oriented Bounding Box (DOBB) is introduced as an online geometric representation that incorporates state estimates, uncertainty, and agent characteristics into a dynamic safety margin. DOBB derives geometric features characterizing agent interactions and a reward function that maintains physical meaning. Evaluation across four environments showed DOBB achieved a low mean total failure rate of 2.6% under out-of-distribution shifts. This robustness stems from the geometric abstraction of the safety margin, reducing coupling to specific uncertainty or actuation regimes.

How Much of Your VLA Is kNN

Vision-language-action (VLA) models achieve high performance, but the extent to which this relies on training demonstrations is unclear. Retrieving nearby demonstrations and reusing their action chunks achieves 91.7% success on the LIBERO benchmark without policy training. Fine-tuning often improves the vision-language backbone while keeping actions close to demonstrations across various VLAs. A metric called Local Action Consistency (LAC) was introduced to measure action similarity between nearby observations. LAC tracks retrieval success and can guide the training of a retrieval key.

Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control for Underactuated Upright Physical Assistance

Ground Reaction Force-Aware Model Predictive Control (GRF-MPC) is presented as a framework for upright physical assistance by incorporating live ground reaction forces and center of pressure into a human dynamics model. This method formulates a real-time quadratic program to optimize assistive forces over a receding horizon. Experiments on an underactuated robot demonstrated that GRF-MPC improved tracking, reduced mechanical work, and increased voluntary excursion compared to a fully actuated baseline. The control system achieved fast solving times, enabling high-frequency replanning while providing stronger assistance with fewer actuators. Overall, integrating human-ground interaction results in more transparent physical assistance.

arXiv - cs.RO

Latent Barrier Steering: Hierarchical Safety for Generative Planning

Latent Barrier Steering (LBS) is a hierarchical framework for safe flow-matching-based generative planning. It decouples behavior selection from path certification by steering the generator's latent toward larger safety margins first. Subsequently, it applies a path-space Quadratic Program only for residual safety violations. This approach increases the margin for the path corrector, leading to finite-time recovery guarantees. Experiments demonstrate that LBS improves goal reaching and reduces correction burden compared to traditional path-level safety filters.

Incorporating World Models in Decision Making for Next Best View Selection in Active Sensing

Pretrained world models can guide next-best-view selection for efficient robotic 3D reconstruction in active sensing. A deployment adaptation methodology is introduced that learns a sensor-return adapter using limited local calibration data. This adapter estimates measurement dropout across predicted surfaces, enabling the selection method to favor views that fill data gaps. Experiments show the adapted method achieves higher surface coverage with 60% fewer captures than nominal selection. This demonstrates that robots can effectively reuse predictive models while adapting decisions to local operating conditions.
