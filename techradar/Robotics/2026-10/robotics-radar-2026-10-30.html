The Sequence Robotics - Issue #962: Rhoda AI and Robots That Predict Their Next Move

Rhoda AI bets that predicting short sensory futures can organize robot behavior. A separate inverse dynamics model translates these predicted futures into actual movements. Recent reports show better video prediction leads to stronger bearing-unpacking performance, and tactile data extends this capability to touch. The core challenge for Rhoda AI is ensuring these training results translate to sustained factory operation. The company is focusing on translating sensory predictions into executable robot actions using video and tactile data.

HALO Teaches Humanoids to Coordinate With Human Partners During Shared Carrying Tasks

HALO research investigates how humanoids can coordinate with human partners during physically coupled tasks like shared carrying. The project focuses on addressing the difficulty of independent learning undermining coordination by adjusting learning updates to align with a shared objective. Demonstrations show HALO collaborating with a human partner in tasks involving pushing, moving through spaces, and handling long objects. Comparative results indicate HALO performs well in confined-space transport and reduces post-halt drift relative to other methods. Despite promising results, the findings are based on limited trials, leaving broader reliability questions for real-world humanoid deployment.

AGIBOT GO-3 Preview Links Robot Pre-Training, Practice and Learning on the Job

AGIBOT introduced GO-3 Preview, an embodied foundation model that integrates reasoning, prediction, and action using the WRAM architecture. Learning is achieved through three stages involving Genie Evolver for post-training and Genie Master for deployment experience. The system uses various mechanisms to combine visual representations, future state prediction, and action generation to learn transferable robot capabilities. AGIBOT reports first-place benchmark rankings on RoboDojo, RoboTwin, and RoboColiseum and plans to fully open-source the model by the end of 2026.

How AGIBOT’s Physical Reasoning Dataset Connects Planning With Robot Actions

AGIBOT's Physical Reasoning dataset connects planning with robot actions by showcasing how a single instruction requires varied physical execution based on context. Examples demonstrate that tasks involve decision-making regarding object quantities, memory for past searches, and assessing action preconditions like space. Annotations link high-level goals to detailed action trajectories and spatial relationships, providing multi-layered training signals for different reasoning abilities. The dataset includes unsuccessful attempts and levels to help evaluate a system's ability to handle deviations and recover. Ultimately, it aims to study how decision-making and physical manipulation integrate to enable more robust robot learning.

Bleu Robotics Raises €5M to Teach Industrial Humanoids New Tasks in Under an Hour

Bleu Robotics raised €5 million in pre-seed funding to develop software capable of teaching industrial humanoids new tasks in under an hour. The process involves gathering human demonstrations, followed by execution, with the claimed workflow taking about 40 minutes. The system utilizes "abductive learning" to minimize the necessary examples needed for task learning. The company aims to deploy this technology in industrial settings by offering software subscriptions to robot manufacturers. Future plans involve testing the system for reliability across changing production conditions.

A Little Wiggle Helps Robots Learn the Ropes

A Wiggle and Go system allows robots to estimate the physical properties of ropes and cables by observing their movement. This method enables robots to plan movements more reliably by predicting rope behavior without prior practice. Computer vision algorithms track the wiggling motion, which informs a model to estimate properties like length, weight, and stiffness. This capability promises to improve robot handling in fields such as manufacturing, construction, and search and rescue. The research team successfully trained their model in simulation before applying the learned estimations to real-world robotics.
