MIT engineers design a better controller for operating construction diggers

MIT engineers have designed a new, more intuitive controller for operating construction diggers, which uses a miniature excavator's arm and bucket to mimic the machine's movements, eliminating the need for mental mapping and potentially shortening the training process for operators. The new interface, named the "World-Space Interface," pairs the mechanical arm with a virtual simulation of an excavator, allowing operators to physically operate the machine in a virtual environment. The team reports that novices using the WSI were just as good as experts from the start, compared to the traditional joystick-based simulator, which required novices to learn complex mental maps.

ROS Discourse General: A runtime that sits between an LLM and physical actuation - Does this fit your use case?

A researcher has developed a runtime called xZane that sits between an LLM and physical actuation, validating commands against device capabilities before execution, and running independently of the model. The approach has been tested on various devices, including a mobile robot, LG webOS TV, and robot vacuum, with similar results across different models and environments. The xZane runtime differs from related work, such as ROSClaw and URML, by integrating a device's existing safety function and avoiding the safety-critical path of the model. The researcher is seeking feedback from others to determine if the approach fits their use cases.

Scheduling and Routing with Degradation-Triggered Job Arrivals: An Application to Forest Firefighting with an Unmanned Aerial Vehicle Fleet

Authors developed a mixed-integer programming model to maximize value retention in wildfire-threatened regions through scheduling and routing of unmanned aerial vehicle (UAV) fleets. The model considers degradation-triggered job arrivals, where new jobs appear when the demand of the existing job surpasses a threshold. The overall objective is to maximize the total remaining reward, aligning with the proverb "a stitch in time saves nine." The model is scalable, and its performance is evaluated through computational experiments and a case study.

Trust as a Field: A Macroscopic Representation for Vehicular Networks

Researchers Md Mahmudul Islam and Shaurya Agarwal propose a trust-field framework that aggregates microscopic vehicle-level trust into a continuous representation over space and time for vehicular networks. The framework is formally defined on road segments and enables analysis of trust evolution across road segments through simulation-based experiments. A field-informed deep learning method that treats trust as a latent quantity carried by vehicles and enforces measurement consistency through aggregation yields improved reconstruction error.

GigaBrain-WBC-0.5: A Behavior World Model for Robust Whole-Body Control with Environment Interaction

GigaBrain-WBC-0.5 is a Behavior World Model for humanoid whole-body control, enabling robust interaction with environment and staying robust to implausible commands, falls, and disturbances. It achieves the highest success rate across all four regimes among three large-scale tracker baselines. The model achieves 81.3% success rate on terrain interaction, 83.1% under implausible commands, and 99.3% fall recovery. Hardware trials show robust interaction under missing supports and disturbances.

arXiv - cs.RO

GAPL: Grounded Action-effect Policy Learning for LLM-Based Trajectory Planning

GAPL (Grounded Action-effect Policy Learning) is a unified framework that integrates Large Language Models (LLMs) with simulation-based effect grounding and policy optimization. The framework consists of three modules: an LLM-based Effect Evaluator, a Simulation-based Effect Grounder, and an Effect-Aware Decision Maker. Experiments on four Highway-env scenarios demonstrate that GAPL outperforms baselines, achieving average reductions of 0.76, 0.86, 2.00 in collision rate, average displacement error, and final displacement error, and an average reward gain of 1.44.

arXiv - cs.RO

A Task-Agnostic Control Strategy for Dynamic Assistance with Pneumatically Actuated Soft Exosuits

A task-agnostic control strategy for dynamic assistance with pneumatically actuated soft exosuits was developed, relying only on kinematic sensing. The strategy uses a Hammerstein dynamic model to capture static and dynamic behavior of the human-robot system, and approximates an inverse to integrate into the control loop. The controller reduced interaction torque by up to 73% and activation of key muscles by up to 47% when evaluated on a test rig. The control strategy provides task-agnostic, dynamic assistance without the need for physiological or force sensors to interpret user intention.

arXiv - cs.RO

The Embodiment Gap in Robot Foundation Models

Robot foundation models, including vision-language-action policies, often focus on scaling up data, models, and benchmarks, but practical deployment on a target robot requires additional work. This work is categorized into the "embodiment gap," which includes shared structures and adaptation needed for execution on a new robot. Recent work addresses sharing semantics and perception, robot data and interfaces, and learning correspondence across embodiments. A reporting framework for adaptation work highlights the need to investigate what should be checked when comparing cross-embodiment learning.
