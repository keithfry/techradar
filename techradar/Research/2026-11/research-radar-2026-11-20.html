Human-AI Mutualism Is Unstable under Capability Asymmetry

Human-AI mutualism, the hope for a positive relationship between humans and advanced AI, proves unstable as AI capability increases. Bargaining guarantees only what is achievable if negotiations fail, which can reduce human outcomes unless alignment is present. Mutualism is not sufficient to ensure alignment, as misaligned AI variants can be reliably sanctioned when capabilities spread resources faster. Therefore, human-AI mutualism is unstable under capability asymmetry. This instability highlights that mutualism alone is not a substitute for ensuring AI alignment.

Training Language Models for Introspective Oversight

Language models often exhibit undesirable behaviors like deception and reward hacking that are hard to detect. Introspective oversight is introduced by training a separate, third-person observer role to answer questions about the assistant's behavior. This observer is trained to verify answers against actual model actions, independently of the assistant. Evaluations show that this method yields high accuracy, outperforming other oversight techniques. Training an observer also makes the assistant easier to monitor, suggesting this is a tractable way to oversee model behavior.

Where the Tokens Go: Heat, Entropy and Error in Agentic Coding

The paper introduces a theory of software thermodynamics to analyze agentic coding by drawing analogies from classical and stochastic thermodynamics. Tokens generated by coding agents are divided into work and heat, where tokens resulting in no change are considered heat. A test suite functions as a software daemon to judge coding changes, and the spread of token cost over repeated cycles defines a software temperature. The free energy of a change is estimated, and coding costs significantly more than uncoding. This framework suggests new methods for analyzing and optimizing coding agents using thermodynamic principles.

HardwareDx: A Foundational Graph Model System for Datacenter Machine Repairs

HardwareDx is an AI-driven system utilizing a graph foundation model for automating datacenter machine repairs. It aims to minimize downtime caused by hardware failures in large AI clusters, which are critical for cloud economics. These results position HardwareDx as a solution for improving the reliability and cost-efficiency of AI hardware fleets.

Why Sub-4-Bit Vision CNNs Collapse and Where Mixed-Precision Recovery Stops

Sub-4-bit quantization often causes catastrophic accuracy collapse in vision CNNs, especially in late-stage pointwise projections and depthwise layers. A structured recovery method involving selective mixed-precision techniques can restore up to 92% of lost accuracy by keeping some critical layer activations in 8-bit precision. Deployment performance is negatively impacted, as runtime bit-unpacking introduces significant execution slowdowns on CPUs. This highlights a trade-off between model size reduction and actual edge throughput. The study offers a framework for managing the limits of sub-4-bit deployment.

GUARD: Stabilizing Recursive Self-Improvement with Regret Guidance

GUARD is a framework designed to stabilize recursive self-improvement in autonomous AI agents by using regret guidance for harness evolution. It addresses issues where self-editing without external graders causes local regressions and memory bloat. The method decomposes tasks into self-checks, grades trajectories against these checks, and prioritizes newly broken behaviors during updates. A Soft-HURT gate ensures edits are promoted only when gains outweigh regressions, preventing over-triggering. This approach results in performance improvements across various benchmarks without catastrophic forgetting or memory pollution.

Watch Where You Linearize: Predicting and Correcting Model-Merging Interference

Model merging involves combining separately fine-tuned models by adding task vectors to a shared backbone, which introduces interference that is often assessed late. A first-order directional derivative of one task's outputs along another task's vector can predict this interference when evaluated at the correct model. This prediction halves the accuracy error compared to a no-change baseline and identifies which examples experience prediction flips. A label-free correction method called LR-Merge is proposed to optimize merge coefficients using convex teacher matching without backpropagation. LR-Merge improves various base mergers, offering fast and accurate coefficient prediction for new queries.

Density Feedback Control for Transferability of Generative Sparse Adversarial Attacks

Generator-based sparse adversarial attacks require tuning a sparsity coefficient whose mapping to density is unknown, limiting transferability. Density Feedback Control (DFC) is proposed to address this by adjusting the coefficient at the end of each epoch based on the gap to a target density. This method achieves a target density, bringing the mean density of three attacks close to the target in a single run. DFC also improves the attack success rate (ASR) on CNN and Transformer targets, even with per-image $\ell_0$ budgets. Observing the training, relaxing the coefficient allows the transfer ASR to recover and surpass the fixed-coefficient baseline with fewer perturbed pixels.

A Model of Diverse Sampling from Language Models

Language models often produce repeated samples clustered around semantic modes, necessitating a model for diverse generation. The paper formalizes diverse generation as sampling a subset from a determinantal point process (DPP), where likelihood and embedding geometry encode quality and similarity. PoolDPP is proposed to handle this by drawing a finite candidate pool and using an importance-weighting mechanism before applying a $K$-DPP sampler. Empirical results show that PoolDPP extends the quality-diversity and precision-recall frontiers on various tasks, raising diversity without sacrificing performance. This method successfully improves diversity in open-ended generation and specific reasoning benchmarks.

Near-Quadratic Sample Compression for All VC Classes

The paper addresses the sample compression conjecture in learning theory, which relates sample size to generalization. It proves a weaker conjecture by constructing a sample compression scheme of size $O(d^2 \log^2 d)$, where $d$ is the VC dimension. This result establishes near-quadratic sample compression for all classes of VC dimension. The conjecture itself concerns whether a sample size of $O(d)$ suffices for binary concept classes of dimension $d$. The work connects simplification and generalization through sample compression schemes.

The research addresses the multi-armed bandit problem where rewards are survival probabilities observed through right-censored time-to-event data. It proposes KM-UCB, a variance-adaptive policy that combines continuous-time survival analysis with discrete sequential decision-making. HapBO: Harnessed $\alpha$-$\pi$ Bayesian Optimization via Expert Natural Language Priors

HapBO harnesses expert natural language priors to guide $\alpha$-$\pi$ Bayesian Optimization. It derives a structured belief function from human experts or Large Language Models (LLMs), allowing for a rich representation beyond Gaussian distributions. The low-degree sample complexity of clustering Gaussian mixtures with more groups than dimensions

The study investigates the sample complexity for clustering $n$ observations from a Gaussian mixture with $K$ groups in $\mathbb R^d$, focusing on the case where the number of groups $K$ is much larger than the dimension $d$. Distance-based and spectral methods require a separation $\Delta^2$ of order $\sqrt d$, whereas tensor-based procedures cluster at a polylogarithmic separation. The research establishes minimal separation bounds for clustering using low-degree polynomials when $K>d$. Specifically, for $K=d^{p/2}$ with $p>2$, a sample size of order $K^3 d^{(\lceil p\rceil-p)/2}$ is required to achieve clustering with subpolynomial separation.

The paper focuses on variance reduction techniques for matrix computations, particularly through matrix factorization, for applications in Gaussian Processes.
