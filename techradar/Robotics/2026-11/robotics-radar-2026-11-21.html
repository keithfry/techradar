China issues five humanoid robot standards covering testing

China's Ministry of Industry and Information Technology issued five humanoid robot standards on November 16, 2026, to address inconsistencies in capability claims, equipment interfaces, and data management. These standards cover capability assessment, training facilities, training data, integrated electric drive joints, and dexterous hands. A capability classification standard uses six levels (L0 to L5) across four dimensions to provide a detailed profile for development and comparison. Additional standards specify requirements for training sites, hardware, data lifecycle, and safety protocols. The goal is to facilitate the comparison and integration of humanoid robots by standardizing various aspects of their development.

‘The doom of humanity’: will the quest to make robotic hands render us obsolete?

Replicating the dexterity of human hands is a major challenge for robotics, as the ability to feedback and feed forward sensory information is inherently complex. Human hands evolved through a combination of cognitive power and physical dexterity, involving intricate sensory feedback and predictive modeling. While humanoid robots are being developed, achieving the necessary dexterity for real-world tasks remains difficult, leading to a race in developing advanced robotic hands. Companies are focusing on specialized dexterous hands for specific tasks, with significant investment in this area globally. Ultimately, the future impact on jobs depends on how quickly artificial dexterity can be replicated in machines.

How to prototype robot control logic before committing to industrial hardware

Prototype robot control logic on a microcontroller first to sketch sequences, timing, and I/O behavior before committing to expensive industrial hardware. Testing on constrained boards reveals timing assumptions and potential failures that are often missed during hardware commissioning. Prototyping allows teams to deliberately test state transitions and handle edge cases that are impractical on live systems. While a prototype proves sequence logic, it does not account for crucial aspects like safety ratings, EMC performance, or fieldbus determinism. The prototype serves as a specification, guiding the design of the final, safety-rated industrial system.

Siemens and AGIBOT design humanoid grippers from text prompts

Siemens and AGIBOT developed an AI agent workflow to design humanoid robot grippers from text prompts. The agent uses a written task description to select a gripper, modify its geometry in Designcenter, and perform virtual simulations. This process coordinates engineering software functions through MCP integration to adapt the design to the user's handling requirements. This method aims to simplify complex engineering workflows by focusing on the required handling, not individual printing steps. Siemens intends to further this work with an adaptive production cell integrating additive manufacturing and automation.

RoboPapers

Ep#118: Devol Robotics

Devol Robotics develops physics-grounded foundation models to enable precise robotic manipulation by incorporating understanding of force and contact. They use an Interaction World Model (IWM) to learn visual-geometric representations that predict stiffness and damping, based on interaction geometry. The Devol-ONE model unifies vision-language understanding, world prediction, and action generation within a single autoregressive framework. This approach allows robots to reliably complete complex, compliant tasks by predicting physical dynamics during action planning. Experiments showed that incorporating physical priors significantly improved success rates and efficiency in contact-rich assembly tasks.
