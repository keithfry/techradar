FCC should focus on robot security, not country of origin

The FCC should focus robot security on the software controlling the devices rather than their country of origin. The actual security boundary lies in the code, data, and control mechanisms, not the physical chassis. Regulated systems must demonstrate secure code updates, data handling, and access controls. This necessitates standards for provenance of control, vulnerability patching, and independent safety layers for AI. Ultimately, regulation should focus on who controls the code and how remote access is managed.

The next generation of bionic hands is here—and they can feel

An experimental bionic hand, wired to nerves in the forearm, allows a patient to feel pressure for the first time. Avi Davidson, a participant in a University of Utah clinical trial, is the first to use this device independently. The research focuses on connecting the prosthetic to the nervous system to enable both movement control and sensory feedback. Despite functional improvements, Davidson experiences phantom pain and wrist strain, prompting further research into the technology's long-term effects.

When robots lose contact: Research examines the resilience of decentralized robot swarms

Research investigated the resilience of decentralized robot swarms when communication is lost, finding that disconnected robots can still perform useful work based on their local memory. A study simulating pressure vessel inspection compared decentralized methods, like M-PSO, against leader-based approaches in extreme conditions. Decentralized coordination achieved a higher success rate in local location estimation compared to leader-based methods. However, the research also showed that while decentralized systems can store information locally, communication failures prevent easy retrieval of that information. The findings suggest decentralized intelligence is valuable for preserving data, but additional engineering is needed for reliable information transmission.

Designing Robot Gripper Fingers for Repeatable Changeovers

Repeatable changeovers require designing the finger interface to ensure consistent workpiece positioning across different installations. This involves tracing requirements from the receiving operation back through the tooling to define necessary geometric relationships and acceptance criteria. Effect-Driven Skill Abstractions for Offline Reinforcement Learning

The research proposes learning discrete skill abstractions to aid long-horizon, goal-conditioned reinforcement learning. It argues that goal-directed skills should be based on how they affect the agent's state rather than simple action quantization. A novel method optimizes action chunk predictions conditioned on the current state and action to ensure skills are distinguished by the resulting future states. This framework can be integrated with offline RL by replacing continuous action heads with discrete skill selection mechanisms. Experiments on navigation and manipulation tasks demonstrate significant performance improvements over existing baselines.

arXiv - cs.RO

Why Does Sensor Depth Fail Learned Manipulation Policies? Finding the Failure Mode with ACT

Learned manipulation policies primarily rely on RGB images, though depth data can offer performance gains. Depth cameras introduce both measurement noise and missing depth values, the effects of which are not fully understood. Calibration showed that complete depth improves success by about ten percentage points, with gains persisting even with significant noise. However, holes covering one-sixth of the image eliminated the benefit of depth, and reliance on depth masks heavily influences performance. Consequently, depth camera utility for learned policies should be assessed based on depth coverage rather than just measurement precision.

arXiv - cs.RO

FOCI: Trajectory Optimization on Gaussian Splats for Gaussian Robots

FOCI proposes an algorithm to optimize trajectories directly on 3D Gaussian Splats for Gaussian robots. It utilizes a novel collision formulation based on the overlap integral between Gaussians to assess traversability. This method represents both the environment and the robot as Gaussian Splats, allowing for orientation-aware planning through narrow spaces. Testing shows that collision-free trajectories for various robot types can be computed quickly, even with numerous Gaussians. The approach offers an advantage over bounding box methods by providing a more accurate representation of traversability.

arXiv - cs.RO

RoboARC: Physical Puzzles for Robotic Manipulation under Spatial Constraints

RoboARC introduces a benchmark of 90 physical puzzles involving spatial constraints for robotic manipulation with rigid and soft materials. The tasks are defined by frozen scenes with physics-based grading and verified reference solutions. Agents, including coding agents and Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies, are evaluated on solving these tasks under privileged (full state access) and standard (real-sensor input) regimes. GPT-6 Astra proved the strongest, solving 86% of the tasks in the privileged regime, while most other tested agents struggled significantly. The work investigates whether robots can reason out necessary motions when manipulation is governed by precise spatial constraints.

An Adversarial Multi-Agent Pursuit Game for Locomotor Agility Pretraining

Agile humanoid locomotion requires controllers that can adapt to changing motion demands while maintaining stability. The research explores using an adversarial multi-agent pursuit game to pretrain locomotion agility. The method involves training neural networks in a physics simulator to learn new motion generation and control strategies through competitive self-play. A task suite called MJTag is presented for role-conditioned locomotion during competitive pursuit-evasion. This setup allows the locomotion controller to adopt skills like dodging and sprinting via sparse feedback and adversarial training.

OnCue: Generating Safety-Critical Driving Scenarios under Occlusion with Temporal Control

OnCue is a framework designed to generate safety-critical driving scenarios under occlusion by incorporating temporal control. It uses scene geometry to create hidden starts and timed waypoints for pedestrian, cyclist, and vehicle trajectories. The method employs an offline response oracle to label encounters as Reactive or Anticipatory and store safe maneuvers as demonstrations. Results show that temporal control significantly impacts collision risk and planner performance under occlusion. This research emphasizes the value of temporal control for evaluating and improving autonomous driving planners.

Model Predictive Control of a Multirotor UAV with a Cable-Suspended Payload: A Comparison of Shifted and Transformer-Based Warm Starts

The study compares model predictive control (MPC) for a multirotor UAV with a cable-suspended payload using different warm-starting techniques. It investigates the use of conventional shifted initialization versus Transformer-based warm starting for a sequential convex programming optimizer. Numerical experiments show that Transformer initialization reduces computation time for independently solved MPC problems. However, shifting the previous control sequence yields the fastest time in sequential closed-loop simulations. All methods resulted in similar tracking behavior during simulation.

arXiv - cs.RO

Skeptic: Grounding Failure Diagnosis for Robot Skill Discovery

Robot agents can fail in skill discovery when they incorrectly judge a program's failure as physical impossibility. Skeptic is introduced as a training-free protocol to diagnose this grounding failure using measured action outcomes and task success. The method employs a grounded event log to separate sensor observations from inferred diagnoses of action effects. Verified promotion incorporates a development-success requirement to check for independent reproduction of the source program. This approach reduced false impossibility judgments across audited tasks and improved held-out success rates.

arXiv - cs.RO

DexEngine: Scaling Human Demonstrations into Physically Executable Whole-Robot Data

DexEngine is a simulation pipeline designed to transform human hand-object demonstrations into physically executable whole-robot data. It achieves this by positioning demonstrations, finding arm motions that follow the human actions, and correcting these motions using physics simulation and residual reinforcement learning. The method generates a large dataset from human data to train whole-robot policies. Policies trained with this data significantly outperform those without physics correction, demonstrating the necessity of incorporating physical constraints. Mid-training on the generated data further enhances performance in both simulation and real-world robot settings.
