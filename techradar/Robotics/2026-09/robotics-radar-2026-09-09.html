Nucleus Pivots to Wheeled Base for "Nucleus II" Following Factory Floor Pushback

Nucleus has pivoted its platform to "Nucleus II," replacing bipedal locomotion with a stable wheeled mobile base based on customer demands for higher payload and uptime. This new configuration offers a 10 kg per-arm payload, sixfold battery capacity, and five to nine hours of continuous operational time. The wheeled system enhances operational safety by eliminating the risk of falls, satisfying factory floor requirements. The company continues to use a hardware-agnostic software stack, emphasizing embodied AI and teleoperation capabilities. This shift reflects a broader industrial trend favoring wheeled dual-arm manipulators in logistics settings.

Inside XPENG’s Bet on IRON: He Xiaopeng on Why the Hardest Path in Humanoid Robotics Is Never Crowded

CEO He Xiaopeng shared a reflection on the six-year journey of the XPENG IRON humanoid robot platform, addressing early public skepticism and setbacks. He emphasized prioritizing end-to-end Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models and automotive-grade mass production over short-term marketing hype. XPENG's approach focuses on autonomous learning and persistent memory, moving beyond teleoperation to achieve real-world functionality. The company acknowledges that early commercial units are works in progress, maintaining a focus on engineering refinement over rushed demonstrations. Ultimately, XPENG views automated manufacturing as the necessary mechanism to make embodied AI practical.

Dynamic Creatures Emerges From Stealth With Boston Dynamics Backing to Build Expressive Guest-Facing Robots

Dynamic Creatures is a startup that has emerged from stealth, partnering with Boston Dynamics to build expressive, mobile guest-facing robots. The company deploys these interactive characters using an AI operating system called SnowJay to react dynamically to environments and guests. Instead of focusing on domestic deployment, the venture targets commercial hospitality and entertainment venues like theme parks and resorts. Dynamic Creatures aims to fill the gap between static animatronics and utility robots by creating characters capable of spontaneous, face-to-face social interaction. The company will use a hybrid hardware model, combining bespoke designs with existing platforms to offer leasing options.

From Stadiums to the Front Line: How China and the US Are Preparing Humanoids for Combat

China is actively developing humanoid robots for military applications, with state-owned entities like Norinco creating teleoperated combat robots and research modeling urban assault scenarios. Chinese manufacturing dominates the global humanoid shipment market, providing a strong domestic industrial base. In contrast, the US is led by startups like Foundation Robotics, which focuses on militarized applications and developing ruggedized systems. Despite advancements, real-world deployment faces challenges related to energy density, physical fragility, and the complexity of battlefield conditions. Ultimately, current deployment relies heavily on human-in-the-loop control due to current technical and ethical hurdles.
