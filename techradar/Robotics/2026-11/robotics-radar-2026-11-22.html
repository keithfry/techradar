The manufacturing data gap holding back physical AI

Physical AI deployment in manufacturing is hindered by a significant data gap, as experienced technician knowledge is often not captured in a format suitable for autonomous learning. Manufacturers must bridge this gap by ensuring data connects process decisions to production outcomes, addressing data silos between operational and quality systems. To prepare for physical AI, companies should focus on capturing data reflecting real production conditions, creating a digital thread linking process records to quality results, and building feedback loops for quality evaluation. Ultimately, industrial autonomy depends less on data volume and more on the ability of robotic systems to reliably leverage connected data for decision-making.

Bot Auto launches ‘Capacity Grid’ with agreements for 200 million miles of driverless trucking

Bot Auto launched the Capacity Grid, a freight transportation network to match customer demand with autonomous trucking capacity. This platform allows shippers and brokers to reserve driverless trucking capacity in advance with agreed prices and schedules. The network includes agreements covering 200 million miles of driverless freight operations, including a 50 million mile deal with FreightVana. The Capacity Grid coordinates available trucks, routes, and schedules to improve vehicle utilization and reduce operating costs for customers. The company aims to establish a repeatable business model for large-scale commercial autonomous trucking operations.

Building a Robotic Hand on a Budget

A project focused on building a robotic hand with an eighteen-degree-of-freedom design costs less than $300 in parts. The hand's skeleton is 3D printed and driven by servo motors located in the forearm, utilizing tendons instead of traditional cables. To minimize friction and maintain joint movement, tendons run through PTFE tubes, and a ribbon cable system was used for the arm's rotation instead of expensive slip-rings. This design allows each finger to move side-to-side and flex two joints independently. The work explores tendon-based approaches for achieving dexterity in robotic hands.

Tesla clears Fremont assembly line for Optimus robot factory

Tesla has cleared the former Model S and Model X assembly line at its Fremont factory to establish a facility for manufacturing Optimus humanoid robots. This decommissioning process took 46 days, and the cleared space is designated for housing the necessary equipment. Elon Musk stated the goal is to eventually produce one million robots annually, though no firm timeline exists for this target. The immediate step involves allocating existing manufacturing space for robot assembly, alongside an ongoing pilot production line. Tesla has also increased hiring for the Optimus program.
