The 0.95 Expectile Is Not the 95th Percentile

Expectiles and quantiles both rely on asymmetric weighting but differ in the loss function they minimize: quantiles minimize expected absolute errors, while expectiles minimize expected squared errors. For a uniform distribution, the 95th percentile is 0.95, but the 0.95 expectile is approximately 0.8134, meaning only about 81.34% of the distribution lies below it. This difference arises because expectiles incorporate the magnitude of the errors, leading to different optimal decisions based on the asymmetry of the loss function. In practical applications, one must choose the statistic based on the specific cost of being wrong, as the expected coverage for the quantile (95%) is not directly reflected by the expectile.
