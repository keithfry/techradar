ROS Discourse General: Ramp Autonomy: a ROS 2 / Gazebo stack for ramp ascent and descent on a loaded AMR

A ROS 2/Gazebo stack is presented for ramp ascent and descent on a loaded AMR using Nav2 MPPI control. The system addresses the issue where standard navigation assumes flat ground, which fails on ramps due to IMU bias, shifting center of gravity, and wheel slip. The framework employs a five-state Finite State Machine, a LiDAR slope classifier, and safety layers to manage these dynamics. The author shares specific findings regarding MPPI profile asymmetry and the impact of speed capping on wheel slip during descent. The project is currently simulated, and the author seeks feedback on parameter tuning and real-world validation.

EvoMisty: Building, Optimizing, and Evolving Capabilities on a Social Robot

EvoMisty is a framework designed to build, optimize, and evolve the capabilities of social robots through a plugin-based approach. It enables coding agents to construct control programs from documentation and verify capabilities on physical robots, storing verified skills in an atomic library. The system collects execution data, measures user-specified metrics, and iteratively improves task programs. EvoMisty distills reusable skills from successful solutions into a skill library for future use. Experiments showed that optimization significantly improved performance metrics, such as reducing dialogue response latency by up to 71%.

Learning from Human Corrections through Temporal Preferences

Human corrections provide information through both the nature of the correction and the timing of the intervention. The work proposes using both pre- and post-intervention behaviors to form preference pairs for learning value functions that approximate a human's latent reward. Post-intervention preferences derived from comparing corrected versus replaced behavior are effective for goal inference. Pre-intervention preferences, based on temporal state relationships, show strong performance for correction forecasting. Combining both temporal and comparative preferences results in strong performance across both downstream tasks.

arXiv - cs.RO

Geometric Control Analysis of an Internally Actuated Flying Wing

A geometric control analysis was performed for a fixed-wing Uncrewed Aircraft System actuated by an internal moving mass, a momentum wheel, and external thrust. This method eliminates traditional control surfaces, simplifying high-fidelity modeling of unsteady flow fields. Lie bracket computations demonstrate that the full nonlinear dynamics are generically small-time locally accessible. The momentum wheel and mass inertia variations allow for full-rank accessibility in the Lie algebra. A nonlinear Model Predictive Controller is subsequently derived for trajectory tracking under various flow conditions.

CRAB-H: Task-Centric Certification for Healthcare Autonomous Systems

CRAB-H introduces a task-centric certification methodology for healthcare autonomous systems by making the task a first-class governance object via Task Requirement Profiles (TRP H). This method assesses certification by comparing demonstrated capabilities against required task capabilities, rather than just benchmark scores. A reliability-gated assurance allocation mechanism handles unevaluable cognitive dimensions by routing them to operational requirements. Governance integrity is maintained by governing unreliable dimensions rather than ignoring them. The Capability Margin is determined by threshold-based compliance, preserving necessary non-compensatory safety logic.

Embodied Neuro-symbolic AI: Making Robot Behavior Auditable, Safe, and Scalable

Vision-Language-Action systems struggle with generalization, physical reasoning, and fine-grained control in real-world robot deployment. Embodied Neuro-symbolic (NeSy) AI is proposed as a paradigm to address these issues by integrating learned perception with explicit, machine-readable background knowledge and formal constraints. This approach uses knowledge-guided perception and constraint-aware planning to allow robots to reason about their environment. Consequently, Embodied NeSy AI enables robots to exhibit behavior that is auditable and verifiable regarding safety and operational requirements. This methodology supports the development of scalable and trustworthy robot systems.

arXiv - cs.RO

Hi3R-VLA: Grounding Semantics and Actions in 3D

Hi3R-VLA is a 3D policy designed to ground vision, language, and action modalities in explicit 3D geometry for robotic manipulation. It integrates spatially-aligned visual features and language conditioning into point cloud representations to enrich scene geometry with task-relevant semantics. The model anchors control commands in the same 3D frame and uses 3D-RoPE enhanced attention to model geometric relationships between action targets and observed points. Analysis shows that semantic fusion within 3D and spatial alignment between observations and actions are crucial. Hi3R-VLA achieves state-of-the-art performance across various manipulation tasks with under 250M trainable parameters.

arXiv - cs.RO

Sampling at Scale: Distributed Multi-Agent Trajectory Optimization

Multi-agent trajectory optimization faces challenges in exploring non-convex landscapes and scaling to large teams. The proposed method introduces a Distributed Sampling-based Trajectory Optimization (DS-TO) architecture using a splitting mechanism. This allows each agent to sample only in its control space, independently of neighborhood size, while still considering other agents' decisions. The algorithm is fully decentralized, relying only on neighborhood communication for parallel local updates. Experiments show improved exploration and constraint satisfaction over other methods for large agent teams.

FabricBench: A Standardized Policy Benchmark for Bimanual Fabric Manipulation with a Contact-Driven Deformable Simulator

FabricBench introduces a benchmark for bimanual fabric manipulation using a contact-driven deformable simulator. It features six tasks involving garments, silk, and bags, with 10,000 simulation-ready assets. The simulator allows stable grasping and release through contact forces, enabling interactions between robot arms and fabric. The benchmark serves as a reproducible testbed for measuring fabric manipulation capabilities and generalization limits.

A Self-Organizing Swarm Control Model for Underwater Robots Based on Turing-Type Reaction-Diffusion Principles

The model proposes a decentralized swarm control for underwater robots operating without GPS or reliable communication. It utilizes Turing-type reaction-diffusion principles, extending a cellular automaton to continuous space, to govern swarm behavior. Swarm formation is driven by robots moving towards regions of higher potential calculated from signal differences between inner and outer neighborhoods. Stable behavior is achieved through temporal averaging, hysteresis-based evaluation, and stepwise turning. Simulations confirm the model's ability to autonomously form and control the spatial dispersion of the swarm, even with reduced communication success rates.

Collision Rendering: GPU-Accelerated Trajectory Planning on Sparse Voxel Radiance Fields with Risk-Bounded Collision Checking

Collision Rendering introduces a framework for trajectory planning on sparse voxel radiance fields by leveraging GPU acceleration. It achieves this through risk-bounded collision checking and a differentiable collision cost derived from rendering pipelines. The method approximates the robot as collision spheres, using rendering to estimate collision probability bounds. This results in a modular collision checker that integrates with sampling-based planners without needing extra geometric maps. Experiments show millisecond collision checking and successful trajectory planning on consumer GPUs.
