Auditable Human-Agent Teams for Autonomous X-ray Experimentation

Closed-loop autonomous experimentation is explored using coding-agent infrastructure for synchrotron X-ray experiments on nanoparticle superlattices. The research demonstrates the use of human-agent and agent-agent transcripts to audit decision provenance and cognitive labor division. Analysis of three experiments revealed that 61-67% of messages involved interpretation or judgment, and methodological contributions increased over time. Error corrections frequently involved multiple participants, and decisions often incorporated input from other agents. These findings suggest design principles for multi-agent scientific systems and autonomous experimentation.

EarthRecord: From Pixels to Land Change with Multimodal Agents over Four Decades of Satellite Imagery

EarthRecord establishes a benchmark for evaluating multimodal agents on land-change analysis using four decades of raw satellite imagery. It incorporates data from Landsat, MODIS, VIIRS, and Sentinel-2, requiring agents to handle cloud masking and cross-sensor harmonization. The benchmark poses 238 land-change questions across ten tasks, assessed against global references and method agreement. Evaluations indicate that while agents complete most analyses, they struggle with mapping from imagery to real-world change. Ultimately, EarthRecord offers a framework to measure and advance agents from pixel-level data to actual land change.

Train on prefill, monitor at decode: Using sparse autoencoders for topic monitoring

A framework called TopicScope uses sparse autoencoders to train causal topic classifiers on Sparse Autoencoder (SAE) features assigned to every token. This allows for topic labeling to change throughout a model's generation process. Monitors trained on features from prefill hidden states were shown to generalize to decoding, even when detecting topics across different multi-topic conversations. These classifiers proved robust to perturbations like inserting off-topic asides or reordering sections. The research demonstrates that compact topic monitors can be trained efficiently in prefill and deployed during generation.

LandslideFM: A Foundation Model Towards Unified Landslide Perception and Understanding

LandslideFM is a unified foundation model designed to improve landslide perception and understanding by connecting visual perception tasks with contextual interpretation. It utilizes a Source-Agreement Graduated Trust semi-supervised learning framework to adapt to multiple visual tasks using limited labeled and abundant unlabeled data. LandslideVL is presented as a new benchmark combining visual imagery with textual prompts for landslide analysis. Experiments show LandslideFM achieves superior performance in specialized landslide reasoning compared to general-purpose Vision-Language Models.

Tracing Psychometric Inference in Large Language Models

The study investigates whether large language models internally represent psychological constructs or merely generate outputs resembling psychometric structures. Using responses from 272 university students, the models' predictions across six psychological scales were analyzed. Larger model variants demonstrated an amplification of human cross-scale correlations at the behavioral level. Contrastive direction analysis showed geometric alignment with the human matrix in larger instruct variants, but less alignment in base variants. The findings suggest a measurable gap between internal structure and output behavior, termed the probe-behavior gap readout attenuation.

Graph Visualizations in Prompts for Knowledge Graph Question Answering

Multimodal input, specifically text-plus-graph visualization, improves knowledge graph question answering performance when using large vision-language models. Adding a graph image resulted in an average F1 gain of 1.85 points, with gains being greatest for low-complexity questions. These multimodal benefits depend heavily on graph layout, as hierarchical Graphviz layouts perform better than less organized renderings. Optimally, visual prompting should be adaptive, selectively applying diagrams to small, well-structured subgraphs to maintain accuracy and efficiency.

AI Governance Should Prioritize Control and Knowledge Boundaries Over Limiting Intelligence

The paper proposes a competence circuit (IKC + KC + C) to decompose an agent's impact into intelligence-mediated discovery, procedural know-how, and direct control. It formalizes conditions showing that fast catastrophic supremacy relies more on rapid knowledge acquisition or control attainment than solely on intelligence. Simulations indicate that intelligence-driven speedups are limited by the ratio of competing intelligence levels. The research argues that controlling and setting knowledge boundaries are more critical safety levers than merely limiting intelligence. Ultimately, safety in agentic systems depends on restricting control and knowledge, especially when institutional delays exist.

Tabular foundation models (TFMs) are evaluated against tuned gradient boosting trees for predicting telecom customer churn, considering profit and calibration. SetPrint: Black-Box LLM Fingerprinting via Setwise Correspondence of Response Changes

SetPrint is a training-free method for black-box LLM fingerprinting by analyzing response changes across perturbed queries for two models. It captures both the relations among response variations within each set and the correspondence between response changes between the two models. The method achieves this by expressing response changes relative to the mean change of their respective sets. SetPrint consistently outperforms existing training-free fingerprinting methods on the LeaFBench dataset. The performance gains are attributed to the captured within-set relations and between-set correspondence.

An ensemble of graph generative models for assisted history matching of the Brugge field case

Assisted history matching for the Brugge field is addressed using an ensemble of graph generative models. The approach models each stratigraphic zone with a dedicated generative model possessing a joint latent space for optimization. Graph-based generators handle the irregular geometry of the grid directly, enabling optimization in the 160-dimensional latent space via Covariance Matrix Adaptation Evolution Strategy. The objective function incorporates well data mismatch, production mismatch, and a geological realism penalty based on the latent metric. The resulting matched models preserve geological relations and reproduce production results comparable to prior studies.

Predictive Surprise as Self-Grounding Concept Bottleneck for Interpretable Time Series

The research introduces ConceptTime, a method using predictive surprise as a self-grounding signal for interpretable time series analysis. A frozen probabilistic forecaster predicts future observations, and the mismatch between its prediction and the actual signal is termed predictive surprise. This surprise locates segment boundaries and characterizes segments into "concepts," which are dynamical regimes. A lightweight head classifies based on these concepts, and the distance to the nearest concept provides a measure of reliability. ConceptTime demonstrates competitive accuracy and faithfulness across various benchmarks without requiring external annotations.

The research investigates whether the utility of a self-update persists after subsequent native updates in recursive self-improvement. The study evaluates five 3-8B backbones on math and code tasks using 64 fresh runs and candidate/continuation evaluations. Results show that immediate utility is often near zero or negative, with continuation slightly reducing utility for some models. Linear Probes to Bayesian Manifolds: Geometry of Arithmetic in Large Language Models

Linear probes are used to study geometric structures in Large Language Models (LLMs) by analyzing their activations. The study involves a four-stage Bayesian pipeline to test claims of decodability, shape, ownership, and causal use of these shapes. Applying this pipeline to two-digit arithmetic in models like GPT-J and Llama 3.1 revealed that probe results alone overstate what a model computes. Results indicate that 19% of shapes are owned and 81% are inherited. The authors propose reporting probe results based on all four claims instead of a single accuracy measure.

Near-Optimal Sample Complexity of Deterministic Agnostic PAC Learning

The study investigates the sample complexity for agnostic PAC learning under deterministic labeling, where labels are provided by an arbitrary deterministic function. The research shows that deterministic labels can improve the sample complexity bound from the classical agnostic guarantee. The main result establishes a sample complexity of $\widetilde{O}\left( \frac{K^{1/3} \, d^{2/3}}{\varepsilon} \right)$ for any class with VC dimension $d$ and finite diameter $K$. The authors construct classes where this bound is tight and also analyze the online learning analogue. This performance is achieved using memorization-based learning algorithms that leverage the deterministic label structure.

Causal mediation analysis decomposes treatment effects into indirect and direct pathways through mediators. Experiments show MediPFN achieves coverage closer to the nominal level and is significantly faster at uncertainty quantification than bootstrap methods.

Outliers and the Hard-Margin Linear SVM in High-Dimension, Low-Sample-Size Settings

The study investigates the effect of outliers on the hard-margin linear Support Vector Machine when the feature dimension increases while sample sizes are held constant. For one-sided contamination, the centered Gram matrix shows a structure of inlier blocks and an outlier-diagonal component, leading to a closed-form dual problem. These effects allow for the explicit determination of thresholds related to loss of support, margin shrinkage, and misclassification. Contamination in both classes results in at most one class collapsing or being misclassified, while symmetric contamination preserves the limiting discriminant function despite margin reduction. Numerical experiments confirm these findings regarding dual allocation and phase transitions.

On the Spectral Estimation of VARMA Processes

The paper addresses the problem of estimating the power spectral density of a Vector-valued Autoregressive Moving-Average (VARMA) process. It proposes a new moment problem based on transportation entropy for learning the spectral density. This method allows for estimating the spectral density without restricting the moving-average (MA) component to be fixed or scalar. Existing methods are limited because they assume a fixed or scalar MA component. The proposed approach extends the class of ARMA models for spectral estimation.

Compositional random forest

The paper constructs a random forest framework designed for compositional responses, accommodating representations on the simplex, log-ratio coordinates, and the unit sphere. It utilizes log-likelihood gain as the split criterion, employing transformations like the square-root and log-ratio transformations to facilitate flexible criteria. The work introduces a tangent-normal (TN) distribution for the sphere and a multivariate logistic-normal distribution for Euclidean space. Simulations indicated that the TN forest was the most accurate across the different compositional designs.
