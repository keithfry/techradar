AI routing is the process of selecting the appropriate model, tool, or policy to handle a specific input. Mixture of Experts (MoE): History, Architecture & Modern LLMs

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) originated from the 1991 Jacobs & Hinton paper, which proposed training multiple specialized models guided by a gating network. The key innovation lies in the "stochastic one-out-of-n selector" to promote true specialization among experts rather than a simple weighted sum. The sparse-gated MoE layer, introduced in 2017, enabled greater than 1000x increases in model capacity with manageable computational costs. This concept was integrated into the Transformer architecture, leading to modern LLMs like Mistral and GPT-4, and models such as Mixtral 8x7B. These models demonstrate efficiency by only activating a subset of experts for specific computations.

Test-Time Training – A Model That Changes While You Use It

Test-time training (TTT) is an ML technique that allows a model to continue learning and adapting while it is being used for inference. It achieves this by creating a self-supervised auxiliary task from new test data, using the model's errors on this task to update some of its parameters. This process blurs the line between training and inference, enabling models to adjust to new conditions during real-world use. TTT is particularly beneficial for world models that need to rapidly adapt to changing states and environments. However, limitations exist, as poor learning signals can cause overfitting or performance degradation.
