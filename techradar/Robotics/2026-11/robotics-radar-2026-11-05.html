Are Drone Ports the Next Annoying Tech Neighbor?

Residential concerns arose when Amazon's delivery drones, specifically the large MK30 models, generated bothersome noise and flew lower than legally permitted. Residents experienced significant annoyance from the high-pitched whine of the rotors, which acoustic research indicates is more irritating than car or airplane noise. Delivery drone companies are expanding rapidly, and while they promote positive uses, communities fear corporate overreach and safety issues. Activists and local governments have responded by challenging regulations and advocating for community engagement regarding drone operations. Ongoing disputes remain concerning noise, safety, and the legal regulation of airspace above private property.

LiberAI Unveils Liber-0, a World Action Model That Grounds Human Experience in Robot Action

LiberAI introduced Liber-0, a world action model pretrained on over 2.2 million hours of human experience to enable zero-shot robot manipulation. This model combines human video, glove-recorded motion, and embodiment alignment to transfer human manipulation skills to robots. In a zero-shot evaluation of 60 GPT-6 tasks, Liber-0 achieved 36.0% overall success without task-specific robot demonstrations. The system achieves this by grounding human experience in measured motion and aligning it with the robot's body representation. Furthermore, incorporating glove recordings can reduce the need for specific robot demonstrations, potentially saving significant data collection efforts.

Clearing up a robot’s fuzzy memory

A new system, UQ-DAAAM, corrects unreliable object descriptions stored in a robot's memory by incorporating uncertainty quantification. It achieves this by comparing multiple viewpoints and captions of the same object to assess the robot's confidence in its descriptions. The system refines captions by selecting additional views that reduce uncertainty, leading to more accurate memory. This process boosts confidence and improves the accuracy of object descriptions by up to five percent. Ultimately, this method enhances a robot's ability to perform complex tasks by ensuring it remembers what it has observed with greater reliability.

Jev with Foresight: Safe Language-Guided Autonomous Driving via a Language World Model

The research introduces Jev-AD, which integrates Jev, a typed decision model, with a language world model for safe autonomous driving. The language world model predicts the movement of nearby vehicles over the next two seconds to generate foreseen consequences for candidate actions. This foresight allows Jev to compare potential outcomes directly rather than implicitly inferring consequences from coordinates. In simulations, this foresight significantly reduced collision rates and improved the model's ability to achieve passenger intent. Real-world testing further demonstrated that this approach lowered collision rates in actual driving scenarios.

Ctrl-F: Force as an Interface for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

Force is proposed as a natural command channel between motion imitation and object interaction, specifying both contact location and force magnitude. Ctrl-F is a hierarchical framework that augments motion tracking with an explicit 3D force command at contact points like hands or feet. This framework includes a unified controller and a high-level force planner that issues online force commands based on object state. In experiments, Ctrl-F successfully enabled humanoid robots to manipulate objects up to 20kg through dragging, pushing, and kicking. This system demonstrated the ability to adapt force commands online as external loads changed during manipulation tasks.

MESA: An Evaluation Framework for Compositional, Semantic, and Spatial Generalization in Robotics

MESA is an evaluation framework designed to precisely measure language-conditioned policy generalization in robotics. It introduces five evaluation suites and four generalization axes: spatial configuration, object instance, object category, and subtask composition. The framework uses MESA-Gen to generate tasks and demonstrations for large-scale evaluation. Testing seven baseline methods, the strongest model achieved 52% on MESA and 49% on the BiMESA benchmark. MESA rankings align with RoboArena scores, suggesting relevance beyond simulation.

ELA: End-to-End Latent Action Policy Learning from Video Demonstrations

ELA is an end-to-end framework designed to learn latent actions from abundant video demonstrations for robot imitation learning. It addresses limitations in prior latent-action methods by jointly optimizing a latent action model, policy, and action decoder. This approach allows policies to effectively utilize video data with minimal action-labeled demonstrations. Experiments on manipulation tasks demonstrate that ELA consistently surpasses previous latent action baselines. The framework integrates easily with existing imitation learning methods through lightweight decoders.

Contact-Implicit Whole-Body Trajectory Optimization for Dynamic Humanoid Locomotion on Parameterizable Uneven Terrain

A trajectory planner is presented for humanoid robots to achieve dynamic motion on complex, parameterizable terrains using a contact-implicit whole-body optimization framework. This planner generates feasible movements by considering the robot's full dynamics and kinematics without requiring predefined contact sequences. A mathematical model approximates ground geometry as a smooth surface, incorporating novel contact constraints for arbitrary smooth interactions with the ground. Nonlinear programming is employed to plan motions across various terrain topologies, and high-level requirements are integrated into the cost function for dynamic trajectory generation. Feasibility tests were conducted on non-coplanar terrains in both simulation and on the ErgoCub robot.

arXiv - cs.RO

$\mathcal{C}^\infty$ Smooth Trajectory Planning with Application to Soft Robots

Smooth trajectory planning is crucial for high motion performance and vibration avoidance in robotics. The required smoothness level depends on the robot's degrees of freedom and system order, and this requirement may be higher for soft continuum robots than previously assumed. The paper introduces a $\mathcal{C}^\infty$-smooth trajectory generator to produce infinitely differentiable paths while respecting kinematic limits. This method generates trajectories with continuous velocity, acceleration, and higher-order derivatives. The approach is verified through simulations and hardware testing, including the case of null boundary conditions.

Correct Robots Before They Make Mistakes: Proactive Human-in-the-Loop Intervention via Preference Learning

A framework called Proactive Human-in-the-Loop (PHIL) allows operators to intervene by previewing policy predictions and displaying trajectories in an XR interface. During intervention, rejected predictions are paired with corrective actions, and preference-based post-training steers the policy to favor these corrections. This method was evaluated on four real-world manipulation tasks using diffusion and vision-language-action policies. PHIL significantly increased success rates and demonstration budgets compared to existing methods like HG-DAgger. Policies trained with proactive corrections also exhibited fewer unnecessary recovery behaviors.

OdorSpaces: Visual-Olfactory Embodied Navigation in 3D Environments

OdorSpaces is a physics-based simulation platform and benchmark for visual-olfactory embodied navigation in 3D environments. It integrates 3D odor transport and dispersion, simulated by the Fire Dynamics Simulator, into Habitat-Sim for navigation. The platform uses both visual and olfactory observations to guide agents through photorealistic indoor settings. It is benchmarked on tasks like locating invisible gas leaks and early-stage fires. A multimodal reinforcement learning policy developed for this system significantly outperforms existing baselines.

OUCH: Objective, Uniform, Controlled Haptic Stimulation for Automated Neurological Assessment

OUCH presents a wrist-worn device using pneumatic actuation to deliver controlled noxious mechanical stimuli for automated neurological assessment. It achieves objective, uniform, and controlled stimulation by combining pneumatic pouches, an indenter, and a compliant spacer. Experimental results showed the indenter concentrated load significantly more effectively than a commercial algometer tip. Preliminary testing indicated that the device elicited discomfort with approximately 89% less applied force than the algometer. This work establishes a foundation for objective neurological assessment through wearable haptic stimulation.

arXiv - cs.RO

GRASP: Gradient-Guided Sampling for Contact-Rich Physics-Based Motion Retargeting

GRASP proposes a method for physics-based motion retargeting of human demonstrations under contact dynamics. It combines sampling-based exploration with gradient-guided refinement, avoiding the need for a differentiable simulator. GRASP-TO extends tempered sequential Monte Carlo by using gradients from local quadratic cost models to guide MCMC rejuvenation. This approach scales to long demonstrations via warm-started receding-horizon planning. Experiments show accurate retargeting for dexterous hands and humanoids, achieving 92.8% success on bimanual demonstrations.
