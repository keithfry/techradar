The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI is betting that robotic intelligence should be transferable across different hardware instead of being rebuilt for each machine. The company is testing whether these capabilities can survive the real-world demands of speed, reliability, and safety in deployment. This approach aims for the model to learn how to understand a body rather than requiring engineers to perfectly specify every physical detail beforehand.

Tesla patent reveals touch-sensitive skin for robot hands

Tesla filed a US patent for a touch-sensitive "skin" to mold around robot fingers and palms for detecting contact, force, and pressure. The technology involves printing tactile sensors onto flexible materials and then heat-forming them into three-dimensional shapes. These sensors function by overlapping conductive regions on flexible substrates, detecting pressure changes via capacitive and piezoresistive methods. The patent emphasizes manufacturability using techniques like screen printing and thermoforming for scalable production. This innovation aims to give humanoid robots more dexterous manipulation capabilities by allowing them to sense how firmly they are holding objects.

Skild AI trains robots to learn physical skills through self-play

Skild AI developed a self-play training method for robots to learn physical skills without human demonstrations, using its S1 robotics foundation model. Robots learned soccer skills through self-play against increasingly capable versions of themselves in a simulated environment. This process allowed the robots to autonomously develop complex physical behaviors like dribbling and tackling based on the objective of scoring. The learned policy was successfully transferred to a physical humanoid robot, demonstrating the potential for physical AGI. This approach aims to reduce the reliance on human demonstration data for teaching robots complex physical tasks.

ETH Zurich scientists turn WUJI robotic hand into self-propelled mobile manipulator

ETH Zurich scientists transformed a WUJI robotic hand into a self-propelled mobile manipulator. Multi-Dimensional Featurizers Yield Fine-Grained Steering and Data Shaping in Robot Foundation Models

Multi-dimensional featurizers are investigated to improve control over robot behavior in foundation models. Block-sparse featurizers (BSFs) are used to recover multi-dimensional features relevant to perception, language, and control. Fine-grained steering experiments show that manipulating these features predictably alters downstream action predictions. Feature-based data attribution allows for identifying minimal training data tokens necessary to remove specific behaviors. Ultimately, aligning featurizers with representation geometry offers more effective means to understand and modify robot behavior.

arXiv - cs.RO

Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle

The method estimates vehicle heading by optimizing a Rayleigh sky model using measurements from a division-of-focal-plane polarimetric camera. This framework incorporates a pinhole camera model, a single-scattering Rayleigh polarization model, and a solar position algorithm to synthesize polarization angle information. A polarization-weighted root mean square error function is used to compare measured and simulated polarization patterns, effectively handling occlusions. Experimental results demonstrated sub-degree RMS yaw errors of $0.718^{\circ}$ to $0.889^{\circ}$ across road vehicle trajectories. The approach significantly reduces RMS heading error by up to 87% compared to other navigation methods.

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

The research establishes feasibility ceilings for drone-mesh interception tasks before any learning policy is applied. It provides closed-form bounds for performance based on the spread and timing of agents covering a predicted arrival point. The analysis reveals that naive area ratios underestimate coverage, and optimal packing arrangements are hexagonal. Analytic placement achieves 85% of configuration performance, while learning methods achieve significantly less. These ceilings should be determined and cited before any learning experiment.

arXiv - cs.RO

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery (PGRR) is a method designed to improve gait selection after irreversible leg damage in robotics. This approach predicts multiple outcomes over repeated gait executions and uses uncertainty-aware estimates to guide short online probes without resetting the robot. Across 108 matched tasks involving leg amputation, fracture, and joint lock, PGRR yielded a 0.106 improvement in final quality and a 0.044 improvement in slot-averaged recovery quality compared to a baseline. Furthermore, PGRR reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points across all damage classes. The study indicates that conservative uncertainty handling is the key factor driving these observed performance gains.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

The methodology employs a clarity-driven ergodic search to plan missions for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels. This approach maximizes information gathering by linking clarity to differential entropy, accounting for limitations imposed by renewable power. The system adapts an online target clarity map based on in-situ measurements to prioritize areas of interest. An ergodic path planner continuously re-plans routes considering this clarity map and the ASV's speed constraints. Simulations at Jordan Lake demonstrated the effectiveness of this method for scientific missions.

arXiv - cs.RO

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing

Existing safety systems fail to recover autonomous vehicles from loss-of-control situations like icing or hydroplaning because they focus on prevention rather than recovery. The research trains a racing policy using reinforcement learning to learn drifting as a time-optimal maneuver. This policy is then adapted through continued training to focus on recovery with a minimum-time reward. The transferred policy significantly improves recovery performance over existing methods, outperforming model predictive controllers. When deployed on a physical vehicle, the policy successfully recovers from states where other baselines fail.

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Sustainably powered mobile robots need to optimize energy allocation to maximize information quality during long-duration missions. The study formulates this as a persistent optimal control problem involving a robot traversing a fixed closed path. The optimization uses the information-theoretic quantity of clarity as a metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control. The research characterizes the nature of optimal control and establishes a high-speed information limit when energy is abundant. Simulation results, based on salinity characterization in the Chesapeake Bay, demonstrate the practical value of these speed trajectory optimizations.

arXiv - cs.RO

BubbleCBF: Constructive Safety for Second-Order Systems and Application to Autonomous Robot Navigation in Unknown Environments

The research develops a constructive method called BubbleCBF for designing feasible safety constraints for systems with second-order dynamics. This involves constructing collision-free spheres, or "bubbles," from a signed distance function to define velocity-dependent safety constraints. A quadratic-program safety filter is used to enforce the safe set, proving the resulting controller is well-defined and forward invariant under mild assumptions. The method is demonstrated by applying BubbleCBF to a differential-drive robot for safe autonomous navigation in unknown environments. This work successfully applies the framework to achieve safe navigation for mobile robots.
