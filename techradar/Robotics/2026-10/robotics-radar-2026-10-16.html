The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI is betting that robotic intelligence should transfer across different hardware instead of requiring a complete rebuild for each machine. Their research focuses on motor adaptation, video-prompted task learning, and self-play to achieve this. The core idea is to separate intelligence from the physical body so that the model learns how to inhabit different bodies. The challenge lies in testing whether these capabilities can survive the real-world requirements of speed, reliability, and safety in deployment. This approach seeks for the model to learn what a body is, rather than relying on perfect prior engineering specifications.

Multi-Dimensional Featurizers Yield Fine-Grained Steering and Data Shaping in Robot Foundation Models

Block-sparse featurizers (BSFs) are investigated to recover the multi-dimensional feature structure underlying vision-language-action models. These featurizers capture task-relevant features such as perception, language, proprioception, episode phase, and control variables. Fine-grained steering experiments causally demonstrate that manipulating these features predictably alters downstream action predictions. Feature-based data attribution allows for isolating specific behaviors within training data, enabling the removal of targeted behaviors with minimal masking. Ultimately, matching featurizers to representation geometry provides effective tools for understanding and controlling robot behavior.

arXiv - cs.RO

Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle

Reliable heading estimation for road vehicles is achieved through a skylight polarization navigation method. This method globally optimizes a Rayleigh sky model using measurements from a division-of-focal-plane polarimetric camera. The framework integrates a pinhole camera model, a single-scattering Rayleigh polarization model, and a solar position algorithm to synthesize polarization angle information. A polarization-weighted root mean square error objective function is used to estimate heading while mitigating occlusions. Experimental results demonstrated sub-degree RMS yaw errors and up to 87% reduction in heading error compared to other methods.

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

A mesh of interceptors defending a site must cover the predicted arrival point of an approaching object. The paper provides closed-form bounds for performance before any policy is chosen for multi-agent robotics interception. Uncertainty in predicted arrival points is bounded by least-squares variance propagation and a delta-method term. A naive area ratio misses coverage ceilings, with separation constraints imposing a limit on achievable coverage. Analytic placement achieves 85% of the configuration's potential, while learned policies achieve only 15%.

arXiv - cs.RO

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery (PGRR) is introduced to improve gait selection after irreversible leg damage by predicting multiple outcomes during repeated gait execution. It uses conservative uncertainty-aware estimates to guide short online probes without resetting the robot's state. Across diverse leg damage scenarios, PGRR yielded improvements of 0.106 in final quality and 0.044 in slot-averaged recovery quality compared to a Gaussian-process baseline. This method also reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points in tests involving leg amputation, distal fracture, and joint lock. The results indicate that handling uncertainty conservatively is the most significant factor contributing to these performance gains.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

A methodology is presented for adaptive mission planning for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels by maximizing information-driven clarity. This approach focuses on using ASVs to prioritize locations of maximum interest identified through in-situ measurements. The strategy employs a clarity-driven ergodic search, linking clarity to differential entropy, to adapt a target clarity map online. An ergodic path planner then continually re-plans based on this map and the vessel's mobility constraints from the renewable power source. Simulations and experiments conducted at Jordan Lake demonstrated the effectiveness of this method.

arXiv - cs.RO

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing

Autonomous driving safety systems need to move beyond preventing instability to actively recovering from loss of control. The research uses reinforcement learning to train a policy on drifting as a time-optimal maneuver, subsequently adapting it for recovery using a minimum-time reward. This approach, employing Gym-Khana, defines recovery relative to a stable equilibrium. The learned policy significantly improves recovery compared to existing methods, outperforming model predictive control and other recovery policies. Testing on a physical vehicle demonstrated superior recovery, including recovering from states the baseline could not handle.

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Sustainably powered mobile robots can optimize energy use to maximize information quality during long-duration missions. The research addresses a persistent speed trajectory optimization problem modeled as a periodic optimal control problem. The study uses information-theoretic clarity as a metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control. Results characterize the optimal control behavior and establish a high-speed information limit. A simulation based on salinity characterization in the Chesapeake Bay demonstrates the practical relevance of these findings.
