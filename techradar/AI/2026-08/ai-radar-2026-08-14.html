High-dimensional networks and mean squared error for possibly misspecified models

In high-dimensional network analysis, including too many variables can lead to conservative estimates of a node's neighbourhood, with the mean squared error (MSE) being low and test variance being large. For possibly misspecified models, a penalty on model volume can be used, and minimum description length leads to correct or low false positive rates in both linear and nonlinear cases.

Sinkhorn Linearization and the Spectral Proxy: Unifying the Statistical and Algorithmic Theory of Feature-Parameterized Inverse Optimal Transport via a Single Spectral Sandwich

Researchers developed the statistical and algorithmic theory of inverse optimal transport (IOT) under feature-parameterized cost C_theta(i,j). The core technical contribution is Sinkhorn linearization, an implicit-function sensitivity formula that yields a spectral proxy. This bound drives the entire theory, establishing four theorems: identifiability, sparsistency, well-posedness, and convergence; one observation regarding misspecification; and providing setting-dependent empirical exponents for the projection map's Holder continuity.

Wasserstein Filtering: A Sample Selection Method for Robust Distribution Learning

Wasserstein Filtering (WF), a novel sample selection framework, discards suspicious samples and estimates the target distribution using empirical measure of remaining data. The goal is to recover underlying clean population distribution from contaminated datasets. The WF estimator achieves minimax optimality under the Far Exclusion and Local Projection contamination model, which characterizes corruptions consisting of well-separated outliers and locally indistinguishable perturbations.

Bagging Robustly Learns VC Classes with Linear Sample Complexity

Bagging robustly learns VC classes with linear sample complexity, proving that VC classes are adversarially robustly learnable with a simple algorithm. The result improves the previous upper bound by an exponential factor and requires $\Omega(d^\star)$ calls to an RERM oracle in general. This is achieved through computing RERMs on $O(d^\star)$ independent bootstrap samples and outputs their majority vote.

Don't Cut Corners: How Training Outside the Prior Makes Simulation-Based Inference More Robust

Large astrophysical simulation campaigns often generate training data by sampling parameters across a Uniform prior box, struggling to learn accurate approximations near the boundaries. A new proposal distribution called Tailed-Uniform pads the original hard-truncated training box with decaying tails, yielding more accurate posteriors near and beyond the edges. This approach improves simulation-based inference, particularly in high dimensions where boundaries dominate parameter space volume.
