The World Won't Wait: Evaluating and Teaching Agents Under a Live Clock

LLM agents face challenges in real-time, dynamic environments where reasoning and tool calls consume time. RealtimeBench is introduced to evaluate agents while games run concurrently, showing performance differences between models under these conditions. Two-Timescale Offline Recursive Self-Improvement (TORSI) is proposed to improve agents by having a slow teacher guide learning while a fast student acts during gameplay. This method improved Jev's test scores significantly across multiple game types. The research advocates for real-time intelligence to keep LLM agents effective in continuously changing worlds.

Evaluating Model Capabilities After Benchmark Saturation

Benchmark saturation occurs when multiple models achieve near-perfect scores, diminishing their utility for differentiating performance. The study investigates whether the detailed transcripts of how models solve tasks retain capability information even after benchmark scores have saturated. Researchers tested methods like pairwise judging and feature extraction from transcripts to assess these retained capabilities. Pairwise judging proved most effective, reducing prediction error significantly compared to using only benchmark scores. Saturated benchmark transcripts contain capability-predicting signals, but robust extraction methods are needed for extended benchmarking.

Value-Informed Schedule Trimming for Accelerated Sequential Monte Carlo Guidance in Discrete Diffusion

Reward-guided discrete diffusion involves computationally expensive Sequential Monte Carlo (SMC) guidance at every denoising step. VISTA is a training-free method that automatically selects guided timesteps under a fixed budget by approximating the full-guidance path. It uses value statistics from warm-up generations to optimize the selection via dynamic programming. VISTA discovers task-dependent schedules and outperforms hand-designed sparse schedules across various generation tasks. This method significantly reduces per-generation latency while maintaining or exceeding full-guidance performance.

When Correctness Feedback Erases Evidence: Phase-Separated Completeness Auditing in Verus

Correctness feedback can suppress the evidence needed for specification-completeness auditing, which aims to find violations of intended behavior. Experiments on Verus/Rust tasks showed that independent no-feedback discovery yielded more certified witnesses than the corrective-feedback loop. Phase-separated execution methods successfully matched aggregate yields without reusing responses, leading to stronger specification repair. Preserving witnesses resulted in higher implementation success rates for contracts compared to those using corrective feedback. This research suggests that completeness auditing and correctness repair have opposing immediate goals.

Counterfactual Neighborhood Risk Fields: Evidence-Grounded Selective Safety for Vision-Language Models

Counterfactual Neighborhood Risk Fields (CNRF) is an evidence-grounded framework for selectively ensuring safety in Vision-Language Models (VLMs). It constructs context-matched benign-harmful counterfactual pairs to derive capability-related risk directions from representation differences. CNRF separates neighborhood support from risk to prevent confusing unfamiliarity with actual harmfulness. This allows for selective intervention based on victim-adaptive interfaces that route requests for review. Experiments show CNRF achieves high macro defense success rates while maintaining utility for benign tasks.

L2G-SR: Learning to Guide LLM-based Symbolic Regression

L2G-SR is a framework for LLM-based symbolic regression that uses a learned structural verifier to guide the search process. It generates a structural supervision signal using tree edit distance, which accounts for commutative operators and alternative expression forms. The verifier first aligns numerical observations with formulas and then scores the structural similarity of candidate expressions to the target. At search time, the verifier aids in selecting mathematical expressions by complementing numerical error. This approach demonstrates the value of learned structural guidance for improving LLM-based symbolic regression accuracy.

FlowBudget: Training-Free Sampling-Budget Estimation for Flow Matching Models

FlowBudget is a training-free method to estimate the sampling budget for flow matching models, addressing the dependence of inference cost on numerical integration. It uses simulation-free interpolation probes of the learned dynamics to estimate the leading one-step discretization error. FLOWBUDGET normalizes this error by motion and maps a numerical tolerance to a step budget based on the solver order. The method derives estimators for numerical solvers like Euler and Heun for ODE and SDE sampling. Experiments on various flow-model architectures validate FLOWBUDGET's ability to estimate the Necessary Function Evaluations without requiring multiple sample generations.

Expanding Attention: A Taylor-Series Route to Linear-Time Transformers with a Fixed Number of Recurrent States

The method expands the exponential attention kernel into a truncated Taylor series of $n$ terms. This factorization collapses causal history into a fixed number of recurrent states, which are updated by rank-one outer products independent of context length. This recursion is lossless, and the states are additive across chunks, allowing for parallel prefill scans. The resulting decoding latency remains nearly constant per token, offering significant speedups as context length increases. Optimal performance is achieved with $n=3$ terms, providing a good balance between accuracy and cost.

Same Answer, New Box: How Much of RLVR's Out-of-Domain Gain Is Answer Format?

Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) can yield domain-specific gains, such as higher accuracy in mathematics, which are often extracted via answer format. The study investigates how much of this out-of-domain gain stems from the answer format rather than the answer content itself. Experiments on 7B SimpleRL pairs show that 82% of the reported out-of-domain multiple-choice gain disappears when considering the format. This format gain occurs because the reinforcement learning process moves answers into a box that extractors prioritize. The magnitude of this format gain is dependent on the specific answer extractor used to read the output.

Flow maps utilize stop gradient operators and adaptive weighting, yet the impact of these heuristics on training dynamics remains unanalyzed. Complete or Sparse: A Tale of Two Identifiabilities

The study investigates identifiability in supervised learning by examining when neural networks can recover true latent variables from data. Two data generating processes are considered: generative, where labels cause latents, and discriminative, where latents cause labels. Identifiability fails when the representation has insufficient dimensions, leading to nonlinear encoders, but constraints can allow for recovery of individual latent variables. In the generative setting, certain constraints imply each neuron encodes exactly one latent, while in the discriminative setting, sparse latents can be perfectly recovered under specific conditions. This work unifies concepts from nonlinear ICA and compressed sensing to delineate the transition between nonlinear distortions and linear representations in neural networks.

Time-partitioning Gaussian Process Mixture Model

The Time-partitioning Gaussian Process Mixture Model (TPGPMM) is a Bayesian nonparametric framework designed to identify persistent market regimes and model nonstationary time series dependence. It integrates an adaptive Pitman--Yor prior with regime-specific Gaussian processes, avoiding strict Markovian dependence within regimes. A split--merge Metropolis-within-Gibbs sampler is used to jointly learn change points and model parameters, quantifying uncertainty over segmentations. Simulations show the model effectively recovers the regime structure, successfully identifying market events in data like the VIX. The model demonstrates a trade-off between interpretable segmentation and predictive accuracy, with variants adjusting observation noise for improved prediction.

Stochastic Gradient Descent: A Nonlinear Time Series Perspective

The paper investigates the statistical behavior of Stochastic Gradient Descent (SGD) with a constant step size under iterated random functions. It establishes convergence in Euclidean distance by demonstrating the Geometric Moment Contraction (GMC) of SGD. The work proves quenched central limit theorem, invariance principle, and $\alpha$-stable convergence for averaged SGD, irrespective of initialization. Finally, a new online inference method and a bias-reduced variant for extrapolated ASGD are introduced.

Ranked Right, Sized Wrong: Ensemble Spread Fails Silently at Streamflow Extremes

Deep learning models using ensemble spread for streamflow forecasting face issues at extreme flow conditions. Prediction error is significantly larger than the spread at high flows, increasing by a factor of up to four. Under-dispersion, where the model underestimates uncertainty, is severe in extreme flows, reaching an $18\times$ discrepancy. While ensemble spread ranking remains near-oracle for uncertainty, deployment relies on this signal despite systematic underestimation of risk for critical extreme events. Consequently, the uncertainty signal in ensemble spread may systematically underestimate the true risk during flow extremes.
