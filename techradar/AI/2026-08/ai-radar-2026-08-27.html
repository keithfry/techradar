Common-Center Geometry and Certified Radial Reconstruction for Energy-Form Full Conformal Regions

The paper studies the geometry of full conformal prediction regions derived from an empirical energy-form pairwise score. It shows that under certain symmetry and convexity conditions, comparison regions contain a common minimizer, implying the exact conformal region is star-shaped about that point. Minimax Alternating Regret for the Experts Problem and Online Convex Optimization

The paper studies alternating regret in online convex optimization, motivated by alternating learning in two-player games. It establishes matching lower and upper bounds for both the expert problem and general online convex optimization. Specifically, for the $d$-expert problem, the minimax alternating regret is $\Theta(\log d)$, independent of the time horizon $T$. For general OCO over a $d$-dimensional set, the worst-case minimax alternating regret is $\Theta\left(d\log \left(1+\frac{T}{d}\right)\right)$. These results improve upon previously established bounds in the field.

Exact Bayesian prediction has fast regret guarantees, but exact posterior updating is often too costly for online learning. The paper investigates how computational approximations of the posterior affect these statistical guarantees. GRAPE: Gradient Refinement and Progress-Aware Exploitation for Query-Efficient High-Dimensional Bayesian Optimization

GRAPE is a two-stage framework for query-efficient high-dimensional Bayesian optimization. It first sharpens the local gradient posterior using a closed-form acquisition function for gradient refinement. Subsequently, it selects update directions by maximizing the expected decrease conditional on descent, exploiting progress-aware exploitation. Theoretical analysis shows that gradient refinement minimizes uncertainty while progress-aware direction converges to steepest descent. Empirically, GRAPE achieves significant query speedups in adversarial attacks and prompt optimization tasks.
