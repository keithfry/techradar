Artificiality's First Exam: An LLM-Referenced C-test for Supraintelligence

The paper proposes a C-test for supraintelligence based on Levin's Kt, which relates difficulty to the complexity of the underlying mechanism. It uses Large Language Models (LLMs) as representational machines to conduct this test. The research formalizes a measure and develops a pipeline to certify test items based on this framework. A battery of certified items was administered to twelve LLMs, showing agent characteristics decreasing with difficulty. This work explores the limits of certification for supra-intelligence beyond current frontier models.

The Silent Failures in Agentic Data Science

Evaluations of agentic data science often focus only on downstream performance and code correctness, overlooking fundamental data science principles. The research argues that these assessments fail to capture methodological failures like data leakage and inappropriate validation. The authors conducted a multi-faceted evaluation across 220 tasks, a custom test suite, and qualitative analysis against expert solutions. Findings indicate that most agentic solutions contain failures, and data leakage correlates with poor predictive performance. Ultimately, principled failure mode analysis should be integrated into agentic data science evaluations for building trustworthy systems.

ClawLoop: Self-Improving Agents via Harness-Native Training Loop

ClawLoop is a framework designed to enable self-improving agents within harness environments without relying on external teacher models. It operates through a self-sustaining cycle involving Task Synthesis, Training, and Diagnosis and Feedback phases. Task Synthesis generates training tasks, while Diagnosis analyzes agent behavior to produce feedback for subsequent task generation. The process involves updating the agent via Supervised Fine-Tuning or Reinforcement Learning. Using Qwen3.6-27B, ClawLoop achieved consistent improvements on several benchmarks, with gains also observed when transferring refined tasks to smaller models.

Correction Training for Diffusion Models

Iterative sampling in diffusion models causes error accumulation due to exposure bias between training and inference. A correction step is introduced to reduce this training-inference gap by training a corrector against the base model's predictions. During sampling, this corrector iteratively refines the denoiser's clean-sample predictions at intermediate denoising steps. This approach substantially improves sample quality without losing diversity and achieves performance competitive with classifier-free guidance. The method shows that the increased inference cost can be managed by distilling refined predictions back into the base model.

Long-Horizon-Terminal-Bench 2.0 extends previous benchmarks to measure an agent's ability to sustain progress over extended workflows using process rewards. Do Vision CIL Methods Transfer to Protein? A Diagnostic Benchmark of Frozen Protein Encoders

The research benchmarks how class-incremental learning (CIL) methods, typically developed for vision, transfer to frozen protein encoders. The study tested nine CIL methods across five different protein encoders and two enzyme function datasets. The results showed that no method reliably transfers across these encoders, indicating instability in the transfer process. Reliable protein CIL requires encoder-aware methods that adapt to the specific geometry of the representation.

Do Perturbation Predictors Learn Gene Interactions? Diagnosing Extrapolation in Agentic Screening

Machine learning models are used in high-throughput perturbation screens to predict gene effects for untested combinations. Standard out-of-distribution methods often fail when predicting unseen gene interactions, even if individual genes were seen during training. The study introduces "additive disagreement" (AD) to measure the extra interaction effect introduced by the model beyond a simple sum of single-gene effects. Additive disagreement proves to be an informative signal across various compositional settings for agentic perturbation-screening workflows.

Token Utility for On-Policy Distillation via Local Trust-Region Optimization

Token importance for on-policy distillation is derived from the distillation objective, specifically how much loss can decrease under a local KL budget. This utility is calculated as $s_t=g_t^\top F_t^\dagger g_t$, where $g_t$ is the logit gradient and $F_t$ is the categorical Fisher matrix. This method, termed Fisher Utility Weighting (FUW), concentrates importance on relevant positions across various distillation criteria. FUW improves performance over uniform distillation for multiple benchmarks and Qwen3 models under both reverse-KL and forward-KL settings. The derived utility is competitive with existing selective methods based on a single objective score.

PMO-Dock introduces a benchmark and protocol for docking-based molecular optimization, addressing the lack of standardization in the field. Results indicate that no single method is universally superior, suggesting method-dependent selection strategies based on generalization and sample efficiency.

IR-PRM+ with Previous-Utility Prediction: Constant Regret and Best-Iterate Convergence

The work introduces an approach for regret matching in game solving that incorporates previous-utility prediction to achieve stronger theoretical guarantees. This method, IR-PRM+, recovers the constant-regret and convergence-rate guarantees previously seen with extra-gradient prediction for simultaneous and alternating updates. The technique bounds strategy movements and uses the relative growth of the regret-vector norm to prevent unbounded norm growth. These guarantees extend to finite two-player zero-sum matrix games and corresponding extensive-form games under specific conditions. Consequently, the current duality gap vanishes, and the current profile converges to the Nash equilibrium set.

How Many Ranks Does a Token Need? A Noise-Floor Squelch for Rank-One LoRA Experts

Low-rank adaptation (LoRA) applies rank-one updates to every token, prompting a question about the necessary number of components per token. The paper introduces SQUELCH, a router-free method that uses soft-thresholding and Stein's unbiased risk estimate to dynamically select active ranks per token. This technique suppresses redundant components by performing an explaining-away step on the value Gram matrix. SQUELCH improves performance over standard LoRA and mixture-of-experts baselines on various reasoning tasks. On commonsense reasoning tasks, SQUELCH activates an average of 8.6 out of 32 components per token.

Bayesian Causal Forests for Zero-Inflated Continuous Outcomes

Estimating treatment effects is difficult when outcomes exhibit a discrete zero mass and a skewed continuous distribution. The proposed method, ZIC-BCF-Smear, is a decoupled, two-part nonparametric estimator for zero-inflated continuous outcomes. It models participation with a Probit BCF and positive intensity with a Gaussian BCF, using Propensity Adjustment and Duan's smearing for re-transformation. Simulations confirm this method matches well-specified models without their assumptions. The technique was applied to estimate effects in a cancer trial concerning severe toxicity.

Delay Complexity Dimension: Learning with Delayed and Censored Labels

The study investigates the PAC learning of binary classifiers when labels are delivered with random delays and are censored beyond an observation window $W$. It introduces the Delay Complexity Dimension (DCD), which combines the VC dimension with the exact label-information rate of the observation channel. The research shows that learners require $\Omega(\mathrm{DCD}/\varepsilon^2)$ samples, and an oracle pseudo-label ERM achieves this rate. The theory also suggests a quantization scheme where delay information reduces residual label variance, offering an expedition policy guarantee. Experiments indicate that a moderate window can outperform immediate labels, supported by synthetic data and log analysis.

The paper investigates Wong--Zakai convergence and Fourier Neural Operator (FNO) learning for stochastic Volterra integral equations (SVIEs) that model systems with memory. A Wong--Zakai theorem supports the convergence of pathwise solutions when using piecewise linear approximations of Brownian motion. Semi-Amortized Bayesian Inference with Reusable Local Posteriors

Amortized Bayesian inference is introduced to estimate parameters when likelihoods are computationally expensive or unavailable. The method divides parameters into blocks, leveraging conditional independence to identify necessary information for each block. Neural estimators are trained via simulations to approximate the full conditional posterior for expensive blocks. A Markov chain alternates between draws from these estimators and conventional updates for the remaining variables. This approach allows for the reuse of estimators across different blocks and datasets in complex models.

The research introduces physics-informed weak regression (PIWR) for parameter estimation in epidemic models, aiming to provide distribution-free uncertainty. PIWR integrates the model over time, estimates the initial state jointly, and requires no data derivatives. This method, combined with split conformal prediction, yields simultaneous parameter intervals with finite-sample coverage guarantees. The approach facilitates fast estimation while ensuring reliable uncertainty quantification for disease models.

Generalizing Identifiability Results in Causal Discovery from Complete Data to Missing Data Settings

Causal discovery algorithms have been developed for complete data, but methods for handling missing values remain limited. The research establishes general conditions under which causal graphs remain identifiable even with various missingness categories, including Missing Not At Random. Identifiability is preserved if missingness mechanisms do not causally interact, no variable causes its own missingness, and faithfulness or generalized linear models hold for the missingness. Based on these findings, two asymptotically correct algorithms are introduced for Additive Noise Models to recover causal graphs. These results allow for the extension of correctness guarantees from complete data to settings with missing data.
