ROS Discourse General: Introducing NoDL: the Node Definition Language (ROSCon recap)

The presentation at ROSCon introduced "The Legible Node," focusing on thin nodes and the Node Definition Language (NoDL) for statically declaring node interfaces. A NoDL document specifies node interactions using sections for parameters, publishers, subscribers, and services/actions. This language allows users to assert node conformity, generate documentation, and map applications statically within ROSGraph. The NoDL project is being developed in the ROSGraph Working Group with milestones focusing on code generation and graph analysis. Interested parties can join the mailing list and resources to contribute to NoDL.

State of Robots in Manufacturing

Robotics in manufacturing is evolving beyond simple programmed arms to include advancements in perception, autonomy, and actuation. Demand is increasing across various sectors due to skilled labor shortages, with industrial robot installations doubling in the decade leading up to 2024. Collaborative robots, autonomous mobile robots, and physical AI are enabling more flexible automation solutions. Despite growth, challenges remain in application identification, integration, and ensuring return on investment. Robot order value increased by 6.6% year-over-year in the first half of 2026, with growth seen in non-automotive sectors.

Robotics and automation? Informal reflections on familiar terms

IEEE experts informally discussed the terms "robotics" and "automation" to explore their relationship and significance. Automation focuses on making processes repeatable and efficient, while robotics involves giving automation a physical presence. The distinction is complicated because not all automation requires robots, and not all robots are autonomous. The definitions impact international comparisons, education, and public perception of technological strength. Ultimately, the relevance of the distinction depends on the context and the audience.

Passive-Dynamic-Walking-Inspired Dynamics Guidance for Energy-Efficient Humanoid Locomotion

The research proposes a framework inspired by passive-dynamic walking to guide learning energy-efficient gait coordination in humanoid locomotion. This method temporarily simulates slope-equivalent conditions during training to encourage economical gait discovery. The framework achieves mechanical cost reduction by leveraging a tilted-gravity field and curriculum-coupled rewards without needing reference trajectories. Experiments on a 29-DoF robot showed a 6.8-15.2% reduction in mechanical cost of transport at commanded speeds of 0.5-2.0 m/s. The approach also extends to omnidirectional locomotion, reducing speed-matched cost of transport by 18.7%.

arXiv - cs.RO

In-Context Learning for Robots: Methods and Applications

In-context learning (ICL) allows general-purpose robots to infer task requirements from demonstrations and interactions. ICL directs existing robot competence by keeping neural parameters fixed during deployment. The literature review categorizes ICL interfaces into four families: context-conditioned policies, geometric demonstration transfer, world-model-based control, and skill- and agent-based execution. The analysis examines how these mechanisms preserve learned requirements across changes in objects, environments, and execution conditions. Ultimately, the work connects method design to evaluation to enable compositional task acquisition and physical recursive self-improvement.

SAKI: Skill Assembly and Kinematic Imitation from Human Videos for Long-Horizon Mobile Manipulation

SAKI is a framework designed for skill assembly and kinematic imitation from human videos to enable long-horizon mobile manipulation. It connects human-video skill acquisition with cross-demonstration assembly and closed-loop whole-body execution. The framework prepares reusable object-centric skills while allowing transfer paths to adapt across different scenes. It selects and orders skills based on a goal, binds object roles, and carries scene estimates during execution. Real-robot experiments demonstrated skill reuse and composition for complex mobile tasks like tidying.

arXiv - cs.RO

MagNav: A Dual-Core Magnetic Track Guidance Framework for Lighting-Invariant Navigation in Two-Wheeled Robots

The MagNav framework provides a dual-core magnetic track guidance system for lighting-invariant navigation in two-wheeled robots. It utilizes a five-channel Hall-effect sensor array to avoid interference from optical sensors, which are susceptible to lighting changes. The control system employs a cascaded PID structure, using an IMU for balancing and magnetic sensor readings for steering. This design maintains accurate tracking even in very bright light conditions, unlike the optical system. Consequently, this method offers a reliable, lighting-independent solution for autonomous navigation in various environments.

arXiv - cs.RO

Scouting the Dynamics Gap: Test-Time Policy Adaptation via Action-Outcome Feedback

Pretrained robotic policies need to rapidly adapt to unobserved physical dynamics during deployment. Existing test-time adaptation methods often fail to use rich geometric and dynamic feedback from physical interaction. SCOUT is a dynamics-aware meta-learning framework that enables policies to adapt by continuously revising internal beliefs about environment dynamics. It couples an action-prediction policy with a forward dynamics model using a shared belief latent space. This approach accelerates online adaptation and improves sim-to-real transfer by updating latent beliefs based on action-outcome mismatches.

arXiv - cs.RO

KPI: A Promptable Kernel for Physical Interaction on Humanoids

KPI is a promptable kernel designed to manage physical interaction for humanoids. It allows a trajectory source to communicate contracts, specifying how the robot should behave upon contact, instead of relying on a fixed controller. The kernel adapts the arms' stiffness, damping, and reference based on tracking error and wrench estimates at the contact rate. This system is demonstrated using an agentic framework where a vision-language agent generates both the reference trajectory and the interaction contract. Examples include instruction-driven winch operation and surface interactions.

arXiv - cs.RO

SkillWeaver: Agentic Exploration over Neural Interaction Skills for Scalable Robot Data Generation

SkillWeaver is an agentic framework designed to autonomously generate large-scale robot experience by exploring over Neural Interaction Skills (NIS). These NIS are reusable, parameterized, closed-loop policies that expose learned physical interaction capabilities to a reasoning agent. A VLM agent uses these skills to interact with the environment, observing outcomes and generating reflection to guide further exploration. The framework scales to a large amount of data and results in visuomotor policies that significantly improve generalization in robot tasks. This approach offers a scalable alternative for generating robot data through agentic exploration.

arXiv - cs.RO

Test-Time Adaptation of Manipulation Policies Under Actuator Degradation

Robot manipulation policies often fail under real-world conditions due to actuator degradation, such as overheating or voltage sags. Telemetry-Aware Action Rectification (TeAR) is introduced as a policy-agnostic method to adapt frozen policies using onboard sensor data. TeAR employs a lightweight Transformer to modify outgoing actions by combining them with live actuator telemetry. Evaluation shows TeAR improves success under heating by 10-15% on a physical arm without requiring on-robot fine-tuning.

arXiv - cs.RO

Design and Validation of an Antagonistic Tendon-Driven Dexterous Robotic Hand with Bidirectional Operation

A 17-DoF robotic hand was designed using an antagonistic tendon-routing mechanism for bidirectional operation. This mechanism allows active control over flexion/extension and abduction/adduction across all degrees of freedom, enabling grasping on both palmar and dorsal sides. Experimental results show low motor transmission error (3.0%) and good force application (up to 29 N). The hand successfully performed all 33 GRASP Taxonomy grasp types, validating its bidirectional grasping capability. The system demonstrates a compact, human-scale platform for dexterous manipulation.

arXiv - cs.RO

Bilinear World Models: Learning Representations with Structured Dynamics for Efficient Control

The work proposes a JEPA-style world model that constrains latent dynamics to a bilinear parameterization for efficient control. This structure is used to enforce action recoverability, which prevents representation collapse by construction. Empirical results show that learning representations with bilinear dynamics significantly reduces planning time while maintaining or improving control accuracy in 2D and 3D tasks. The method is demonstrated to succeed in demanding regimes like longer-horizon planning and real-time control. Ultimately, the approach extends JEPA-style world models beyond short-horizon offline planning.
