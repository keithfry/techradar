World Action Models (WAMs) aim to combine predicting how the world changes with generating actions for robots. World models capture how the world changes by learning the rules behind physical and digital transitions. Why Temperature 0 Isn't Deterministic

Temperature 0 outputs are mathematically deterministic but can exhibit unpredictability due to floating-point arithmetic and hardware-dependent operations. Differences arise from non-batch-invariant kernels and the dependence on the order of operations when summing logits. The actual risk of a token flip depends on the "gap" between the two most probable tokens and the magnitude of numerical noise, proportional to the density of ties. Risk is highly concentrated on "knife-edge" tokens, leading to sudden, spiky divergence in long completions. Predictions can be made by estimating a per-token flip rate based on the model's properties and the system's noise level.

Approaches to VLA have explored various architectures, including Gemini Robotics for embodied reasoning, $\pi_0$ for flow-based action modeling, and SmolVLA for device accessibility.
