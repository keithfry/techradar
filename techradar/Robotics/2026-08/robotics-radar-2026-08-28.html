ROS Discourse General: first VIO demo: global-shutter camera + IMU streamed over one GMSL2 coax, mapped live in ROS 2

A demonstration was performed using a global-shutter camera and an IMU streamed over a single GMSL2 coax cable. This data was mapped live in ROS 2 using a Jetson platform and OpenVINS. The system utilized an NXS sensor module featuring a Sony IMX900 global shutter and an IAM-20680 IMU. The setup involved real-time estimation without any wheels, markers, or prior map. The test resulted in a return-to-start error of 42 cm after a 25-meter loop.

How Robots Are Reshaping Automotive Glass Production

Automotive glass manufacturing is difficult to automate due to the material's tendency to shatter and the precision required for downstream processes. Modern automation uses six-axis articulated robots combined with vision systems to accurately pick and place irregularly shaped glass blanks. The shift to electric vehicles is increasing demands for higher precision and consistent quality in glass components. Successful automation requires assessing volume consistency, geometry stability, breakage costs, and downstream process tolerances before investment. Future advancements will focus on predictive breakage models to move from reactive handling to proactive quality control.

Constraint-Aware Physics-Informed Neural Networks for Static Shape Estimation of Co-Manipulative Continuum Robots

A constraint-aware physics-informed neural network (PINN) is proposed for the static shape estimation of co-manipulative continuum robots (CCRs). The method incorporates residuals for static equilibrium and geometric loop-closure constraints based on the geometric variable strain formulation. Simulation results show the PINN significantly reduces errors compared to purely data-driven models when trained with limited and noisy data. The PINN achieved high accuracy with fast inference times, demonstrating physically consistent and computationally efficient shape estimation. Experimental fine-tuning further improved marker RMSE and the R-squared value of the estimation.

arXiv - cs.RO

Dispersive Forward Tree Search for Optimal Control: Coverage, Complexity, and Computation

The paper develops a propagation-based kinodynamic planner with near-optimal guarantees for state-to-state boundary value problems in nonlinear systems. It proposes a Dispersive Forward Tree search method using locally dispersive control commands to find near-optimal trajectories. The method includes a cost-conditioned dominance pruning procedure to maintain near-optimality while keeping the search tree size polynomial in the horizon. The resulting Dispersive Forward Tree search (DFT*) is implemented as a breadth-first expansion suitable for parallel hardware. DFT* demonstrates competitive and improved solution quality over existing planners on embedded processors.

Cross-Platform Benchmark of Neural 3D Reconstruction for Autonomous Laboratory Robots

The research benchmarks neural 3D reconstruction methods like NeRF and 3D Gaussian Splatting across various compute platforms for autonomous laboratory robots. The study evaluates Meta's SAM3D single-image reconstruction on these same platforms to assess performance gaps. Results indicate that Gaussian Splatting offers better rendering quality than NeRF at higher computational costs. Onboard compute is generally insufficient for full per-scene optimization at interactive rates. Consequently, a tiered pipeline is suggested where lightweight methods handle real-time loops and heavier methods are applied selectively.

arXiv - cs.RO

RTNav: Towards Real-Time Zero-Shot Object Navigation

Zero-shot object navigation methods struggle with performance under real-time execution constraints due to inference latency. The proposed RTNav architecture addresses this by explicitly considering inference latency, asynchronous stepping, and compute bounds. This approach treats these factors as explicit design considerations rather than ignoring them in sequential execution models. Evaluation on real-time variants of HM3D models showed RTNav improved success rates by up to 11% and success weighted by completion time by up to 5.1 points. RTNav demonstrates a method for more efficient, real-time navigation in unknown environments.

Closing the Loop on the Poppy Humanoid: Bipedal Locomotion with Linear-Quadratic Control and Learned Cost Functions

The research addresses the lack of a reliable methodology for unassisted bipedal locomotion on the Poppy Humanoid. The authors propose a closed-loop walking controller utilizing the Linear-Quadratic Regulator (LQR) framework for trajectory tracking. They learn a quadratic cost function for the LQR controller using data from open-loop playback of a nominal walking trajectory. This learned cost function significantly improves the reliability of the motion. Empirical validation showed statistically significant improvements in walking performance compared to open-loop playback.

Memory Anchors for Continual Robot Learning

To prevent catastrophic forgetting in continual robot learning, the paper introduces "Memory Anchors" derived from past experiences. These anchors are identified in regions where new task representations overlap with old ones despite conflicting action requirements. Rehearsing data in these anchor regions is crucial for preventing the overwriting of prior knowledge. Including these anchors in the replay buffer significantly reduces high-conflict task forgetting. This approach enables successful continual learning across multiple task sequences on real robots.

arXiv - cs.RO

TrapVLA: Trapping Vision-Language-Action Models in Configured Failure Modes

This work introduces Configured Failure Trapping, a novel backdoor attack task for Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models that requires controlling specific failure modes. It necessitates attackers to control how the robot fails, making the attack more challenging and harder to detect than previous methods. The authors developed a data engine and measurement suite to support this task, creating benchmarks like Trap-LIBERO and Trap-RoboTwin. TrapVLA is proposed to learn trigger-induced action residuals to successfully steer the policy into the configured failure behaviors. Experiments confirm that TrapVLA effectively injects these failure modes while largely maintaining performance on clean data.

arXiv - cs.RO

SOLO: Stable Omni-terrain Long-Horizon Perceptive Humanoid Locomotion

SOLO is a framework designed for stable, long-horizon, perceptive humanoid locomotion across various terrains. It addresses fragility in humanoid policies by managing dense terrain reconstruction and temporal credit assignment errors. The Query Reconstructor (QR) uses Fourier-encoded queries to preserve sharp terrain boundaries from depth-proprioception data. Trajectory-Aware MSE Distillation enhances learning by incorporating next-state disagreement into the reward signal. SOLO achieves high success rates on stress-test terrains and can complete autonomous navigation routes using limited sensory input.

Relaxation-Aware Multimodal Sensing of Soft Gripper Driven by Structure-Perception-Learning

Soft robotic grippers struggle with force decay due to viscoelastic stress relaxation during holding. This research introduces a framework combining structure-perception and learning for relaxation-aware sensing. The system uses onboard vision and infrared thermography to track soft gripper deformation and temperature in real time. A temperature-coupled viscoelastic force representation and a physics-informed learning model are used to compensate for force decay. Experiments demonstrated this method maintains desired grasp force with high accuracy, outperforming existing baselines.
