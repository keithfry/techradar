This Disembodied Hand Is All the Robot You Need

Researchers at ETH Zurich transformed an off-the-shelf robotic hand into a self-contained robot that walks on its fingertips to manipulate the environment. This work demonstrates that body parts can perform functions beyond their original design, opening possibilities for new robotics. The team used reinforcement learning and novel penalty systems to train the asymmetric hand to crawl and navigate various surfaces. The mobile hand successfully demonstrated the ability to right itself, turn, and push objects autonomously in real-world tests. This research supports a vision for "autonomous modular embodiments" where robot parts can dynamically transition between different tasks.

Does Dynamic-Point Filtering Help When Texture Is Scarce? A Controlled Study of ORB-SLAM2 Front-Ends in Synthetic Indoor Scenes

A controlled study investigated whether dynamic-point filtering assists ORB-SLAM2 front-ends when texture is scarce in synthetic indoor scenes. The experiment varied surface texture and scene dynamics factorially across identical camera trajectories. Three filtering methods, including an optical-flow filter and a multi-view depth-consistency filter, were compared against the unfiltered ORB-SLAM2. Filtering primarily helped in the most dynamic areas, but for static scenes, filtering reduced tracking completeness and sometimes led to poor initialization. The results indicated that the benefit of filtering depended on the specific filter and the environmental conditions.

Teaching a Robot Dog New Tricks: Diverse Quadruped Skills via Combined Reinforcement and Imitation Learning with Adversarial Task Selection

A method is proposed to teach legged robots diverse quadruped skills by combining Reinforcement Learning and Imitation Learning. The approach uses a three-stage process involving training multiple teacher policies on specific tasks. Subsequently, a student policy is trained using a multi-teacher distillation setup with a combined RL and IL objective guided by adversarial task selection. This method trains a student policy capable of performing 22 tasks, preserving motion quality and showing generalization to new tasks. The resulting policy was successfully deployed on a Unitree B1 quadruped.

arXiv - cs.RO

TacHair: Tactile Contact-Distribution Guided Online Correction for Robotic Hair Stroking and Perception

TacHair is a framework that uses high-resolution tactile observations to guide online correction for robotic hair stroking and perception. It represents tactile data as a spatial hair-contact distribution to address the difficulty of regulating interactions with deformable hair. A visuotactile imitation policy generates the nominal motion, while a residual module separately corrects local contact deviations. Evaluations showed that this method improved both task progression and contact maintenance during hair stroking. The results confirm that spatial tactile contact distributions effectively aid contact-preserving interaction with visually occluded surfaces.

Masked Generative Motion Planning with Geometry-Guided Token Search

Masked Generative Motion Planning (MGMP) extends learned motion priors for structural repair, moving beyond local refinement. It utilizes a masked generative transformer to create discrete trajectory candidates in parallel. Geometry-Guided Token Search (GGTS) then uses scene geometry to efficiently search for the best edits and alternatives among these candidates. This approach enables route-level restructuring rather than just local trajectory deformation. The method achieved high success rates on various planning tasks and generalizes well to unseen scenarios.

TAPNAV: Humanoid Navigation through Tactile Active Perception

TAPNAV is a tactile active-perception framework designed for humanoid navigation in vision-denied environments. It allows the robot to actively probe the surroundings to gather localization cues without relying on vision. The framework combines uncertainty-aware global route planning with information-gain-driven local probing. A whole-body controller coordinates locomotion and end-effector actions to execute the planned navigation and probing. Evaluations show TAPNAV achieves lower state estimation errors and higher task completion rates than existing methods.

NavGPT-3: Harnessing Context in a Hierarchical Navigation Runtime

NavGPT-3 is a harness that connects language models with physical action policies via an operating system-like runtime. This runtime manages reasoning, acting, and monitoring threads, allowing the robot to react to real-world events through interruption and thread switching. The system utilizes a NavGPT VLA action policy that allocates visual tokens based on scene changes. With the complete harness, NavGPT-3 achieves state-of-the-art performance in navigation benchmarks and approaches human-level performance in following paths. The integration significantly reduces reaction time from language-model decisions to action-policy steps, demonstrating a crucial connection between intelligence and physical control.

Same Action, Different Outcome: Variability in Dynamic Cloth Manipulation

Variability exists in the outcomes of dynamic cloth manipulation even when the same trajectory is applied. This variability has not been systematically characterized, making it crucial for assessing the reliability of learned policies and simulations. Experiments involved running the same trajectory across various tasks with different cloth properties and speeds, yielding significant variability in marker positions. This variability is primarily driven by cloth physical properties and increases with motion speed. Furthermore, current cloth simulators fail to reproduce the magnitude and ordering of the variability observed in real cloth.

arXiv - cs.RO

Diagnosing and Recovering from Observation-Space Shift at Long-Horizon Skill Seams

Long-horizon skill chaining often suffers from Observation-Space Shift (OSS) where performance degrades due to displaced scene states left by preceding skills. The dominant cause of this shift is displaced scene state, rather than robot configuration or manipulated objects. A proposed solution is a detect-restore-resume system that monitors for stalls, restores scene components, and allows the skill to resume. This system significantly improves full-chain success on the BOSS-44 benchmark, outperforming other methods. Results suggest scene restoration is a more effective strategy for long-horizon composition failures than simply retrying from an off-support state.

arXiv - cs.RO

Skill-SLM: Agent Skill-driven Small Language Models for Reliable Robot Operation

Skill-SLM proposes a framework for reliable robot operation by treating it as a task-decomposition and skill-composition problem for Small Language Models (SLMs). It uses a robot operational skill-aware context-free grammar to create a skill library from natural language task instructions. LLM teachers are used to synthesize training data, enabling SLMs to reliably decompose tasks and orchestrate skills. A progressive skill orchestration strategy further enhances the reliability of skill implementation. Experiments on UAV and ground vehicle tasks show Skill-SLM significantly outperforms distillation-oriented baselines, especially on unseen tasks.

arXiv - cs.RO

Cross-Embodiment Robot Foundation World Models with Latent Actions

The Latent Action-Conditioned Robot World Model (LAC-WM) introduces a unified latent action space to address the challenge of building robot world models that generalize across different embodiments. This unified space improves world model performance when adapting to novel robot embodiments compared to an Explicit Action-Conditioned World Model (EAC-WM). Evaluation on dexterous manipulation and LIBERO benchmarks shows LAC-WM outperforms EAC-WM by significant margins. The unified action space allows LAC-WM's performance to scale positively with the number of pretraining embodiments. These results emphasize the benefit of a unified action space for efficient cross-embodiment learning in robotics.
