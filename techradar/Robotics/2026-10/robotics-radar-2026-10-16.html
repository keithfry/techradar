The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI bets that robotic intelligence should be transferable across different hardware rather than being rebuilt for every machine. Their research focuses on connecting motor adaptation, video-prompted task learning, and self-play to achieve this goal. The core idea is to separate intelligence from the physical machine so that understanding a body is learned by the model. The company aims for the model to learn body understanding rather than requiring engineers to specify every detail perfectly beforehand. The ultimate test is whether these capabilities can survive the real-world requirements of speed, reliability, and safety in deployment.

Closing the Loop: How Automated Plants Turn Wastewater into Process Water

Industrial water reuse involves treating effluent to be returned as process water, which can reduce fresh water consumption and discharge volume. Successful reuse requires a four-step process: diagnose water, engineer treatment, pilot the system, and deploy the full-scale system. Automation systems instrument the water loop using probes for conductivity, pH, and ORP, with a PLC controlling chemical dosing and water diversion. Recovered water is used for various applications like cooling tower makeup, washdown, or process feed, each with specific quality requirements. Implementing this system rewards the same control discipline used in production lines by measuring real input and proving performance before scaling.

Tesla patent reveals touch-sensitive skin for robot hands

Tesla filed a US patent for a touch-sensitive "skin" designed to mold around robot fingers and palms for detecting contact, force, and pressure. The patent describes a manufacturing process where tactile sensors are printed onto flexible materials and then heat-formed into three-dimensional shapes. The proposed sensor utilizes overlapping conductive regions on flexible substrate layers to register pressure changes through capacitive and piezoresistive methods. The technology emphasizes manufacturability using established techniques like screen printing and thermoforming for scalable production. This innovation aims to advance humanoid robots beyond basic grasping by enabling them to feel how firmly they are manipulating objects.

Skild AI trains robots to learn physical skills through self-play

Skild AI developed a self-play training method using its S1 model to teach robots physical soccer skills through simulated competition. The robots learned complex behaviors like dribbling and tackling by playing against increasingly capable versions of themselves, rather than receiving explicit behavioral instructions. This method allowed the robots to develop skills from a single objective, leading to capabilities that emerged from the competitive process. After 140 years of simulation, the learned policy was successfully transferred to a physical humanoid robot. This approach aims to reduce the reliance on human demonstrations for teaching robots complex physical skills.

Causal Analysis of Robot Foundation Models Enables Efficiency Gains

Causal analysis of vision-language-action (VLA) models investigates how perceptual and semantic representations are transformed into actions. The study found that causally relevant information influences the action expert through a specific "action-conditioning interface" in mid-to-late cross-attention layers. Restricting the action expert conditioning in one model reduced inference FLOPs by up to $33\%$ while maintaining task success. This principle was also demonstrated for real-robot deployment of other models. The research suggests that causal understanding can be directly used to improve the efficiency of robot foundation models.

arXiv - cs.RO

Multi-Dimensional Featurizers Yield Fine-Grained Steering and Data Shaping in Robot Foundation Models

Multi-dimensional featurizers, known as block-sparse featurizers (BSFs), are investigated to recover interpretable structure in robot foundation model features. These featurizers successfully capture task-relevant features such as perception, language, and proprioceptive state underlying the model's computation. Fine-grained steering experiments demonstrate that manipulating these features causally affects downstream action predictions. Furthermore, feature-based data attribution allows for isolating the specific training tokens responsible for observed behaviors like failure recovery. Overall, matching featurizers to representation geometry provides a more effective method for understanding and controlling robot behavior.

arXiv - cs.RO

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

The paper establishes feasibility ceilings for drone-mesh interception before any learning policy is chosen. It provides closed-form bounds for performance based on the spread and timing of agents covering a predicted arrival point. Analysis shows that naive area ratios miss coverage ceilings, indicating constraints from separation, not the problem itself. Optimal packing is achieved by hexagonal arrangement, and other configurations yield less coverage. Analytic placement achieves 85% of the configuration's potential, while learned policies achieve significantly less, suggesting these bounds should guide learning experiments.

arXiv - cs.RO

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent profile-guided reset-free recovery (PGRR) is introduced to improve final gait selection after irreversible leg damage. This method predicts multiple outcomes over repeated gait execution and uses uncertainty-aware estimates to guide short online probes without resetting the robot. Across 108 matched tasks involving leg amputation, fracture, and joint lock, PGRR improved final quality by 0.106 and slot-averaged recovery quality by 0.044 compared to a baseline. Furthermore, it reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points in all damage classes. Conservative uncertainty handling was identified as the clearest contributor to these performance gains.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

A methodology is presented for adaptive mission planning for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels. This strategy maximizes the information-driven quantity of clarity, which is mathematically linked to differential entropy. The approach uses a clarity-driven ergodic search to adapt a target clarity map based on in-situ measurements. An ergodic path planner continually re-plans considering the target clarity map and mobility restrictions from the solar power source. Simulations at Jordan Lake demonstrate the effectiveness of this approach for such ASVs.

arXiv - cs.RO

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing

Existing safety systems fail when autonomous vehicles lose control due to events like icing or hydroplaning, as they focus on preventing instability rather than recovery. The research trains a racing policy using reinforcement learning to learn drifting as a time-optimal maneuver, then adapts it for recovery with a minimum-time reward. The method defines recovery based on a stable equilibrium and uses Gym-Khana for controller training and evaluation. This transferred policy significantly improves recovery over baseline methods and outperforms other controllers. The policy, when deployed on a physical vehicle, demonstrated the ability to recover from states the baseline could never handle.

arXiv - cs.RO

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Sustainably powered mobile robots can maximize information quality by optimizing their speed trajectories while managing energy. This involves solving a persistent, infinite-horizon speed trajectory optimization problem posed as a periodic optimal control problem. The study uses information-theoretic clarity as a metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control, leading to follow-on properties characterizing the optimal speed choices. The research also establishes a high-speed information limit as energy reserves approach infinity. Simulation results, based on salinity characterization in the Chesapeake Bay, demonstrate the practical relevance of these optimized trajectories.

arXiv - cs.RO

BubbleCBF: Constructive Safety for Second-Order Systems and Application to Autonomous Robot Navigation in Unknown Environments

The research introduces BubbleCBF, a constructive method for designing feasible safety constraints for second-order control-affine systems. This involves constructing collision-free spheres, or "bubbles," from a signed distance function to define a velocity-dependent safety constraint. A quadratic-program safety filter is used to enforce the resulting safe set, which is represented by the overlap of these bubbles. The method proves the resulting controller is well-defined, continuous, and forward invariant under mild assumptions. The approach is demonstrated to achieve safe autonomous navigation for a differential-drive robot in unknown environments.
