Adding One More Agent: How AI Team Size and Formation Shape Productivity and Diversity in Human-AI Brainstorming Teams

The study investigated how the number and formation of AI agents affect productivity and diversity in human-AI brainstorming teams. Experiments compared teams with one versus two AI partners against human-human dyads. With one AI partner, humans paired with an average-calibrated AI generated more ideas, while with two partners, this effect disappeared. AI presence increased the global diversity of human-authored ideas, but a second AI was necessary for increased within-session differentiation. Ultimately, the number and formation of AI partners significantly reshape human contributions to the brainstorming process.

Coherence by Construction: Synthesizing CTI Knowledge Graphs and the Formal Limits of Coherence Validation

The study proposes a generation by simulation paradigm for knowledge graphs, focusing on constructing coherence through a causal process rather than describing the artifact directly. Language model agents propose intents, and a deterministic referee validates outcomes against an explicit world model, ensuring relations are emitted only when represented events occur. The framework is applied to cyber threat intelligence knowledge graphs using methods like evolutionary genetic operators on graph genomes. The research proves that a validator cannot determine coherence simply by checking if a generated graph matches a random graph structure. Ultimately, the work cautions that structural validation alone is uninformative for coherence in construction-based knowledge synthesis.

TOUMAII: A 4.5M-Pair Tool-Calling Preference Dataset and Its Source-Agnostic Generator

The research introduces TOUMAII, a large-scale dataset of 4.5 million tool-calling preference pairs derived from Agent-Ark/Toucan-1.5M conversations. This dataset is designed to address the scarcity of preference data for tool-calling, which is crucial for training models with direct preference optimization (DPO). The authors also release TOUMAII-Gen, a generator pipeline to create synthetic preference data reflecting model failures, ensuring source-agnostic training. Performance validation showed the DPO approach achieved parity with other high-quality datasets when fine-tuning a 3-billion-parameter model. The work provides code and datasets, emphasizing the importance of preference data for autonomous agent capabilities.

ChatAtom: A Multi-Agent LLM System for Molecular Dynamics Simulation and Visualization

ChatAtom is a multi-agent Large Language Model system designed to automate molecular dynamics (MD) simulation workflows. It translates natural language requests into complete MD workflows, eliminating the need for users to write input scripts or analysis code. Agents like an orchestrator, LAMMPS, and OVITO, along with a HPC agent, manage the process using generator-reviewer pairs for script preparation. The system features a browser interface for streaming simulation data and supports loadable skills for autonomous hypothesis testing. This system aims to lower the barrier to conducting MD studies by integrating script generation, execution, visualization, and analysis into one observable pipeline.

Beyond Final Answers: Interpretable Tumor Immune Microenvironment Reasoning from H&E and mIF

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) struggle with reasoning about the tumor immune microenvironment (TIME) when relying solely on Hematoxylin and Eosin (H&E) stains. The study proposes HERMES, an R1-style MLLM that reasons jointly over paired H&E and multiplex immunofluorescence (mIF) data. HERMES is trained with a process reward that checks stated quantities against the ground truth provided by mIF, improving reasoning accuracy significantly. This method yields an 84.5% accuracy on colorectal cancer TIME tasks, outperforming other MLLMs. The integration of mIF provides the cell-level ground truth necessary to supervise the reasoning steps, leading to more verifiable and accurate conclusions.

Memorization and Generalization in Generative Models: A Perspective From Energy-based Modeling and Associative Memory

The research explores the coexistence of memorization and generalization in generative models by framing them through Associative Memory (AM) principles. It posits that memorization and generalization can stem from a single retrieval process based on basins of attraction. Diffusion models are shown to behave as AMs, exhibiting spurious states that indicate nascent generative capability. Uniform-state discrete diffusion models also function as AMs, where expanding basins for unseen examples demonstrate generalization. Ultimately, AM provides a framework for understanding when a generative model recalls versus when it creates.

Beyond Demographic Discrimination: Benchmarking Normative-Descriptive Calibration in LLMs

Large language models require benchmarking to assess whether they exhibit normative-descriptive calibration. This involves testing if a model treats matched candidates differently when asked to describe an outcome versus decide one. The study introduces the Normative-Descriptive Calibration Benchmark (NDCB) to measure this calibration across different LLMs. Results show that some models, like Claude Sonnet and Opus, strongly separate these framings, while others, like GPT-5.5, show negative calibration. Separating disparities and prescribing them are distinct behaviors that vary across different language models.

Towards Robust Partial-Edit Deepfake Localization: An edit and Generator Conditioned Analysis

The research analyzes partial-edit deepfake localization using an edit- and generator-conditioned analysis of Segment-Aware Localization (SAL). The study evaluates localization performance across different editing conditions, categorized by content, meaning change, and editing operation. Findings indicate that localization is weaker on the SSR-Speech subset, and deletions pose the most challenging editing operation for both representations. Single aggregate scores mask these specific weaknesses, suggesting separate evaluation across generators and edit types is necessary. Ultimately, the work emphasizes the need for finer-grained analysis of partial-edit localization performance.

Budget-Aware Predict-and-Verify Theorem Proving with Heterogeneous LLM Solvers

LLM theorem solvers vary in proving ability and inference cost, leading to inefficient computation when not managed properly. BudgetPV is an agentic system that allocates computation across subgoals and heterogeneous LLM solvers based on a cross-solver predictor. The method demonstrates significant computation savings when incorporating cheaper solvers.

AstronautBench: Measuring Agent Self-Improvement Across Episodes Through Knowledge, Evidence, and Progress

AstronautBench measures agent self-improvement across episodes by assessing knowledge, evidence, and progress in an embodied world. It uses the game Outer Wilds structure where knowledge persists across episode resets, focusing on what the agent knows, what evidence supports that knowledge, and how far it advances. The benchmark involves measuring reached locations, revealed facts, and the correctness of answers to world knowledge questions. Evaluations using different LLM judges show that these three measures do not align, providing a complete picture of self-improvement. For example, correctness increases, but the support from evidence does not always follow, revealing limitations in simple progress tracking.

Parasite Features: Causal Abstraction Through Spurious Pathways

Causal Abstraction provides a framework for mechanistic interpretability by defining equivalence between neural networks and algorithms. The paper introduces the concept of "parasite features," which are spurious features that pass Causal Abstraction equivalence tests. These features arise when interpretability methods spuriously simulate the combined behavior of underlying feature directions. Experimentally, Distributed Alignment Search was shown to find parasite features using linear alignment maps in syntactic agreement and two-digit addition tasks. This work raises important considerations for the application of Causal Abstraction in mechanistic interpretability.

Cross-Attack Robust Aggregation for Federated Learning

Federated learning models are vulnerable to poisoning attacks from compromised devices, which existing robust aggregation methods often fail to adequately address. FedTether is proposed as a method to enhance robustness by reweighting client updates based on their deviation from a coordinatewise median. This reweighting uses a robust, dead-zoned score applied during the server-side optimization step. Experiments show FedTether outperforms other methods by maintaining accuracy under various attack regimes, including stealthy and high-magnitude attacks. FedTether maintains strong performance even with partial participation without requiring shared data or validation sets.

Depth-Linked Residual Tokens for Multimodal Retrieval and Generation

Depth-Linked Residual Tokens (DepThink) is an efficient framework for multimodal retrieval and generation that addresses the latency of iterative architectures. It appends learnable residual tokens whose states are coupled via a lightweight, learnable residual recurrence within a single forward pass. This mechanism allows each token to capture complementary information missed by preceding tokens, proving expanded geometric directional reachability. Evaluation shows DepThink outperforms baselines and matches or exceeds looped architectures across various backbone sizes. Furthermore, it accelerates both training and inference compared to looped methods.

Don't Discard Your Rollouts: Reusing Teacher RL Traces for Student Distillation

Reusing teacher Reinforcement Learning (RL) rollouts can be a viable method for student distillation, addressing the computational expense of methods like PPO and GRPO. Naive approaches using rollouts perform worse than canonical distillation or are unstable for DPO methods. The authors propose treating rollout reuse as a data selection problem using metadata from GRPO to select student-compatible completions. This involves ordering data using prompt pass rates and using teacher log-likelihoods as a proxy for selection. The resulting two-stage recipe achieves performance comparable to canonical distillation across reasoning and coding tasks without extra inference costs.

LEAN: Library-Based Adaptation for Asynchronous, Federated Fine-Tuning

Federated fine-tuning with low-rank adapters often uses averaging, which is problematic in asynchronous settings with heterogeneous data. LEAN proposes library-based adaptation to replace averaging by maintaining a library of rank-1 basis pairs. Participants check out, locally fine-tune, and check back in these adapters, shuffling them across sites for exposure to diverse data. This method operates asynchronously and communicates only low-rank parameters, avoiding aggregation. LEAN significantly outperforms averaging baselines across benchmarks while handling data heterogeneity and asynchrony effectively.

Graph-XGB: A Hybrid GNN Framework for Outdoor-to-Indoor 5G Signal Reconstruction via Aerial-Only UAV RF Sensing

Graph-XGB is a hybrid Graph Neural Network (GNN) framework designed to reconstruct indoor 5G signals from aerial-only Radio Frequency (RF) sensing data. It employs a modified SpiderGNN encoder on a graph incorporating a Floor-Aware 3D Distance metric to process UAV boundary measurements. This process maps the measurements into a 10-dimensional manifold using PCA, which is then fused with spatial features for prediction. Independent, physics-tailored XGBoost regressors are used to decode these embeddings, effectively decoupling path-loss and interference-dependent modeling. Evaluation on a real-world dataset showed Graph-XGB achieved the highest accuracy for RSSI and RSRP reconstruction compared to other architectures.

SDG: State-Dependent Gating at Chunk Boundaries for Linear Attention

State-Dependent Gating (SDG) introduces element-wise gates based on the change in memory across chunk boundaries to provide state feedback. This method enables efficient chunkwise computation while maintaining parallel processing within each chunk. The approach addresses limitations in existing gated linear attention models by capturing state changes explicitly at chunk boundaries. Theoretical analysis confirms potential reconstruction benefits from this state-dependent gating under a signal-noise model. Empirical evaluations on large models show improvements across various language and reasoning tasks.

Spectral Estimation with Deformed Decompression

Sample covariance matrices contain valuable information encoded in their eigenvalues, but large-scale matrices pose computational challenges. Spectral decompression methods, such as spectral inversion and Free Decompression, exist but have limitations. Deformed Decompression (DD) is introduced as a novel framework for stably extrapolating spectral densities. DD uses properties of the companion matrix to frame the problem as a directed evolution of the Stieltjes transform. This approach offers a robust method for scaling covariance matrices, including those without unitary invariance, and successfully captures complex spectral signatures in experiments.

Semiparametric Efficiency for Entropic Optimal Transport under Structured Distribution Shift

The study investigates the semiparametric efficiency of entropic optimal transport (EOT) when structured distribution shifts occur. It examines whether structural information can improve the estimation of EOT and quantifies the variance gain from restrictions under specific conditions. The work involves a fixed-dimensional exponential density ratio model with a nonparametric baseline and a fixed positive regularization. The analysis shows that the variance gain can be zero and depends on the behavior of the tilt space as samples grow. Finally, the study explores phase transitions in the limiting distribution based on the balance of source fluctuations and quadratic tilt errors.

A framework called SALVES is introduced for budgeted subset selection by decomposing the problem into value estimation and search. Communication-Efficient Federated Learning of Latent Patient Representations from Multi-Institutional EHRs

The method proposes a communication-efficient, non-iterative federated spectral approach for learning shared latent representations from multi-institutional Electronic Health Record (EHR) data. Institutions share aggregate marginal frequencies and a low-rank spectral summary instead of patient-level records to preserve privacy and reduce communication costs. A central server aggregates these summaries to recover the shared low-rank structure and the direction for interpretable representation recovery. Simulations demonstrate that the method accurately estimates latent components and outperforms existing benchmarks when applied to stroke patient data. This approach aims to uncover clinically meaningful, subgroup-specific patient representations across different healthcare facilities.

Optimal Linear Regression Without a Variance

The study examines high-probability linear regression in a regime where regression noise has a finite conditional $\alpha$-moment $\nu$. A black-box reduction is provided to convert finite-variance regression procedures into weak-moment procedures via empirical residual pruning. Under a finite fourth-moment condition on covariates, a finite-variance subroutine achieving the sub-Gaussian rate yields a specific error bound dependent on the sample size. This result includes a matching minimax lower bound, and the reduction is computationally efficient. Furthermore, the work provides distribution-free prediction guarantees and an analogous $\alpha$-agnostic reduction for mean estimation.

Language Diffusion Models are Associative Memories Capable of Retrieving Unseen Data

Language diffusion models, specifically Uniform-based Discrete Diffusion Models (UDDMs), exhibit behavior consistent with Associative Memories (AMs) possessing emergent creative capabilities. This is explored by examining how these models memorize training data and generalize to unseen data. A transition from memorization to generalization can be quantitatively detected by analyzing the conditional entropy of predicted token sequences. Memorization is indicated by vanishing conditional entropy, while generalization involves a finite conditional entropy across most tokens. Therefore, conditional entropy provides a method to probe the memorization-to-generalization boundary in deployed models.

On the loss landscape of the linear neural network: beyond critical points

The paper addresses the lack of a global description for the loss landscape of linear neural networks beyond their critical points. It introduces a new structure called the critical thalweg, which encompasses all critical points and possesses specific geometric properties. This framework provides a global view of the loss landscape that is easily visualized in two dimensions. The work offers a geometric proof of existing results using simpler assumptions and suggests that gradient descent is attracted towards this thalweg. Empirical evidence indicates that gradient descent follows this path to reach the global minimum.

Learning under Bounded Manipulation for Quadratic Incentive Design

The research studies a repeated quadratic incentive design game where a leader learns a follower's private type from noisy actions. The leader uses these estimates in an affine incentive rule, while the follower can manipulate observations to influence future estimates. A manipulation budget $C_T$, constraining the expected cumulative squared deviation from the myopic best response, is introduced. The paper establishes a minimax regret rate of order $\max\{\log T, \min\{C_T,T\}\}$ for approximately rational followers. Furthermore, increasing the follower's manipulation budget can unexpectedly reduce the leader's regret under a fixed learning rule.
