AI-assisted learning platform helps robots reason beyond literal commands

Researchers developed FLEX, an AI-assisted learning platform to enable robots to reason beyond literal commands from natural language. FLEX uses a large language model (LLM) to draw larger generalizations from human instructions, improving motion-planning. The process involves three steps: Add, Generalize using the LLM, and Mask to update the robot's understanding of the environment. In simulations, the robot successfully inferred higher-level intentions, such as avoiding electronics when told not to spill coffee. This method allows robots to adapt to unspoken preferences, making them more intuitive for human interaction.

Precision Spatial Tracking for Autonomous Systems: Training Humanoid Robots and Drones with Optical Motion Capture

Optical motion capture provides robots with an independent reference for training and hardware validation, addressing the measurement problem in imitation tasks. It allows researchers to distinguish between a policy's quality and the system's physical execution by establishing an external truth. Different capture methods, such as optical, inertial, and markerless, offer varying trade-offs in accuracy, latency, and handling of occlusions. Valid evaluation requires comparing the reference trajectory with the robot's estimated state to measure divergences like foot-placement errors or trajectory deviations. Ultimately, precision tracking is valuable for separating demonstration collection from simulation-to-real testing and for verifying autonomous navigation.

Q-OIL: Optimistic Exploration in Learning from Human Interventions

Human interventions offer valuable feedback for improving robot behavior, but current methods rely on inaccurate human models. The research found that humans intervene when agent progress stalls, and their corrections are often suboptimal. Q-OIL introduces a progress-based model of human intervention to create realistic benchmarking in simulation. This method uses optimistic bonuses to guide exploration toward human corrections, learning more efficiently than competitive baselines. Q-OIL achieved 10-50% higher success rates in simulation and on real robots for bimanual manipulation tasks.

Meituan-Robotics-0: A Vision-Language-Action Foundation Model for Desktop Manipulation

Meituan-Robotics-0 is a vision-language-action foundation model designed for stationary-base desktop manipulation. It integrates 147 million vision-language samples and 15,060 hours of robot trajectories across twelve embodiments. The model uses a three-stage training recipe involving action-token pre-training and flow-matching to align multimodal representations with robot behavior. Rollback is introduced to provide supervision for policy training without value learning. The system includes features for heterogeneous end-effector interfaces and event-driven task management for improved execution.

arXiv - cs.RO

CS-Pi: Constraint-Satisfying Flow Policy Sampling

Flow-matching policy sampling aims to enforce constraints on deployed policies at inference time without discarding learned behaviors. The method combines rejection, which ensures plausibility, and projection, which ensures efficiency. CS-Pi introduces a "draw-project-renoise" sampler that projects draws to the nearest safe action, adds scaled noise, and re-runs the policy for safety. This approach successfully balances the efficiency of projection with the plausibility of rejection. Experiments show CS-Pi achieves high safe success rates across various robotic tasks.

arXiv - cs.RO

ForPC: Force-Aware Whole-Body Control for Precise Humanoid Cart Pushing

ForPC is a force-aware whole-body control framework designed to achieve precise humanoid cart pushing. It uses two-stage reinforcement learning to regulate cart velocity by inferring force and state latents from onboard observations without external sensors. The policy coordinates arm, waist, and leg adjustments with base-velocity commands, penalized for internal opposition and grasp slip. Simulation results show ForPC reduces velocity and cross-track RMSE significantly compared to locomotion-only baselines. The trained policy successfully transferred to a physical robot to push a cart with a 30 kg payload.

National and State-Level Approaches to Automated Vehicle Certification: A Comparative Framework for State Operational Governance

Variation in state regulation of automated vehicle deployment in the U.S. stems from the lack of a unified federal certification framework. The paper compares how U.S. states regulate SAE Level 4-5 automated driving systems against international practices. Three state certification models were identified: baseline compliance, administrative self-certification, and permit-based authorization. International trends favor scenario-based testing, safety-case documentation, and lifecycle oversight. The research develops a framework to compare state approaches based on regulatory involvement and governance complexity.

arXiv - cs.RO

VICTR: Value-Informed In-Context Learning for Vision-Language-Action Models

VICTR is a framework designed for in-context learning in Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models through progress-informed context retrieval. It introduces an In-Context Value Function Estimator (IC-VFE) to estimate task-relative progress based on a reference demonstration, language, and current observation. This progress estimation guides the selection of visually relevant demonstration chunks for conditioning the action generation policy. Applying VICTR significantly improves performance on unseen out-of-domain real-world manipulation tasks. Specifically, VICTR increased the success rate from 6.2% to 23.8% and more than doubled the task progress rate from 28.9% to 55.5%.

It Takes Two to Tangram: Multi-Agent Object Centric Reinforcement Learning for Assembly

A multi-agent object-centric reinforcement learning framework is presented for geometric assembly tasks. Each part is treated as an agent learning a shared policy for continuous assembly control. A graph neural network models the pieces and their relationships to predict translational and angular velocities. Proximal policy optimization with a centralized graph critic learns motion and temporal coordination across the agents. The method demonstrates concurrent assembly in simulation and successful transfer to real robot execution.

arXiv - cs.RO

STRIDE: Learning Humanoid Locomotion in Minutes with Acceleration-Based Rewards

STRIDE learns humanoid locomotion by using acceleration-based rewards to train robots with only onboard Inertial Measurement Unit (IMU) data. Instead of tracking base velocity, the method rewards the robot for following a commanded acceleration profile. The approach utilizes the off-policy RL algorithm XQC combined with high gain joint position controllers and left-right symmetry augmentation. In simulation, STRIDE successfully taught robots to walk on Unitree G1 and Booster T1 humanoids within fourteen minutes. This method significantly reduces training falls by 81% compared to other approaches.

arXiv - cs.RO

What Limits mmWave Human-State Sensing for Robot Perception?

Millimeter-wave radar offers an alternative for human state sensing in challenging environments, as it operates without ambient light and penetrates smoke. Limitations arise from compact arrays, which result in joint-localization errors of about decimeters at several meters. A new framework models the human and environment as a time-varying scattering field to establish a Cramer-Rao error floor for localization. Experiments show that techniques like frame fusion and multiple viewpoints can reduce localization errors by moving them in predictable directions. This provides a basis for robotics to decide between observation design and estimator capacity for reliable mmWave sensing.
