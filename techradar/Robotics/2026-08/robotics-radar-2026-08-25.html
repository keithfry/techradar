AdaPT Bridges Sim-to-Real to Teach Humanoids the Tennis Styles of Federer, Nadal, and Djokovic

AdaPT is a framework that teaches humanoid robots the tennis playing styles of Federer, Nadal, and Djokovic by extracting kinematics directly from broadcast video. It uses a decoupled planning-and-tracking hierarchy enhanced with explicit speed adaptation to compensate for sim-to-real gaps and real-world latency. The system mined 3D body meshes from video to create training data, ensuring the replication of nuanced biomechanics like topspin follow-through. Testing on robots like the Unitree G1 and Dobot Atom showed significant improvements in hit rates and serve success compared to non-adaptive methods. AdaPT demonstrates a method for transferring complex athletic skills from visual data to physical humanoids in real-world settings.

How to Select Roller Brushes for Robotic Solar Panel Cleaning Systems

Selecting roller brushes for solar panel cleaning must balance cleaning action with controlled contact, serviceability, and module limitations. The choice depends on the specific site contamination, cleaning method, and robot interface, not just brush softness. Loose dust is suitable for soft brushing, but hard deposits like bird droppings require more nuanced approaches. Module cleaning guidance and surface protection depend on the entire system, including brush condition and operating conditions. A good roller controls routine soiling while acknowledging when alternative cleaning methods are necessary.

RoboShape: Information-Theoretic Point Cloud Representations for Privacy-Aware Robot Perception

RoboShape introduces information-theoretic compression for point cloud representations to enable privacy-aware robot perception. It uses the Donsker-Varadhan formulation of mutual information to maximize the information between embeddings and object understanding while minimizing the leakage of private attributes. This method results in embeddings that are 87.5% smaller while retaining 98.7% of object classification utility. RoboShape successfully reduces sensitive attribute predictions by 39.3% across indoor LiDAR datasets. The resulting privacy-preserving embeddings are efficient for network transmission and downstream model training.

On the Optimized Use of Non-Orthonormality Constraints for the Quasi-Static INS Alignment of Autonomous Underwater and Surface Vehicles

Inertial navigation systems require precise initial alignment for autonomous vehicles, often achieved through Coarse Alignment and Fine Alignment processes. Conventional methods for this alignment suffer from slow convergence and limited bias estimability. This paper introduces an optimized Coarse Alignment method, TRIAD-CBE, and a novel Fine Alignment EKF observation model incorporating Non-Orthonormality constraints. These constraints are derived from TRIAD and directly relate errors to inertial sensor biases. Simulations and experiments show these approaches significantly accelerate alignment and bias estimation while maintaining accuracy.

ODG-NoMaD: Overhead-Camera Direction-Guided NoMaD

ODG-NoMaD enhances NoMaD by providing a global sense of direction for exploration in unseen environments. It utilizes an overhead depth camera to create an occupancy map and plan a global path, which is then refined by per-frame traversability maps. A cosine direction cost gradient is injected into denoising steps to rotate trajectories towards this global direction. In simulations, ODG-NoMaD significantly reduces the distance to the target compared to unguided exploration and achieves collision-free paths in all trials.

Active Interaction-Aware Model Predictive Path Integral via Ego-Conditioned Generative Predictions

Dense traffic necessitates an active interaction-aware planning framework due to continuous influence among agents. The proposed method integrates an ego-conditioned generative autoregressive prediction model into Model Predictive Path Integral (MPPI) control. This model generates stochastic, multi-modal predictions of surrounding agents based on ego actions. A nested sampling scheme is used to evaluate expected cost and collision risk under these induced distributions. Closed-loop simulations show improved safety and efficiency compared to traditional planning methods.

Selective Cross-View Consistency for World Action Models: Held-Out Viewpoint Robustness Without Test-Time Camera Information

World action models (WAMs) jointly denoise future video frames and robot actions, where camera viewpoint change is a major challenge. The study investigates where to apply consistency loss when training WAMs with same-state cross-view image pairs. It demonstrates that imposing consistency on view-covariant coordinates is harmful, shrinking legitimate view-specific content. Selective cross-view consistency (SCVC) constrains only the view-invariant block, eliminating the need for test-time camera information. This method improves closed-loop success in held-out viewpoints without requiring any camera labels or synthesis.

Tolerance-Dependent Inspection Disagreement Between a Fixed CMM and a Portable Articulated-Arm CMM

Fixed and portable articulated-arm Coordinate Measuring Machines (CMMs) often differ in their accuracy specifications for the same inspection task. The study investigated whether the difference in measurement results crosses the specified tolerance boundaries, rather than just the magnitude of the difference. Dimensional disagreements were found, with the fixed-CMM being approximately $11.2 \ \mu\text{m}$ higher than the articulated-arm CMM. Form profile differences also showed disagreement relative to the upper limit. The research proposes a tolerance map to identify feature-tolerance combinations where instrument choice can alter the inspection label, necessitating controlled equivalence studies.
