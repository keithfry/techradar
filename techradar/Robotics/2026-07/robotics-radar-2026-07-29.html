Kall Morris tests orbital ‘tow truck’ for space debris removal aboard International Space Station

A US startup, Kall Morris Inc, has successfully tested its Responsive Engaging Arms for Captive Care and Handling (REACCH) system aboard the International Space Station. The technology is designed to capture, relocate and remove space debris using a robotic system with tentacle-like arms and gecko-inspired adhesive pads. The ISS National Laboratory provided opportunities for over 170 capture trials, allowing engineers to refine the technology. REACCH could be used to steer satellites away from collisions, extend satellite life through servicing missions, and guide obsolete spacecraft towards controlled deorbiting.

FCC updates covered list to include foreign-produced advanced robotic devices and power inverters

The US Federal Communications Commission has updated its "Covered List" to include foreign-produced advanced robotic devices and power inverters, following determinations by a White House-convened interagency body that these products pose unacceptable risks to national security. The new additions are due to concerns about supply chain vulnerabilities and cybersecurity risks, which could be exploited by foreign governments or malign actors. Producers of affected devices can apply for Conditional Approval from the Departments of War and Homeland Security to receive FCC equipment authorizations, while existing models are unaffected.

Robot Finger Feels in Color

A team of scientists in Europe has developed a robotic fingertip with synthetic skin that reflects different colors of light in response to mechanical deformation, allowing it to generate high-resolution maps of topology, strain, and contact pressure. The skin uses a Bragg reflector, which is deformed by an object to change the reflected wavelengths. This technology provides 100-micrometer resolution with no computational latency, making it a promising option for roboticists to experiment with. The sensor embeds tactile sensing directly into the material of the finger, translating the deformation into digital signals without using traditional taxels or pixels.

Egocentric Station Holding of Robotic Fish in Unknown Turbulent Background Flow

Researchers developed the SWiFT framework, a Swimming With Flow Toolbox, to enable efficient exploration of egocentric station-holding policies for robotic fish in unknown turbulent background flows via reinforcement learning. The policy achieves substantial improvements over state-of-the-art methods, particularly in root-mean-square error (RMSE) of distance. Egocentric feedback alone enables station-holding without explicit flow sensing, mirroring the biological phenomenon of rheotaxis.

Steeringless Drifting: Differential-Torque Control of a Four-Wheel Independently Driven Vehicle

A double-track vehicle model incorporating four-wheel differential actuation is established, which enables a drift-equilibrium calculation method and a closed-loop drift controller to be developed. The proposed approach achieves steady circular drifting with a sideslip angle of approximately 20° and performs figure-eight drift tracking through differential wheel torques in a steering-free four-wheel independently driven vehicle architecture. This study demonstrates the feasibility of drift control using only differential wheel torques for near-limit control in such vehicles, providing a new perspective on autonomous driving near handling limits. The approach is validated through simulations and experiments on a 1:10-scale vehicle.

FIRMGrasp, a family of friction-volatility-aware grasp quality metrics, predicts failures in force closure across the range of friction values. Unlike classical metrics, it evaluates the force-closure margin at the Conditional Value-at-Risk-discounted mean of the adverse friction tail. FIRMGrasp establishes its monotonicity and differentiability in grasp parameters and provides a probabilistic closure certificate guaranteeing force closure with probability at least β.

Reactive 3D Motion Planning for a Franka Arm via Star-World Workspace Reshaping

Researchers at Saayuj Deshpande, Gia Dcosta and Samhitha Vedire investigate Star-World workspace reshaping for three-dimensional reactive control of a Franka Emika Panda manipulator. At each update, intersecting inflated obstacles are clustered and replaced by star-shaped proxies before a dynamical-system-based end-effector controller is evaluated. The method resolves the canonical overlapping-wall case and requires 0.68--8.70\,ms per workspace update for scenes containing one to seven obstacles, but increases path length in some cases and produces near-equilibria.

arXiv - cs.RO

Leveraging Semantic Maps for City-Scale Cross-View Localization

A method leverages semantic maps from OpenStreetMap to enable robots to localize in previously untraversed environments against prior data. The approach involves extracting relevant landmarks from panoramas and identifying feasible correspondences between these landmarks and prior overhead landmarks using Variational Likelihood Machines (VLMs). A lightweight matcher is then distilled from the VLM to compute correspondences for all entities in a map, which is fused with a Bayes filter to create a time series of pose estimates. The method generalizes across location, lighting, weather, and other challenges, and code and datasets are available.
