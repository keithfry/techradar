Stop Calling the First Significant Day a Win

Checking an A/B test until it crosses p < 0.05 can result in nearly 28 percent false-positive rate, compared to about 5 percent if checked only once at the end. The problem is that every new look gives the same random process another chance to wander across the line. If a team stops early on day four, for example, the pressure to stop becomes real, and the result can be overstated by up to a quarter. Using an always-valid p-value or group-sequential testing with calibrated boundaries can help mitigate this issue.
