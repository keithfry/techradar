Context Scaling: A New Frontier of AI Scaling

Failures in deployed AI often stem from a mismatch between the task state and available context, which the paper terms context scaling. The effort to reduce this bottleneck is called context scaling, which is framed by three budgets: training ($B_\theta$), decision-time ($B_d$), and context-infrastructure ($B_c$). Context scaling specifically focuses on improving the system's capacity to acquire, represent, deliver, and persist deployment state for model use. Effective-context value is defined based on task- and intervention-dependent attribution, separating deployment state from model-visible context. The research proposes an agenda focusing on grounded reporting, infrastructure, and safety for persistent AI systems.

Constraint-Aware Test-Time Scoring for LLM and VLM Candidate Selection

A scoring framework is proposed to select the best candidate answers from LLMs and VLMs by combining hard and soft checks. This framework uses a drafting model, a configuring model, and scoring models to evaluate candidate properties. Application to tasks like code generation and QA showed that combining hard checks with candidate comparison significantly improves correctness and safety. The gain is largest when the initial sample is weak, with empirical improvements up to +0.28 for code safety. The method emphasizes grounded hard checks over soft-factor configuration for achieving performance gains.

AutoformBench: A Lean Formalization Benchmark for Advanced Mathematics

AutoformBench is a benchmark for evaluating AI systems on the task of formalizing advanced mathematics in Lean. It comprises 341 formalization tasks drawn from 24 advanced mathematical sources. In these tasks, agents are given informal proofs and must construct the necessary definitions and supporting infrastructure in Lean. The benchmark assesses frontier models' ability to build this missing formal infrastructure. Performance results indicate that success depends more on constructing formal infrastructure than on specific mathematical domain knowledge.

General-Purpose Decision Models for AI Configuration Tuning

AI configuration tuning seeks optimal settings for AI performance within budget constraints, where the choice of decision strategy is critical. The research investigates whether general-purpose decision models can effectively guide this tuning process using a representative agent called Jev. Comparisons showed that conventional optimizers and predictors performed well, but region guidance led to configurations with lower perplexity. This approach provided quality gains when additional tuning time and fees were permitted. The findings suggest actionable advice for selecting decision roles and balancing tuning quality against cost.

What, Who, and When: Structured Harm Assessment for Mental Health Safety via Short Reasoning

The research introduces a clinically grounded taxonomy for assessing harm in mental health contexts based on what, who, and when harm occurs. This framework separates harm characterization from intervention decisions, allowing for explicit, auditable safety policies. A reasoning-based guard model named MindGuard2 is trained using short reasoning traces and reinforcement learning to minimize inference overhead. MindGuard2 achieves competitive accuracy while significantly reducing latency compared to larger models. The work results in a safety guardrail model with state-of-the-art performance for mental health safety.

Certify What You Plant: Shortcut-Free Proof Depth in Logical Reasoning Data

Procedurally generated logic problems use planted proofs to evaluate and train reasoning in language models. The research measures the actual proof depth required for problems relative to stated depths and inference rules. Certificates are exported to verify the required steps, revealing what a problem truly necessitates. This method constructs problems where every proof has a requested structure, incorporating shortcuts. Training with these certified and mixed problem types improves model performance for Qwen3.

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis in Jacobian-Space: Language and Vision Models Agree Approximately Up to a Rotation

The Platonic Representation Hypothesis suggests AI models converge to a shared world representation across different modalities. This research investigates whether vision and language models also converge in how they operate on these representations, specifically focusing on the Jacobian-space (J-space). Evidence shows that the J-spaces of vision and language models converge approximately up to a rotation. Rotating a word's vector between modalities demonstrably changes the perceived object in vision or the output of image generation. This implies that models converge not only in representation but also in the actions taken upon that representation.

Brand Recall and Category Accuracy in Language Models: An Audit of 1,000 B2B SaaS Brands

A study audited 1,000 B2B SaaS brands across 333 product categories regarding brand recall and category accuracy in language models. Category prompts elicited 510 brands, and a five-candidate probe ranked the reference label first for 787 brands. The overall error rate across separated rankings was 19.8%, which dropped to 3.5% for the best-performing brands. Measured brand recall varied across four Gemma generations, ranging from 39.8% to 51.1%. The findings suggest reporting brand recall and category accuracy separately is advisable.

Confidence scores for gated automation of large language models

Large language model agents require reliable confidence scores to gate automated decisions, as LLMs can produce incorrect answers with high confidence. This work trains models using supervised learning to predict the correctness of LLM-generated answers. A linear confidence score head fitted to the frozen LLM backbone is used to generate these scores. The proposed confidence scoring framework outperforms baselines for automating LLM-based agents with high accuracy. This method enhances the safety of automation by allowing uncertain outputs to be routed for human review.

Partial Routing: Trace-Tuned Delegation for Resource-Efficient Agentic Workflows

Partial routing guides a lead model to delegate subtasks to cheaper worker models in agentic workflows. This is achieved by analyzing past runs to identify recurring subtasks and measure their quality and cost for each model. Delegation guidance is produced with a parameter $\delta$ controlling the delegation amount, allowing the lead to decide delegation dynamically. On benchmark tasks, partial routing achieved higher performance than using the lead alone or task-level routing. This demonstrates that analyzing past runs supports more efficient planning and delegation with minimal overhead.
