Censored vs. Truncated Regression in Python, Explained

Censoring, where values are unknown above a threshold, and truncation, where observations above a threshold are excluded, represent distinct statistical issues in regression analysis. Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression can yield biased estimates when applied to such data, as demonstrated by comparing results from censored and truncated scenarios. The Tobit model and truncated regression methods provide alternative frameworks to appropriately account for this missing or limited information. Machine learning practitioners should consider methods like censored-aware loss functions to incorporate this probabilistic reasoning into more flexible models. Understanding how data is collected is crucial for correctly interpreting the results of any statistical model.
