Mixture of Experts (MoE): History, Architecture & Modern LLMs

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) originated from the 1991 Jacobs & Hinton paper, which proposed using a gating network to select an expert for each input case, fostering specialization. The key innovation in modern MoE is the Sparsely-Gated MoE layer, which achieves massive model capacity while maintaining computational efficiency through conditional computation. This approach allows models to scale to trillions of parameters by distributing tasks across multiple specialized experts. MoE has been integrated into Transformer architectures, leading to highly efficient Large Language Models like Mistral and Grok-1. This architecture is now driving the development of various state-of-the-art LLMs.

NVFP4 enables 4-bit matrix multiplication on NVIDIA RTX Blackwell GPUs, offering higher throughput for attention workloads than BF16.
