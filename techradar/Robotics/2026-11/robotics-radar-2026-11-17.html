The Sequence Knowledge - Issue #973: Learning RSI: World Models as Training Grounds for Self Improving Agents

World models are presented as training grounds for self-improving agents by generating the consequences of actions. A learned simulator can create experience on demand, which, when combined with task proposal and evaluation systems, forms an improvement machinery. DeepMind's SIMA 2 and Genie 3 offer early examples of this setup. The discussion questions whether this generated experience teaches transferable capabilities. The analysis also touches upon the economic cost of experience generation and the recursive nature of improvement.

Robot swarms trained by trial and error in simulation keep working on real robots

Reinforcement learning trained robot swarms in simulation reliably perform on physical robots despite the "reality gap." Researchers overcame this by restricting the learning algorithm to choose from a set of tried-and-tested behaviors rather than directly controlling every motor. This method ensures that the learned skills are grounded in real-world constraints rather than simulator quirks. Experiments with 20 e-puck robots showed this approach significantly outperformed other methods. This work bridges the gap between reinforcement learning and reliable swarm robotics for complex real-world tasks.

Opinion: Moravec’s paradox explains why physical AI needs a sense of touch

Moravec's paradox highlights that while intellectual tasks are easy for machines, physical interactions are profoundly difficult. This disconnect arises because human sensory processing evolved to hide complex physical computations below conscious awareness. The point of contact presents a major hurdle for physical AI, as visual systems lack the necessary data to handle real-world resistance. Integrating force and torque sensing allows robots to 'feel' the physical world instead of relying solely on visual predictions. Therefore, grounding physical AI requires integrating precise, real-time sensory feedback into the core hardware architecture.

Welcome, AstraBrain!

AstraBrain is an embodied AI software system from Beijing Galbot AI Co., Ltd., powering their humanoid robots rather than being a physical robot itself. Its architecture features a planning component (brain), a body control component (cerebellum), and a connecting bridge module. The system's controller is named AstraBrain WBC, highlighting the separation between the planning and control layers. It achieved a Humanoid.Guide Brain Score of 8 out of 10 and was presented at the World Robot Conference in August 2026. AstraBrain functions as a shared software layer across Galbot's broader robotics lineup.
