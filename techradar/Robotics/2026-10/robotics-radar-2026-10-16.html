The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI is researching whether robotic intelligence can be transferred across different hardware rather than being rebuilt for each machine. Their research focuses on motor adaptation, video-prompted task learning, and self-play to achieve this transferability. The core idea is to separate intelligence from the physical body, suggesting that understanding a body should be learned by the model. The company's goal is to determine if these capabilities can survive the real-world constraints of speed, reliability, and safety in deployment. This effort explores the idea of inhabiting unfamiliar machines to test the limits of flexible intelligence.

arXiv - cs.RO

Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle

Reliable heading estimation for road vehicles is achieved by a skylight polarization navigation method optimized using a Rayleigh sky model. This method estimates vehicle heading by globally optimizing the model against measurements from a polarimetric camera. The framework synthesizes the expected polarization image using a pinhole model, a single-scattering Rayleigh model, and a solar position algorithm. A polarization-weighted root mean square error objective function is used to compare measured and simulated polarization patterns, effectively handling occlusions. Experimental results demonstrated sub-degree RMS yaw errors and up to an 87% reduction in heading error compared to other methods.

arXiv - cs.RO

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

The work provides closed-form bounds on the feasibility of drone-mesh interception before learning a policy is selected, considering sizing and timing. It analyzes the uncertainty in predicted points from noisy linear tracks, agreeing with simulations within 1.8%. Coverage probability for a packed mesh is calculated with high accuracy, revealing a limitation in simple area ratios. The optimal packing arrangement is hexagonal, and centroidal-Voronoi control offers less coverage than this optimal arrangement. The paper argues that these feasibility ceilings should be established before any learning experiments are performed.

arXiv - cs.RO

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery (PGRR) is introduced to improve final gait selection following irreversible leg damage. This method predicts multiple outcomes across repeated gait executions and uses uncertainty-aware estimates to guide online probes without resetting the robot. Across 108 MuJoCo tasks involving leg amputation and fracture, PGRR improved final quality by 0.106 and slot-averaged recovery quality by 0.044 compared to a Gaussian-process baseline. PGRR also reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points in all damage classes. Conservative uncertainty handling was identified as the key factor contributing to these performance gains.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

A methodology is presented for adaptive mission planning for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels. This strategy maximizes information gathering by focusing on locations of maximum interest identified through in-situ measurements. It employs a clarity-driven ergodic search, linking clarity to differential entropy to prioritize mission areas. The system adaptively updates a target clarity map based on new data and re-plans routes considering the vessel's mobility limitations. Simulations and experiments on a solar-powered ASV confirmed the effectiveness of this approach.

arXiv - cs.RO

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing

Autonomous vehicle safety systems often fail to recover from loss-of-control states like hydroplaning because they focus on preventing instability rather than recovery. The research trains a racing policy using reinforcement learning to learn drifting as a time-optimal maneuver, which is then adapted for recovery training. This method defines recovery relative to a stable equilibrium and uses Gym-Khana for controller evaluation. The transferred policy significantly improved recovery over existing methods and outperformed specialized controllers in recovering from difficult states. Testing on a physical vehicle demonstrated the policy's effectiveness, including recovering 50% of the time from a state that other baselines could never recover from.

arXiv - cs.RO

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Sustainably powered mobile robots must optimize speed trajectories to trade energy for maximizing information quality over time during long-duration missions. The study frames this as a periodic optimal control problem involving super-linear propulsion costs and dynamic information gain and decay. The research uses clarity as an information metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control and characterize follow-on properties. Key findings include a high-speed information limit approached as energy reserves become infinite. The results are illustrated with examples and a simulation based on salinity characterization in the Chesapeake Bay.

arXiv - cs.RO

BubbleCBF: Constructive Safety for Second-Order Systems and Application to Autonomous Robot Navigation in Unknown Environments

BubbleCBF develops a constructive method for designing feasible safety constraints for systems with second-order dynamics. This involves constructing collision-free spheres, or "bubbles," from a signed distance function to define velocity-dependent safety constraints. A quadratic-program safety filter is used to enforce the safe set, and the method proves the resulting controller is well-defined and forward invariant under mild assumptions. The approach is demonstrated on a differential-drive robot for safe autonomous navigation in unknown environments. The work shows that this BubbleCBF successfully enables safe navigation for the robot.
