Ep#117: Residual Modeling Closes the Regression and Generative Policy Gap in Robot Learning

Generative policies like diffusion and flow-matching often outperform direct action regression (MSE-Policies) in robot learning from demonstrations. This performance gap is attributed not primarily to multimodality, but to the mismatch in loss functions stemming from state-dependent variations in action residuals and heavy-tailed data. The authors propose HT-Policies, which learn input-dependent residual scales and incorporate a Student-t residual model to mitigate the influence of these heavy tails. HT-Policies achieve performance competitive with generative baselines while offering faster training and inference. This research suggests that accounting for residual distribution and scale is crucial for effective policy optimization in robot learning.

arXiv - cs.RO

Passage-Focused Soft Coordination for Hierarchical Multi-Robot Navigation in Bottleneck Environments

A passage-focused framework is proposed for hierarchical multi-robot navigation in bottleneck environments by combining soft global coordination with flexible local execution. It utilizes a passage-induced graph of convex sets for priority-ordered planning and passage-aware route selection. Route-restricted continuous space-time planning is used to penalize temporal conflicts, guiding a model predictive path integral controller. The system incorporates adaptive tracking and selective replanning to handle execution deviations while maintaining coordination. Experimental results show this framework achieves high success rates and outperforms baselines in makespan and flowtime.

arXiv - cs.RO

Correcting Persistent Failures in Point-Cloud Grasping Policies using Human Demonstrations in Virtual Reality

Reinforcement learning for point-cloud grasping policies often leaves specific grasp failures unsolved despite optimizing for plausibility. The authors propose a human-in-the-loop failure loop to correct these persistent failures. When an RL policy fails, the specific situation is sent to a virtual reality application for a person to demonstrate the correct grasp. This human demonstration is then used to retrain the policy, effectively adding two grasp points. This method significantly boosts the success rate on hard cases, nearly doubling performance from 24% to 45% for those failed grasps.

arXiv - cs.RO

Optimal Operating-Point Selection for Single-Linearization Trajectory Planning of Nonlinear Robot Manipulators

The paper proposes an offline, performance-driven method for selecting an optimal single linearization point for trajectory planning of nonlinear robot manipulators. Candidate operating points are sampled from a cubic seed trajectory, and each yields an affine model used in a constrained quadratic program. The selected operating point is determined by minimizing the nonlinear trajectory objective after executing the resulting control sequences on the nonlinear dynamics. Evaluation on a three-DOF UR5 manipulator showed that this selection method reduced the nonlinear trajectory objective by significant amounts compared to conventional linearization methods. This systematic selection improves trajectory planning while maintaining a fixed affine planning model.

arXiv - cs.RO

TMA: Sim-to-Real Forceful Humanoid Manipulation via Tactile-Driven Motor Adaptation

Tactile-Driven Motor Adaptation (TMA) is a method for achieving zero-shot sim-to-real forceful humanoid manipulation by incorporating tactile feedback. It structures policy learning based on interactions observable through tactile feedback and uses cone geometry as a bias for multi-finger contact representation. The approach adapts motor behavior online from tactile history to handle real-world uncertainties in contact dynamics. TMA successfully demonstrated this by outperforming other baselines on real-world round doorknob opening tasks. The system coordinates upper-body manipulation with a pretrained locomotion controller for whole-body stability.

arXiv - cs.RO

Refinement vs. Replanning: Inference Compute Allocation in Robot Policies

A frozen robot policy can allocate deployment-time inference compute either to refine a predicted action chunk or to initiate more frequent policy replans. The study evaluates this allocation strategy without updating model weights, using a SmolVLA checkpoint in LIBERO simulation. The evaluation considers both nominal compute rate and complete attempt cost across discovery and held-out tasks. Results show that the optimal allocation depends on evaluating generation effort and feedback frequency jointly, rather than a universal setting.
