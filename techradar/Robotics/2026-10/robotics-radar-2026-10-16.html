The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI bets that robotic intelligence should transfer across different hardware instead of being rebuilt for every machine. Their research focuses on motor adaptation, video-prompted task learning, and self-play to achieve this. The core idea is to separate intelligence from the physical body, allowing the model to learn how to understand a body rather than requiring engineers to specify it perfectly. The company is testing whether these capabilities can successfully operate under real-world constraints of speed, reliability, and safety. This approach seeks to enable more flexible and adaptable robotic systems.

Tesla patent reveals touch-sensitive skin for robot hands

Tesla filed a US patent for a touch-sensitive "skin" that can be molded around robot fingers and palms to detect contact, force, and pressure. The patent describes a method where tactile sensors are printed onto flexible materials and then heat-formed into three-dimensional shapes. This proposed sensor uses overlapping conductive regions in two flexible layers to form individual sensing points for pressure changes. The technology is based on both capacitive and piezoresistive approaches, utilizing materials like thermoplastic polyurethane. Tesla suggests this technology could be used for robot fingertips, palms, and end-effectors to improve dexterous manipulation.

ETH Zurich scientists turn WUJI robotic hand into self-propelled mobile manipulator

ETH Zurich scientists demonstrated a WUJI robotic hand capable of self-propelled locomotion by using its fingers for crawling and recovering from falls. The research trained the hand to support its own weight and move without a separate mobility system. This self-supported manipulator could perform manipulation tasks, such as typing on a keyboard and pushing objects to targets. Reinforcement learning was used for training, accounting for the unique characteristics of each finger. This work establishes the hand as a compact, mobile manipulator that integrates locomotion and manipulation capabilities.

Multi-Dimensional Featurizers Yield Fine-Grained Steering and Data Shaping in Robot Foundation Models

Multi-dimensional featurizers can recover multi-dimensional task-relevant features underlying robot foundation model computations. These features include perception, language, proprioceptive state, episode phase, and control variables. Fine-grained steering based on these features causally alters downstream action predictions. Feature-based data attribution allows isolating specific behaviors within training data by masking only a small fraction of tokens. Ultimately, matching featurizers to representation geometry provides more effective tools for understanding and modifying robot behavior.

arXiv - cs.RO

Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle

The method estimates vehicle heading by optimizing a Rayleigh sky model using measurements from a division-of-focal-plane polarimetric camera. This framework integrates a pinhole camera model, a single-scattering Rayleigh polarization model, and a solar position algorithm to predict polarization distributions. A polarization-weighted root mean square error objective function is used to compare measured and simulated patterns, effectively mitigating occlusions from clouds or buildings. Experimental results showed sub-degree RMS yaw errors ranging from $0.718^{\circ}$ to $0.889^{\circ}$ across urban road trajectories. The proposed approach significantly reduces RMS heading error compared to other navigation methods.

arXiv - cs.RO

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

The research establishes feasibility ceilings for drone-mesh interception before any learning policy is applied. It provides closed-form bounds for the spatial coverage and timing constraints for a mesh of interceptors defending a site. These bounds account for uncertainty arising from noisy tracking and time estimation, validating them through Monte Carlo simulations. The analysis shows that naive area ratios miss a ceiling, and optimal packing arrangements are specific to separation constraints. The study argues that these feasibility ceilings should be calculated before any learning experiment and reported with results.

arXiv - cs.RO

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent profile-guided reset-free recovery (PGRR) is introduced to improve gait selection after irreversible leg damage. PGRR predicts multiple outcomes and uses uncertainty-aware estimates to guide online probes without resetting the robot. Across 108 MuJoCo tasks involving various leg injuries, PGRR improved final quality by 0.106 and slot-averaged recovery quality by 0.044 compared to a Gaussian-process baseline. This method also reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points in all damage classes. Conservative uncertainty handling was identified as the primary factor contributing to these performance gains.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

The methodology uses a clarity-driven ergodic search to plan missions for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels (ASVs). This approach maximizes information gathering by linking clarity to differential entropy. The system adaptively updates a target clarity map based on in-situ measurements to prioritize areas of interest. An ergodic path planner then continually re-plans routes based on this map and the vessel's mobility constraints from its renewable power source. Simulations and experiments on a solar-powered ASV demonstrated the effectiveness of this adaptive planning strategy.

arXiv - cs.RO

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing

Existing safety systems fail to recover vehicles from loss-of-control states like icing or hydroplaning, as they focus on prevention rather than recovery. The research trains a racing policy using reinforcement learning to learn drifting as a time-optimal maneuver. This policy is then adapted through continued training to focus on recovery with a minimum-time reward. The transferred policy significantly improves recovery over baseline methods and outperforms other controllers across many loss-of-control states. Deployment on a physical vehicle demonstrated the policy's effectiveness in recovering from states previously deemed unrecoverable.

arXiv - cs.RO

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Sustainably powered mobile robots must optimize their speed trajectories to optimally trade energy for information over time. This is framed as a periodic optimal control problem where robots repeatedly traverse a closed path while balancing propulsion costs with dynamic information gain. The study uses clarity as an information metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control. The research characterizes the nature of optimal control based on the importance of path points and establishes a high-speed information limit. Simulation results, based on salinity data in the Chesapeake Bay, demonstrate the practical utility of these findings.

arXiv - cs.RO

BubbleCBF: Constructive Safety for Second-Order Systems and Application to Autonomous Robot Navigation in Unknown Environments

BubbleCBF develops a constructive method for designing feasible safety constraints for second-order control-affine systems. This approach utilizes signed distance functions to construct collision-free "bubbles" in position space, which are then used to create velocity-dependent safety constraints. A quadratic-program safety filter enforces these constraints based on the overlap of these bubbles. The method proves that the resulting controller is well-defined, continuous, and makes the safe set forward invariant under mild assumptions. The technique was successfully applied to a differential-drive robot for safe autonomous navigation in unknown environments.
