The Sequence Robotics - Issue #957: Generalist and the Rise of Promptable Robots

Generalist hypothesizes that large-scale physical pretraining creates a reusable foundation for robot behavior. The method involves short demonstrations to teach new tasks instead of requiring bespoke datasets. Models across GEN-0, GEN-1, and GEN-1.5 have achieved nearly 99% success on selected tasks via in-context physical prompting. The core question remains whether these performance gains translate to successful, long-horizon real-world deployments. The work focuses on enabling robots to perform complex physical interactions through conversational learning.

Warehouse PPE and Hi Vis in the Age of Automation: Keeping People Safe Around the Machines

Automation in warehouses necessitates specific Personal Protective Equipment (PPE) for personnel sharing space with machinery. While no single legal checklist exists, essential items like high-visibility clothing, safety footwear, and gloves are standard practice based on risk assessments. Visibility is crucial as it helps drivers and sensors perceive people, especially in challenging conditions like low light or cold storage. High-visibility clothing must be selected based on the specific role and risk zone, adhering to standards like EN ISO 20471. A comprehensive PPE strategy must also include footwear, hand protection, and proper garment maintenance for optimal safety.

Ep#112: SlipSense and Touch2Trace

SlipSense uses a multimodal tactile sensor, the TacV5, combining a piezoresistive array and an accelerometer to detect object slipping with high accuracy. The system detects slip events within 23.1 milliseconds, achieving a 96.7% Macro F1 score on a large dataset of 1.4 million frames. The framework utilizes modality-specific encoders and cross-attention to fuse the spatial pressure and vibration cues, demonstrating that the two modalities are crucially complementary. This system achieves zero-shot transfer to new robotic platforms without retraining, showcasing strong generalization. Touch2Trace further applies tactile feedback to dexterous cable tracing, showing that this tactile input significantly improves performance over proprioception-only methods.

TerraTransfer++: Demonstration-Free End-to-End Planner

The TerraTransfer++ method proposes a demonstration-free, end-to-end planning approach for autonomous driving. It leverages self-play in vectorized simulators to generate a diverse distribution of driving scenarios without relying on expert demonstrations. A flow-matching trajectory teacher is distilled from this self-play data to guide the planning process. Lightweight adapters are trained to reproduce the teacher's latent space using specific loss functions. This method yields superior closed-loop policies and planners compared to imitation-based methods on relevant benchmarks.

MPBRS: Multi-Potential-Based Reward Shaping for Unified Reward Configuration in Humanoid Locomotion

Designing effective reward functions for humanoid locomotion in reinforcement learning is challenging due to issues with naive reward shaping. Multi-Potential-Based Reward Shaping (MPBRS) is proposed to address this by shaping rewards using potential function differences to guide exploration without altering the optimal policy. The study implements MPBRS for humanoid locomotion and demonstrates a unified reward configuration across nine different robots. Experiments show that MPBRS yields more consistent scaling and reduced sensitivity to tuning compared to standard reward shaping. This makes tuning reward functions simpler across various humanoid robots.

Tactile-Based Robotic Manipulation: From Mechanics-Driven to Data-Driven Perspectives

Tactile sensing is crucial for dexterous robotic manipulation by providing direct access to physical contact. Research in this area has evolved from mechanics-driven methods, which interpret signals through contact mechanics, to data-driven approaches learning from tactile data. The paper reviews the fundamentals of tactile sensing and the progression from mechanics-driven to data-driven manipulation. Data-driven methods include imitation learning, reinforcement learning, and vision-language-action models for tactile control. Open challenges remain in achieving scalable, generalizable, and robust tactile-enabled robotic systems.

Decision Making and Ride Comfort Evaluation Based on Lane-Change Prediction for Autonomous Vehicles

Predicting lane-change intentions allows autonomous vehicles to make proactive decisions instead of reactive ones. The study evaluates three driving strategies—reactive, prediction-based longitudinal action, and prediction-based lateral action—in simulated merging scenarios. Prediction-based strategies successfully avoided collisions, reducing peak deceleration before impact by 31.5% and 91.0% compared to reactive driving. Ride comfort is assessed using ISO 2631-1-based horizontal vibration estimates. Overall, lane-change prediction supports safer, smoother driving by enabling proactive decision-making and ride comfort evaluation.
