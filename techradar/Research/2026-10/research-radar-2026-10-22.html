[2604.22750] How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money? Analyzing and Predicting Token Consumption in Agentic Coding Tasks

The study systematically analyzes token consumption patterns in agentic coding tasks across eight frontier LLMs. Agentic tasks are found to be significantly more expensive, consuming 1000x more tokens than standard code reasoning, with input tokens driving the overall cost. Token usage is highly stochastic, and accuracy does not necessarily increase with higher token expenditure. Furthermore, models exhibit large variations in token efficiency, with some models consuming substantially more tokens for the same tasks. Finally, frontier models demonstrate poor ability to accurately predict their own token usage.

Pareto-Optimal RTL Code Generation via Multi-Objective Reinforcement Learning with Large Language Models

The work proposes a method for Large Language Models to generate Pareto-optimal RTL code by optimizing the quality and diversity of the entire circuit set, not just a single circuit. This approach moves beyond aggregating PPA objectives into a single reward to handle varying design trade-offs. It employs a generation-based training pipeline to treat coverage improvement across the Pareto set as a sequential generation problem. A reward function based on Hypervolume Contribution (HVC) is used to evaluate the quality of generated circuits relative to the Pareto front. Evaluation shows the method significantly improves relative Hypervolume and circuit diversity over existing optimization techniques.

Fix, Not Just Resample: Bounded Compiler-Guided Repair in Lean 4

Automated theorem provers are improved by a bounded, online repair module for failed steps in Lean 4. This module samples candidate tactics and uses a pretrained model to fix failed tactics based on the compiler's error messages. This repair strategy improves the solve rate over sampling-only methods, achieving up to a $32\%$ relative gain. When Planning Problems Become Hard for LLMs: Separating Structural Complexity from Semantic Interpretability

LLMs struggle with classical planning, but existing benchmarks do not clearly separate structural difficulty from semantic interpretability. The zero-training delete-relaxation heuristic $h_{\mathrm{add}}$ outperformed other learned methods in predicting structural difficulty on planning domains. Renaming predicates causes a significant drop in LLM planning success, indicating sensitivity to semantic information. LLMs using semantic conditioning predict difficulty better than trained methods. Therefore, improving hybrid planning should focus on modeling LLM behavior sensitive to predicate semantics rather than solely on structural predictors.

Motion becomes a gauge symmetry in sequential data because changes are defined by motion rather than absolute states. Standard recurrent networks lack equivariance to motion, as their memory is fixed while input moves. The research extends flow-equivariant networks to capture time-varying motion, treating a sequence as a field over time. MELSTM outperforms existing models in tasks like future-frame prediction and action recognition while being more parameter-efficient.

Depth Separation for Neural Networks under Gaussian Measure

The paper investigates depth separation for neural networks when operating under the standard Gaussian measure in $L^2$ approximation of radial functions. It demonstrates that a depth-3 ReLU network can approximate such a function within a small $L^2$ error, regardless of the polynomial size in $d$. However, approximating these functions with depth-2 networks requires exponentially large weights if the network size is to remain small. A general result shows that radial functions with significant high-frequency mass in their Hermite expansion resist approximation by small, bounded-weight depth-2 networks.

Is In-Context Reinforcement Learning A Posterior Sampler? An Empirical Study

The study investigates whether in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL), particularly methods like Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT), functions as a Bayesian posterior sampler in practice. The researchers establish two necessary conditions for a Bayesian posterior sampler: sufficient-statistic invariance and the martingale property. Statistical hypothesis tests are used to assess whether practically-trained ICRL models satisfy these conditions. The results consistently show that both conditions are violated across Gaussian, Bernoulli, and linear bandit settings. Consequently, the study suggests that ICRL may not behave as Bayesian posterior samplers in practice.

Internal Guidance as a Sampling Method for Tilted Diffusion Models

Internal guidance proposes a novel sampling method for tilted diffusion models by modifying where the output of the base denoiser is evaluated, rather than altering the denoiser itself. This method works by shifting and rescaling the input when dealing with discrete distributions embedded in the hypercube $\{-1,1\}^{d}$. The training algorithm is modified using random diagonal rescalings to ensure the denoiser learns both in-distribution and out-of-distribution evaluations. A single denoiser network can sample binary reward tilts, class labels, and inpainting without requiring external guidance terms or fine-tuning. This approach simplifies guidance by focusing on evaluating the learned denoiser at shifted points.

Schr\"odinger-Bass Bridge Barycenter Models for Measure-Valued Data

Barycenter models based on optimal transport bridges are useful for learning from measure-valued data. The paper introduces four novel barycenter models—SBB-encoder, SBB-decoder, SBB-symmetric, and SBB-Markovian—derived from the Schrödinger-Bass Bridge. Near Minimax Rate for Quantile Distributional Reinforcement Learning

The paper studies the statistical accuracy of estimating the quantile Bellman fixed point $\bm\eta_m$ in quantile distributional reinforcement learning. It constructs a quantile fixed point estimator $\bm\eta_m^{(n)}$ from empirical Markov decision processes and establishes non-asymptotic error bounds under Wasserstein distances. The authors derive root-$n$ high-probability bounds simultaneously over all quantile resolutions $m$ and Wasserstein orders $p$. Further results show resolution-independent rates when reward densities vanish at endpoints, and characterize the exact local asymptotic minimax risk.
