ROS Discourse General: ROS 2 on macOS: measured topic rates for each RMW, a Fast DDS shared-memory pitfall, and tuned presets

Benchmarking ROS 2 RMW implementations on macOS revealed significant performance differences based on the transport used. Fast DDS's default shared-memory (SHM) transport on macOS suffers from a polling tick delay, leading to message bursts and high latency. Cyclone DDS with iceoryx offered the fastest performance, achieving up to 200 kHz rates with very low latency. Zenoh proved to be a good all-rounder, particularly when batching is disabled for high rates and shared memory is enabled for large messages. Optimal configurations involve selecting RMWs based on message size and required throughput.

ROS Discourse General: Fault correlation for ROS 2: one cause, many faults (and what automotive does about it)

Robot startup and chain reactions often present multiple reported faults stemming from a single underlying issue. The example demonstrated how a single lidar connection failure led to several cascading errors in ROS 2 nodes like costmap and navigation. A correlation rule was developed to link the lidar fault as the root cause to subsequent symptoms like costmap timeouts and goal rejections. This rule utilized a time window to associate related events and mute symptoms once the cause was identified. The system suggests using fault history to propose correlation rules, although human approval is still needed for implementation.

BiCAT: Bimanual Coordination Action Tokenization

BiCAT addresses the challenge of modeling bimanual coordination in vision-language-action models by proposing a novel action tokenization method. It shares a common motion representation across both arms while using symmetric cross-arm attention and a joint decoder to condition each arm's tokens. This approach allows the policy to predict both arm tokens in parallel, generating the left arm first and then the right arm conditioned on it. Evaluation across various tasks showed BiCAT significantly improved success, especially in coordination-heavy tasks, with minimal latency. The results suggest that the action tokenizer is an effective mechanism for modeling bimanual coordination.

Learning and Control in Physically Coupled Dynamical Systems

A compact robotic platform called TAG is introduced to study learning and control in physically coupled dynamical systems. TAG integrates an actively tilted maze board and a top-mounted rotary inverted pendulum that physically interact with each other. The study addresses the challenge of accounting for one subsystem's dynamics on the learning process of the other. Complementary model-based and model-free reinforcement learning methods are used to learn control policies for the maze and pendulum. Experimental results show that both policies successfully control their respective systems despite the physical coupling.

RageVLA: Augmentation-Trained Perception and Action for State-of-the-Art Robustness on LIBERO

Vision-language-action (VLA) models struggle with robustness on LIBERO, particularly when objects move or instructions change. RageVLA addresses this by decoupling the task dependencies in the data and policy. It uses a perception engine on frozen DINOv3 features and TinyCLIP embeddings to provide the action model with decoupled scene and instruction views. A flow-matching diffusion transformer is then trained from scratch on this input. RageVLA achieves state-of-the-art performance on LIBERO-PRO, demonstrating robustness to object position changes.

RESCUE Jumper: A Centimeter-Scale Rolling and Jumping Robot for Search and Rescue

RESCUE Jumper is a centimeter-scale mobile robot designed for search and rescue operations in confined spaces. It combines ground rolling with jumping to overcome obstacles that exceed its rolling capability. The robot uses its actuators to roll forward and reverse motion to wind a spring steel tail for jumping. Winding speed controls the jump direction, resulting in jumps with heights up to 9.2 cm. The work provides a model and experimental results for developing jump mechanisms in space-constrained robots.

Learning the Dynamics of Deformable Linear Objects Through Coupled Oscillators for Bimanual Robotic Manipulation

The research proposes $\Phi$-CON, a model combining a floating-base coupled oscillator network (FBCON) with a learned affine diffeomorphism ($\Phi$) to learn the dynamics of deformable linear objects (DLOs). This approach explicitly models the mechanical response of objects, moving beyond simplified deformation models. A model-based proportional-derivative (PD) controller is designed using this structure to ensure closed-loop stability and convergence. Evaluation on simulations and real-world robot experiments shows the method significantly reduces error and improves endpoint regulation compared to model-free baselines.

Panini- Feasible Grammar For Robot Designs

A typed production grammar is introduced to generate both manipulation skills and robot morphologies using case-role relations and parameter inheritance. A geometric realization map translates grammar composition into transformations in SE(3), which shows a measurable defect when inheritance is active. The grammar results allow for geometrically coordinated limb placement, achieving 95% jointly feasible edits. A cross-entropy search method, PANINI, was explored over grammar-based representations. This work demonstrates that typed grammars enhance interpretability and geometric coordination in robot design.

LuxTac: A Flexible, Scalable, and Open-Source Optical Taxel Array with Distributed Three-Axis Force Sensing for Tactile Robot Learning

LuxTac is a flexible, scalable, and open-source optical taxel array designed for tactile robot learning. It consists of a 3.5 mm thick array where each taxel acts as a digital RGBC light-to-digital converter. The array can sense normal force using a plain gel cartridge, and shear force by using a cartridge with colored walls that encode shear information. Distributed three-axis force sensing is achieved across the array, showing strong correlation with indenter forces. Incorporating LuxTac pressure readings into a policy significantly improves peg-in-hole success.

arXiv - cs.RO

SPICE: Stochastic Policy for Interaction in Cluttered Environments

SPICE is a tactile-conditioned diffusion policy designed for robotic reaching through dense plant canopies using only tactile sensing for environmental feedback. It demonstrates three capabilities: moving through foliage, maneuvering around stems, and manipulating obstructing branches. Evaluation across 180 real-robot trials showed that spatial tactile information significantly improved branch maneuvering success compared to other baselines. SPICE achieved 95% success in branch maneuvering and 87.5% zero-shot transfer success on unseen plants. These results highlight the potential of tactile-conditioned generative policies for complex physical interactions in visually occluded environments.

arXiv - cs.RO

Recoverability, Not Invariance: Certified Safety for Robots at the Limits of Performance

Safety certification in robotics should focus on recoverability rather than forward invariance, especially at performance limits where models are uncertain. The paper introduces recoverability, a contraction-theoretic concept, to certify a robot's ability to switch to a trusted recovery mode. This approach guarantees exponential convergence to the recovery mode, even with model errors and disturbances. A switching-time program is derived to dictate when to switch, ensuring safety and optimality. Validation in simulations and hardware tests demonstrated this method's superiority over invariance-based safety filters for aggressive maneuvers.

arXiv - cs.RO

How Uncertain Is My Action? Self-Correcting Visual Planning with Frozen World Models

A frozen latent world model combined with sampling-based model predictive control forms a visual planning method. The system can reverse the model to quantify actuation shifts by comparing predictions with actual observations. A specific score detects these shifts, estimates them, and compensates the planner when an anomaly is detected. This self-correction method significantly improved success rates in vision-based tasks compared to other adaptation methods. Ultimately, the frozen world model acts as a filter to test action validity and correct planning failures.
