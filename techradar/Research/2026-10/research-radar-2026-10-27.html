Inverse Modeling for Laser Pulse Shape Design in Inertial Confinement Fusion

A generative inverse modeling framework is introduced to design optimal Laser Pulse Shapes for Inertial Confinement Fusion. This system maps desired outcomes and target pellet configurations directly to optimized laser pulses. A multi-objective loss function is used to ensure the generated pulses satisfy physical constraints and experimental requirements. Fine-grained control is achieved through constraint-conditioning, inpainting, and gradient-based LP editing mechanisms. The method establishes a data-driven inverse design framework for laser pulse shape optimization in ICF.

IRON-Meme: A Benchmark for Structured Understanding of Identities and Roles in Online Meme Narratives

A framework is introduced to structure the understanding of identities and roles within online meme narratives, incorporating social identities, viewpoints, and narrative roles. IRON-Meme is a human-annotated benchmark of 1,110 political memes across numerous templates to test this understanding. Analysis of IRON-Meme reveals that identities, roles, and templates are systematically interconnected, and most memes assign derogatory roles. Vision-language models, especially frontier-scale and reasoning models, perform best in evaluating this benchmark. The study concludes that meme interpretation depends on adaptation for specific communicative purposes rather than template background knowledge.

Prospect Theory in Large Language Models: A Meta-Analysis of Loss Aversion, Diminishing Sensitivity, and Probability Weighting

A meta-analysis of 102 studies on 32 language models examined the presence of Prospect Theory components. Loss aversion was found to be modest, with a pooled weight ($\lambda$) of 1.26, significantly lower than human estimates. The value functions exhibited linearity rather than strict concavity under the Tversky-Kahneman method, but showed more concavity under the Tanaka method. Probability weighting displayed a human-like inverse S shape, though this finding was consistent across a few studies. Overall, LLMs exhibit little loss aversion and diminishing sensitivity, but retain human-like probability distortion.

Does LeWorldModel Preserve Local Action Geometry?

The study examines how closely the pretrained LeWorldModel (LeWM) reproduces the effects of small action changes using matched simulator and model responses. Action sensitivity is measured with Gram matrices across different perturbation scales for states from Reacher and OGBench Cube. Reducing perturbations by tenfold decreases the median normalized Gram discrepancy by approximately 70\% on Reacher and 47\% on Cube. Geometry measured in pixels and embeddings varies more across scales than the chosen physical-state geometry. The findings suggest that action sensitivity should be evaluated based on scale-specific metrics, though the overall effect on control remains an open question.

End-to-End Differentiable Diffusion Conditioning for Physics-Informed Optimization

Differentiable Diffusion Conditioning (D2C) is proposed for offline Model-Based Optimization (MBO) of high-dimensional physical systems. This method exploits the differentiability of the reverse denoising chain to enable end-to-end gradient-based optimization. When and How Does Muon Precondition? The Role of Structured Gradient Noise

Muon, an optimizer for large language model training, is studied in relation to the geometry of gradient noise. Under classical isotropic noise, Muon offers no acceleration beyond a learning rate rescaling. Structured noise from mini-batch sampling in associative memory introduces effects that shape Muon's preconditioning benefits. The analysis reveals Muon can soften the dependence on task frequency, balancing learning for frequent and rare tasks. Experiments confirm that momentum reduces sampling noise while tracking changing gradients, suggesting practical benefits for LLM pretraining.

A Measure-Geometric Theory of Robustness in Machine Learning

The paper develops a measure-geometric theory for robustness in machine learning. Robustness is defined as escaping a retained task state relative to a reference measure, a perturbation gauge, and an admissible realization mechanism. It establishes a unified geometric account distinguishing the structural possibility of escape from its ambient realization under observable readouts. The theory introduces a mesoscopic organization of escape geometry to characterize phenomena like vulnerable mass and progress. Ultimately, robustness is recast as the geometric formation, organization, realization, and persistence of escape, offering an analysis beyond scalar criteria.

The study focuses on online inference for averaged Riemannian stochastic gradient descent (RSGD) where parameters reside on a manifold. Affine-invariant Cubic Newton with Weak Learners

The paper generalizes the Affine-Invariant Cubic Newton scheme to convex optimization with weak learners to address limitations in gradient boosting methods. It analyzes the scheme using average cosine values and directional improvements in the Hessian-induced norm. The weak-AICN update is shown to be equivalent to a damped weak-Newton step with an explicit stepsize. Under semi-strong self-concordance, the framework establishes a global $\mathcal{O}(1/\overline{\Theta}_k^4 k^2)$ rate, improving upon previous bounds. The work also presents methods like weak-AdaAICN and shows that Newton coordinate descent is a special case of this optimization.

On the Error Analysis of Triangular Transport Maps for Conditional Generation via MMD-based Objectives

Conditional generative models using triangular transport maps lack theoretical quality guarantees. The study investigates minimum divergence estimators of these maps using MMD-based objectives to analyze the error between joint and conditional errors. It demonstrates that minimizing the joint MMD does not generally recover the conditionals, but proposes an estimator minimizing the conditional criterion. A non-asymptotic error bound is derived, decomposing the estimation error into approximation, estimation, and sampling errors. The results suggest training criteria should focus on the conditional error rather than the joint error.

Projection-Based Federated Learning on Compact Submanifolds with Geometry-Independent Differential Privacy

This method introduces DP-RFedProj, a record-level differentially private federated learning approach for models on compact submanifolds. It achieves privacy by aggregating through closed-form projections and privatizing gradients within a fixed ambient geometry. The technique guarantees $f$-DP independently of the manifold, aggregation scheme, and local optimizer. When using Riemannian SGD and projected averaging, privacy loss scales with the manifold's intrinsic dimension, not the ambient one. Experiments show this method achieves better utility than prior methods with less noise.

MetaARIMA proposes using metalearning to perform configuration-level transfer learning for ARIMA models. Intrinsic Gradient Barriers in Pushforward Deep Generative Models

Deep generative models often struggle with heavy-tailed or multimodal distributions due to intrinsic properties of the transport problem. This work establishes lower bounds on the gradients and Jacobians of pushforward mappings between log-concave source and complex target distributions. Large gradients are required when transporting to heavy-tailed targets or between well-separated modes in multimodal targets. These large derivatives represent trade-offs between model expressivity and numerical stability. Simple reparametrizations can mitigate these gradient obstructions when the objective is density approximation.
