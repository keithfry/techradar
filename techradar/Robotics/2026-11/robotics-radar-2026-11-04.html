New ISO interoperability standard covers collaboration among mobile robots from multiple vendors

A new international standard, ISO 21423:2026, has been published to facilitate communication among industrial mobile robots regardless of vendor. This standard establishes common requirements for automated guided vehicles, autonomous mobile robots, and similar systems operating in the same facility. It specifies how mobile robots, fleet managers, and other equipment can exchange information such as location, speed, and tasking. This interoperability aims to improve system efficiency by reducing robot interference and increasing availability. The standard also covers communication with external systems like ERP tools and building automation systems.

Tokio Marine HCC and Adrian Flux Launch UK Home Insurance for Humanoid Robots

Tokio Marine HCC and Adrian Flux have launched the UK's first household insurance policy for domestic humanoid robots. This coverage is available through Adrian Flux's home insurance offering and is underwritten by Tokio Marine HCC International. The policy provides protection for the robot, damage it causes, and personal liability, including options for emergency recovery or safe disablement. The coverage includes protection against damage, theft, and liability, with a specified limit of £5,000 for sudden damage to the home or belongings. This initiative aims to address the insurance needs arising from the ownership of domestic humanoid robots.

Ben Waters on the latest wireless charging strategies

Charging strategy is crucial for optimizing robot fleet performance, affecting uptime, infrastructure, and battery size. Opportunity charging, where robots move to charging stations when batteries are low, is presented as an efficient wireless solution for large fleets. Companies should integrate power infrastructure planning and battery specifications into the initial system design process. Furthermore, charging should be viewed as an integral part of robot design rather than an afterthought. WiBotic is innovating by developing smaller, higher-power, and more robust wireless charging systems for various applications like drones and humanoids.

LiberAI Unveils Liber-0: Learning from Human Experience for Zero-Shot Robot Manipulation

Liber-0 is a world action model pretrained on over 2.2 million hours of human experience to enable zero-shot robot manipulation. It integrates broad visual learning with glove-recorded motion and kinematic alignment to facilitate human-to-robot transfer. The model's performance benefits from grounding human video in measured actions, which helps it learn behaviors absent from robot demonstrations. Liber-0 demonstrated zero-shot manipulation on tasks generated by GPT-6, achieving 36.0% overall task success. The approach suggests that accumulated human experience can effectively guide robots when paired with reliable action grounding and embodiment alignment.

Humanoid Robots in Warehouses vs AMRs: Which Automation Makes Sense in 2026?

Autonomous Mobile Robots (AMRs) are proven for moving inventory and are recommended as the initial investment in warehouses due to faster deployment and clear payback. Humanoid robots offer the potential to automate manipulation tasks in human-designed spaces, focusing on handling objects. AMRs automate movement, while humanoids aim to automate specific handling steps, requiring more complex training data. Successful implementation depends on task fit, operational costs, and gathering high-quality data for robot learning. Most operators should use AMRs for travel optimization while piloting humanoids for specific, difficult manipulation tasks.
