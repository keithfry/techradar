Quantile Regression Caught the House Price OLS Missed by $55K

Ordinary Least Squares (OLS) regression is problematic when data exhibits heteroscedasticity, meaning the variance of the errors is not constant across predictor values. OLS models the mean and fails to capture the spread of potential outcomes, leading to unreliable predictions, especially for high-variance data. Quantile Regression offers a solution by estimating different quantiles of the conditional distribution rather than just the average. By fitting models for specific quantiles, one can create prediction intervals that reflect the true uncertainty for individual observations. This approach provides a more robust and complete picture of market dynamics compared to relying solely on OLS estimates.

How to Build Multi-Agent Systems with the OpenAI Agents SDK

Multi-agent systems can be built by splitting responsibilities using two primary delegation patterns: agent-as-a-tool and handoff. Agent-as-a-tool allows a calling agent to use specialist agents as tools to gather information, while handoff involves transferring control to a specialist agent for completion. These patterns are used to construct complex systems based on various topologies and execution strategies. Common multi-agent patterns include fan-out/fan-in, pipeline, hierarchical structures, evaluator-optimizer, and swarm architectures. Ultimately, these systems are founded on the interaction mechanisms of agent-as-a-tool and handoff.

Hugging Face Blog

Your Experts Have Partners. There's HOPE for Keeping Them Together

Mixture-of-Experts (MoE) models face a memory bottleneck because all experts must occupy memory at inference, even when most are idle. Expert pruning removes less useful experts, but existing first-order methods ignore the cooperative interactions between experts. HOPE (Higher-Order Pruning of Experts) is a second-order method that accounts for these pairwise expert interactions by optimizing over an interaction matrix. This approach results in superior pruning performance, especially at higher removal rates, by preserving expert cooperation. HOPE's process involves calibrating experts, solving a quadratic program to find the optimal prune-set, and finally pruning the model.
