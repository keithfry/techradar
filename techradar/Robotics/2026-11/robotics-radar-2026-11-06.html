Researchers at the University of Tokyo built a life-size robotic leg system using 3D-printed structures mimicking muscles, tendons, and ligaments. Apple's HALO Teaches a Unitree G1 to Navigate Unseen Rooms With Simulation-Generated Data

Apple researchers trained a Unitree G1 humanoid using a system called HALO to navigate unseen rooms based on simulation-generated data. HALO successfully completed 91% of 360 single-task trials across six real-world environments without teleoperation. The system uses a multi-layered approach involving a vision-language model, a motion generator, and a motion tracker. Training involved generating 162,698 episodes using synthetic data from 1,000 procedurally generated rooms in Isaac Sim. Although successful in single tasks, chaining skills proved less reliable, highlighting ongoing challenges in real-world generalization.

arXiv - cs.RO

Ctrl-F: Force as an Interface for Humanoid Loco-Manipulation

Force is proposed as a natural command channel bridging motion imitation and object interaction for humanoid manipulation. Ctrl-F introduces a hierarchical framework that augments motion tracking with explicit 3-D force commands at designated contact points like hands or feet. This framework allows for the online adjustment of force commands based on object state and proprioception, which is difficult for force-blind trackers. Experimental results show Ctrl-F successfully handles tasks like dragging, pushing, and kicking boxes up to 20kg, adapting to changing loads. Consequently, the system enables effective locomotion manipulation requiring significant, sustained interaction forces.

MESA: An Evaluation Framework for Compositional, Semantic, and Spatial Generalization in Robotics

MESA is an evaluation framework designed to precisely measure language-conditioned policy generalization in robotics. It offers five evaluation suites and four generalization axes: spatial configuration, object instance, object category, and subtask composition. The framework utilizes a pipeline called MESA-Gen for generating tasks and demonstrations at scale. Evaluation on seven baseline methods showed moderate performance, suggesting room for further progress. MESA rankings are broadly consistent with RoboArena scores, indicating relevance beyond simulation.

arXiv - cs.RO

ELA: End-to-End Latent Action Policy Learning from Video Demonstrations

Robot imitation learning often suffers from limited action-labeled data despite abundant video demonstrations. ELA is an end-to-end framework that learns from video by optimizing latent actions jointly for policy learning and action decoding. It trains a latent action model, policy, and action decoder to leverage video data with few demonstrations. Experiments on manipulation tasks show ELA consistently surpasses previous latent action baselines. The method easily integrates with existing imitation learning approaches using lightweight decoders.

arXiv - cs.RO

Contact-Implicit Whole-Body Trajectory Optimization for Dynamic Humanoid Locomotion on Parameterizable Uneven Terrain

A trajectory planner is presented for humanoid robots to perform dynamic movements on complex, parameterizable terrains using a contact-implicit whole-body optimization framework. This method generates feasible motions by considering the robot's full dynamics and kinematics without requiring predefined contact sequences. A mathematical model approximates ground geometry with a smooth surface, modeling ground interaction through novel contact constraints for arbitrary surfaces. The approach is validated using nonlinear programming across various terrain topologies, with feasibility tested in simulation and on the ErgoCub robot. Dynamic trajectory generation is achieved by embedding high-level requirements into the optimization cost function.

arXiv - cs.RO

$\mathcal{C}^\infty$ Smooth Trajectory Planning with Application to Soft Robots

Smooth trajectory planning is crucial for high motion performance and avoiding vibrations in robots, but existing smoothness requirements may be insufficient for soft continuum robots. The work introduces a $\mathcal{C}^\infty$-smooth trajectory generator to address this gap by creating infinitely differentiable paths. These trajectories ensure continuity in velocity, acceleration, jerk, and other derivatives between sequential states. The method specifically handles null boundary conditions where all derivatives at the start and end are zero. The approach was verified through simulations and hardware testing.

arXiv - cs.RO

Correct Robots Before They Make Mistakes: Proactive Human-in-the-Loop Intervention via Preference Learning

A framework called Proactive Human-in-the-Loop (PHIL) allows operators to intervene by previewing policy predictions before they cause mistakes. During intervention, PHIL uses an extended reality interface to display predicted trajectories and pairs rejected predictions with corrective actions. Preference-based post-training encourages the policy to favor these human corrections. Evaluation on manipulation tasks showed that PHIL increased success rates for diffusion policy and X-VLA compared to other methods. Policies corrected proactively also exhibited fewer unnecessary recovery behaviors in successful episodes.

OdorSpaces: Visual-Olfactory Embodied Navigation in 3D Environments

OdorSpaces is a physics-based simulation platform and benchmark for visual-olfactory embodied navigation in 3D environments. It augments Habitat-Sim by simulating 3D odor transport and dispersion using the Fire Dynamics Simulator (FDS). The system allows agents to navigate photorealistic indoor spaces using both visual and olfactory inputs. Experiments focus on source-seeking tasks like gas-leak and fire localization. A multimodal reinforcement learning policy developed for this system outperforms baselines in visual-olfactory navigation.

arXiv - cs.RO

OUCH: Objective, Uniform, Controlled Haptic Stimulation for Automated Neurological Assessment

OUCH is a wrist-worn device utilizing pneumatic actuation to deliver controlled, noxious mechanical stimuli for automated neurological assessment. It employs stacked pneumatic pouch actuators, an indenter, and a compliant spacer to ensure the indenter contacts the skin during actuation. Testing demonstrated that OUCH achieved significantly higher localized contact pressure compared to a commercial algometer while eliciting discomfort with less applied force. This work demonstrates a method for objective, uniform, and controlled haptic stimulation. The system provides a foundation for automated neurological assessment in hospitalized patients.

arXiv - cs.RO

GRASP: Gradient-Guided Sampling for Contact-Rich Physics-Based Motion Retargeting

GRASP proposes a method for physics-based motion retargeting by combining sampling-based exploration with gradient-guided refinement. It extends tempered sequential Monte Carlo by fitting local quadratic cost models to archived rollouts to guide Markov chain Monte Carlo rejuvenation. The approach utilizes gradients from these rollouts to refine the sampling process without needing a differentiable simulator. GRASP-MPC scales this optimization to long demonstrations using warm-started receding-horizon planning. Experiments on hands and humanoids achieved accurate retargeting with minimal task-specific contact rewards.
