What battery technology will power the next generation of humanoid robots?

Current humanoid robots primarily use NMC-based lithium-ion batteries, balancing energy density with necessary performance criteria. Future developments are exploring solid-state batteries, which offer higher energy density and improved safety by replacing liquid electrolytes. Lithium-sulfur cells present an alternative with high theoretical energy density but face challenges with cycle life and fast charging limitations. Improvements to NMC cathodes, by increasing nickel content, can also boost performance while reducing cobalt use. Ultimately, the transition to advanced battery technologies will be driven by the need for higher energy density, safety, and longevity in humanoid applications.

From Teleoperation to Long-Horizon Dexterity: Evaluating Visuomotor Skills and Offline Proxies for Dexterous Manipulation

Imitation learning policies often fail in real-world robotic manipulation because conventional optimization signals do not align with actual success. Evaluation of 281 policies using real-robot trials on grasping and insertion revealed that observation and action representations were dominant factors. Specific representation choices, such as region-of-interest cropping for point-cloud policies, significantly improved performance. Furthermore, absolute action prediction rather than relative prediction boosted insertion success for vision-language-action policies. Representation choices are critical determinants of imitation learning performance, though offline metrics alone are insufficient.

FlapSim: A Composable MuJoCo Simulation Platform and Reduced-Order Aerodynamic Model Hierarchy for Flapping-Wing Robotics

FlapSim is a modular MuJoCo simulation platform designed for flapping-wing robotics by combining robot descriptions, wing geometry, and composable aerodynamic models. It evaluates five reduced-order aerodynamic configurations against a high-fidelity $50\,\mu\text{s}$ blade-element reference across various flight conditions. Incorporating multirate synchronization, inflow/gust coupling, and harmonic synthesis significantly improves simulation fidelity. The results indicate that timing, inflow, geometry, and harmonic representation are critical factors in the accuracy-computation tradeoff of the reduced-order model. Ultimately, the work characterizes the necessary trade-offs for simulating flapping-wing dynamics effectively.

Confidence-Modulated Safety Filtering for End-to-End Policies

End-to-end policies require safety filters, which typically maintain a fixed level of conservativeness regardless of the policy's actual competence. The authors propose a method to modulate this filtering based on the policy's confidence level at runtime. Inside the operational design domain (ODD), high confidence allows for performance gains, while low confidence introduces a margin to flag where the filter may be failing. This approach combines the strengths of both permissive and conservative filters. Preliminary simulations suggest this confidence-modulated filtering effectively combines these filter types.

arXiv - cs.RO

STAR: Steering Action Representations during Inference

Vision-language-action models show strong capabilities in robotic manipulation but action representations near the output do not fully preserve task-relevant visual grounding. STAR introduces a training-free inference method for pre-trained VLAs by applying an exponential moving average over late-layer residual updates. This method allows later action representations to inherit object-aware update directions from earlier layers without model updates. STAR also uses proprioceptive states to adjust mixing strength, employing conservative mixing when execution dynamics change rapidly. Experiments demonstrate that STAR improves late-layer object awareness and increases average task success rates by 4.0 percentage points.

Contact Imitation Interface: Transferring Dexterous Skills by Showing How Contact Unfolds

A framework called Contact Imitation Interface (CIMI) is presented to transfer dexterous skills from human demonstrations to robots. CIMI utilizes contact-grounded demonstration and learning interfaces to address challenges in producing robot-transferable actions. The demonstration interface combines shared contact geometry and fingertip RGB views with contact-alignment-first retargeting. The learning interface employs a hierarchical policy that predicts contact-surface trajectories and coordinates finger/wrist commands. This method improves the mean final-stage success by at least 50 percentage points across six hand-task pairs.

Mechanistic Interpretability for End-to-End Self-Driving

End-to-end self-driving models lack transparency despite strong planning performance. Mechanistic interpretability is applied using Sparse Autoencoders (SAEs) on a single-camera path planning model's internal activations. Unsupervised clustering of SAE latents reveals interpretable structures related to route intent, speed, and braking, accurately recovering route intent. These SAE features are found to be visually grounded and systematically respond to scene changes. Finally, using these representations in an adapter improved trajectory selection error, confirming their utility for interpretability.

arXiv - cs.RO

A Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter for Heterogeneous Multi-Robot Navigation

Safe navigation for heterogeneous multi-robot teams is complicated by differing braking and turning capabilities and the need for coordinated control actions. The Capability-Aware Backup-Recoverability Filter (CA-BRF) is proposed to address this by ensuring navigation actions preserve access to certified recovery maneuvers. It constructs backup-recoverable sets based on pairwise safety and robot constraints to define capability-dependent recovery certificates. CA-BRF filters candidate controls by selecting admissible interpolations, preserving recoverability while satisfying all pairwise recovery conditions. This method establishes recursive feasibility and safety for the system during complex interactions involving up to 30 heterogeneous robots.
