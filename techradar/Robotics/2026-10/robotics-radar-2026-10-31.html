What Is Worken? AGIBOT’s Proposed Measure of Productive Robot Work

Worken is a proposed measure of productive robot operation combining the number of active robots, autonomous working hours, and an output efficiency ratio. It differentiates independent task execution from nonproductive periods like waiting or manual intervention. The formula multiplies these three factors to calculate "Effective Intelligent Work Hours." This measure is not a price for robot labor and requires defining task-specific benchmarks for accurate results. Practical use depends on consistent operational data and further testing to establish reliable definitions.

Why Automated Cells Are Pulling Inspection Onto the Shop Floor

Automated cells are bringing inspection closer to the shop floor to improve efficiency. As automation grows, moving Coordinate Measuring Machines (CMMs) next to production allows for rapid part checking. This proximity shortens the feedback loop, enabling immediate adjustments to machining processes. Shared data standards like MTConnect allow robots and inspection equipment to communicate effectively. This integration enables real-time quality control, catching variations early and increasing production confidence.

Ep#114: Oopsie Data

Most existing robotics data consists only of successful robot behaviors. Research areas like world models and reward model learning require examples of failure, such as unsuccessful manipulation trajectories. Claas Voelcker, Arpit Bahety, and Paul Zhou are presenting their work on "oopsie data," which involves collecting these valuable failures. Their goal is to create the first large, community-driven open-source dataset of failure examples. This effort aims to provide necessary data for advancing robotics research.
