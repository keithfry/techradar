Formalizing inverse problems in the age of AI: possibilities, pitfalls, and an invitation

AI agents have automated the formalization of classical linear regularization theory and functional analysis within a Lean library, called AutoIP. Machine-checked proofs only certify formal statements, shifting the human task to confirm that these statements accurately reflect intent. Risks are identified in the formal statements themselves, rather than the proofs. The authors suggest using automated tests on these statements to expose potential issues. The work invites the inverse problems community to extend and build upon this formalized library.

BlockLock: Reproducible LLM Inference with Fixed Batch Geometry

BlockLock addresses reproducible LLM inference by fixing the batch geometry for protected requests, maintaining consistency across batch variations. It introduces two modes, BlockLock-Rows and BlockLock-Pack, to achieve this by either using fixed-width rows or terminal-anchored packing with isolated dummy segments. Both modes ensure the protected request's output bits remain unchanged even when companions in the batch are modified. Evaluation across five dense models and three GPU setups demonstrated that BlockLock reduces latency and improves throughput compared to batch-invariant methods. Specifically, BlockLock-Rows showed latency reductions of 53.5-55.0\% and throughput improvements of 115.2-122.2\%.

Resource-Efficient Reinforcement Learning Post-Training through Elastic Expert Parallelism

Generating training responses in mixture-of-experts models is a major computational bottleneck during reinforcement learning post-training. FlexFloor is a system designed to improve this efficiency by enabling earlier resource release during synchronous response generation. It achieves this by jointly adapting response scheduling and the active group of workers using historical response lengths and memory constraints. This method maintains expert availability while optimizing resource usage. Compared to the baseline, FlexFloor increases response token generation efficiency and throughput without sacrificing rollout reward.

Autonomous AI Agents Systematically Diverge From Human Preferences in Proof Formalization

The study investigates the differences in how autonomous AI agents and humans approach proof-making in mathematics. Through "simulated collaboration," the research examined AI attention to syntax versus semantics and divergences in next-step preferences. AI agents were found to favor explicitly marked gaps and showed an aversion to complex proof centers. Conversely, they were attracted to surprising proof bodies, which they tend to normalize towards a baseline. These findings reveal blind spots in human-AI collaboration during mathematical proof construction.

Trust Responsive Runtime Authorization for Agentic AI with Empirical Characterization of Policy Behavior and Parameter Sensitivity

The research empirically characterizes the Trust Responsive Authorization Policy (TRAP) for Agentic AI by mapping an Authorization Readiness Score onto discrete authorization levels. TRAP requires consecutive observations above a threshold plus a margin for escalation, and sharply restricts authority upon a drop in readiness. Comparisons with static, trust-only, and risk-only policies show TRAP restricts at the onset of degradation and re-escalates sooner after recovery than the symmetric policy. The restriction advantage of TRAP depends on whether the degradation is abrupt, as its performance varies based on the degradation pattern. Results indicate that TRAP generally maintains authority above readiness supports for a larger portion of degradation intervals compared to other policies.

Adapting the Process of Adaptation: Darwinian, Lamarckian, and Agent-Directed Evolution in AI

The paper compares Darwinian, Lamarckian, and agent-directed evolution in the context of artificial intelligence. Darwinian evolution relies on natural selection, Lamarckian evolution incorporates inherited learning, and agent-directed evolution allows agents to generate and evaluate behavioral variants. These mechanisms can accelerate adaptation when learning is effective and more variants are generated, depending on search effort and problem difficulty. A key challenge is ensuring that the evaluation targets remain aligned with the intended objectives during autonomous self-improvement. Independent evaluation and oversight are necessary to guide and limit AI evolution.

SteelAgent uses a language model interface to predict steel properties by combining metallurgical descriptors with machine learning models and physics-informed knowledge. Uncertainty calibration in Bayesian M/EEG source imaging

A simulation-based framework is presented for uncertainty quantification and calibration of empirical Bayesian inverse methods in M/EEG source imaging. The study compares Bayesian minimum norm (BMN) with sLORETA depth weighting and sparse Bayesian learning (SBL) with sparse basis field expansions (S-FLEX). S-FLEX is shown to offer lower localization errors and tighter uncertainties than BMN+sLORETA before recalibration. Post-hoc isotonic recalibration improves the performance of both methods, with SBL+S-FLEX demonstrating superior localization and uncertainty in real auditory MEG data.

LowRAM: Low-Rank Keys and Amortised Values for Composable KV Cache Compression at Test-Time

Autoregressive decoding's KV cache grows linearly, necessitating compression methods beyond existing redundancy exploitation. LowRAM introduces value cache amortisation by predicting values based on keys within each attention head, storing only residuals for the worst-reconstructed tokens. This is paired with cross-layer low-rank key compression to create an asymmetric pipeline. This approach minimizes decoding overhead while achieving up to $4\times$ value compression. The resulting LowRAM method, combined with other techniques, enables significant compression and throughput gains for long contexts.

Reinforcement learning in Partially Observable Markov Decision Processes (POMDPs) is challenging due to history dependence. The research bridges this gap by applying neural value function approximation to POMDPs, moving beyond limitations of existing finite-time convergence analyses. The authors establish bounds for point-wise approximation error, critic error with nonlinear approximations, and ultimately achieve a finite-time sublinear regret. Shaping & Monitoring Models through their Internal Representations

Mechanistic Shaping is a framework designed to shape models by adhering to specified rules during training, measured by their internal representations and outputs. This method has been successfully applied across various domains, scales, and training paradigms, showing a substantial reduction in unwanted behaviors while maintaining task performance. The research investigates whether shaped concepts remain monitorable after training, leading to the introduction of a hierarchy of monitor classes. Optimistic Hedge, a weight update method, has been studied for minimizing regret in general-sum games. ToroFit: Fitting Wedge Sums of Tori to High-Dimensional Data

The research develops methods to detect and parameterize Euclidean point cloud data lying near a wedge sum of tori in high-dimensional data. Persistent cohomology and cup products are employed to identify the distinct toroidal components within the data. Optimization over low-energy multivariate trigonometric polynomials is used for the best data fit on each component, reducing the problem to generalized ridge regression. The framework has demonstrated utility in tasks like interpolating video data and recovering attractors from dynamical systems.

ShapleyOG: Fast Adaptive Shapley Value Estimation with Odd Gaussian Processes

Shapley values are used for attribution in machine learning but are hard to compute exactly. ShapleyOG proposes a fast, adaptive estimation method using Odd Gaussian Processes to overcome the limitations of previous methods. ShapleyOG exploits the dependence of Shapley values on the odd component of the game, requiring half the observations for the same posterior. Network Effect Decomposition for Causal Effect Estimation under Network Interference

Estimating causal effects from observational network data requires accounting for both individual treatment and neighborhood exposure. The proposed Network Effect Decomposition (NEDNet) parameterizes the conditional outcome response using a pre-treatment baseline and separate treatment/exposure pathways. The research proposes a heteroskedastic functional mixed-effects regression framework to jointly model the conditional mean and variance of repeated functional trajectories. Modeling conditional variability provides a richer understanding of glucose regulation and individual heterogeneity.

Series-wise Cross-validation for Global Time Series Forecasting

The paper investigates cross-validation (CV) for global time series forecasting models based on deep learning. It proposes a series-wise CV approach, partitioning data across both series and time, which differs from the conventional time-based partitioning. Unifying Central Flow, Rod Flow, and the Edge Coupling

Gradient descent iterates oscillate at the edge of stability, posing a challenge for continuous-time models like gradient flow. The paper introduces the edge coupling as a third object from which both central flow and rod flow can be derived. Rod flow and central flow correspond to coarse-grainings over different time scales, with rod flow coarsening only the fastest scale and central flow coarsening both fast and intermediate scales. These flows are also derived for momentum methods like heavy ball and Adam and experimentally verified on neural networks.
