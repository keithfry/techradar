How to Calculate and Operationalize Customer Lifetime Value

Customer Lifetime Value (CLV) is a unit-economics metric estimating the total profit expected from a customer relationship over its lifespan, incorporating revenue, costs, frequency, retention, and acquisition expenses. Operationalizing CLV involves gathering detailed customer history and behavior data, building prediction models using methods like Buy 'Til You Die (BTYD) models, and mapping revenue and cost streams across customer lifecycle events. Key to operationalization are identifying High-Value Moments (HVMs) and High-Value Actions (HVAs) to guide interventions. A structured customer segmentation model, often based on RFM and CLV, is necessary to calibrate marketing and retention spending based on customer archetypes. Ultimately, CLV provides a framework for optimizing investment by ensuring that the marginal value of an intervention equals or exceeds its cost.

The Birthday Paradox Shows Small LLMs Pick From About 6 of 40 Words

A study used the birthday paradox to estimate the effective number of options a small language model selects from. By asking a model to randomly select from 40 words 300 times and counting repetitions, the researchers found the models behaved as if they only selected from about 6 words. This method, using the effective number of options formula, reveals how concentrated the model's answers are, not the total possible diversity. Chat-tuned models generally showed a lower effective number of options compared to their base models. The results suggest that models do not pick truly evenly when they have inherent biases.
