Laboratories can only simulate certain aspects of real-world environments, making it challenging for robots to be resilient and predictable in deployment. Early field testing helps identify flaws early in design, speeding up the design feedback loop cycle. Building a strong robotics deployment pipeline requires iterating development on platforms and designing technology that people will trust, including factors like signaling intentions and safety points. Light-Loco-Parkour: Versatile Perceptive Whole-Body Locomotion via Multi-Skill Distillation

LLP, a single deployable policy, enables autonomous perceptive whole-body locomotion on a humanoid in outdoor settings using only onboard sensing. The system acquires terrain-conditioned skills from sparse seeds and learns autonomous skill transitions from reward. LLP outperforms existing humanoid systems by tracking velocity in open terrain, executing whole-body traversal at obstacles, and resuming locomotion afterward, with no reference input or skill label. Simulation and real-world experiments show high success across benchmarked terrains and unseen obstacle variations.

arXiv - cs.RO

Semantic Haptic Feedback Enhances Dexterous Robotic Teleoperation

Semantic haptic feedback enhances dexterous robotic teleoperation by using abstract patterns to convey critical information about robot states, simplifying hardware requirements and enabling one-to-many mappings. The approach categorizes robot states into "Confirmations" and "Exceptions", implements a modular haptic rendering pipeline in robot simulation, and delivers semantic haptic feedback through pneumatic and vibrotactile wristbands. Three evaluation studies show that semantic haptics performs similarly to other feedback approaches in unimanual tasks but achieves superior performance in bimanual tasks with reduced task workload, increased situational awareness, and overall preference.

Toward Certified Functional Safety for Industrial Humanoid Robots: The Fail-Passive Gap and a Feasibility Study

Industrial humanoid robots face a lack of functional safety certification due to the fail-passive gap, where actively-controlled states on the robot-side violate the assume-safe state assumption. A certified external safety chain is used to locate this issue precisely, pinpointing it to the robot-side reaction chain. The approach involves analyzing the balancing policy and its residual risk, but hosting an industrial software-defined automation controller reinforces the fail-passive gap and localizes it to the SDA-to-balancing-policy interface.

arXiv - cs.RO

Biconvex Optimization for Smooth Minimum-Time Trajectories around Convex Obstacles

Biconvex optimization is presented for minimum-time motion planning around convex obstacles, ensuring convergence and supporting derivative constraints to arbitrary order. The method jointly convexifies the objective and constraints through a change of variables, handling collision avoidance via time-varying separating planes. The approach reliably produces high-quality trajectories with computation times comparable to state-of-the-art decomposition-based motion planners in drone navigation and dual-arm bin unloading experiments.

Control Barrier Functions via Minkowski Operations for Safe Navigation among Polytopes

Researchers have proposed a method to control barrier functions via Minkowski operations for safe navigation among polytopes in robotics. The approach computes an exact Signed Distance Function (SDF) using companion convex programs and leverages geometric properties of 2D Minkowski operations to derive a unified analytical expression for the gradient of the SDF.

arXiv - cs.RO

Contact-Driven Localization in a Freeform Robotic Self-Assembled Structure

Researchers Mohammadali Rashidioun, Michael Sosa, and Petras Swissler propose a novel contact-driven localization method for modular robots that uses local communication through binary contact information to form diverse structures. The method leverages a virtual-force framework where robots attract toward dock-connected neighbors and repel from non-connected ones, requiring minimal onboard sensing and no external infrastructure. Simulations show effective localization during the assembly of towers and cantilevers, enabling accurate, scalable, and free-form self-assembly.
