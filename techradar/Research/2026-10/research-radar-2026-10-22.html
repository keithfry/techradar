[2604.22750] How Do AI Agents Spend Your Money? Analyzing and Predicting Token Consumption in Agentic Coding Tasks

The study systematically analyzes token consumption patterns in agentic coding tasks using eight frontier LLMs on SWE-bench Verified. Agentic tasks are found to be uniquely expensive, consuming 1000x more tokens than code reasoning, with input tokens dominating the cost. Token usage is highly variable and stochastic, and accuracy often peaks at intermediate costs rather than higher ones. Models exhibit significant differences in token efficiency, with some models consuming substantially more tokens than others on the same tasks. Furthermore, frontier models generally fail to accurately predict their own token usage.

When Planning Problems Become Hard for LLMs: Separating Structural Complexity from Semantic Interpretability

Large language models struggle with classical planning, but existing benchmarks do not effectively separate structural difficulty from semantic interpretability. The zero-training delete-relaxation heuristic outperforms learned alternatives in predicting structural difficulty across tested domains. Therefore, improving hybrid planning requires modeling LLM sensitivity to predicate semantics rather than focusing only on structural predictors.

Motion Equivariant Convolutional LSTM: When Motion Becomes a Gauge Symmetry

Motion becomes a gauge symmetry in sequential data because changes in data are due to motion, which is a symmetry of the data. Standard recurrent networks lack equivariance to motion because their memory is not sensitive to input movement. The proposed Motion Equivariant Convolutional LSTM (MELSTM) treats motion as a gauge field, making the network provably equivariant to any motion. MELSTM outperforms existing models in tasks like future-frame prediction and action recognition while using fewer parameters. This approach allows for more accurate and generalizable modeling of sequences involving motion.

A Reverse-Gold Theorem: Positive-Data Identification by Substrate-Bounded Contrastive Learners

The work presents a method for positive-data identification using substrate-bounded contrastive learners. It establishes a "Reverse-Gold Theorem" showing that a contrastively trained scorer can achieve PAC conditional identification from positive data alone. This identification relies on an innate recursive contrast operator and a discriminative scorer trained via InfoNCE-style contrastive estimation on self-generated perturbations. The results yield polynomial sample complexity for the identification, with bounds derived from the geometry of the contrastive loss. The framework is instantiated for context-free languages and subregular classes, leading to an open conjecture.

Depth Separation for Neural Networks under Gaussian Measure

The paper revisits depth separation for neural networks in the context of $L^2$ approximation of radial functions under the standard Gaussian measure. It demonstrates that a depth-3 ReLU network can approximate such functions within a small $L^2$ error using polynomial size. However, approximating these functions with depth-2 networks requires exponentially large weights unless the network size is also exponential in the dimension $d$. A general result shows that functions with significant high-frequency mass in their Hermite expansion resist approximation by shallow networks with bounded weights.

Is In-Context Reinforcement Learning A Posterior Sampler? An Empirical Study

The study investigates whether in-context reinforcement learning (ICRL), particularly Decision-Pretrained Transformer (DPT), functions as a Bayesian posterior sampler in practice. The researchers establish two necessary conditions for a Bayesian posterior sampler: sufficient-statistic invariance and the martingale property. Statistical hypothesis tests were used to assess if practically-trained ICRL models satisfy these conditions across Gaussian, Bernoulli, and linear bandit settings. The results consistently show that both conditions are violated, suggesting ICRL may not behave as Bayesian posterior samplers in practice. The work provides a framework for testing the Bayesian properties of ICRL.

Internal Guidance as a Sampling Method for Tilted Diffusion Models

Internal guidance is proposed as a sampling method for tilted diffusion models, diverging from existing external guidance methods. It modifies the evaluation point of the base denoiser rather than the output itself. For discrete distributions on the hypercube, the denoiser of a linearly tilted density is the base denoiser evaluated at a shifted and rescaled input. The training algorithm is modified with random diagonal rescalings to allow the denoiser to learn both in- and out-of-distribution evaluations. This approach enables a single denoiser to sample binary reward tilts, class labels, and infilling without fine-tuning or external guidance.
