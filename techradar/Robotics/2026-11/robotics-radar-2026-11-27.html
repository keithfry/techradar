The Sequence Robotics - Issue #981: How NVIDIA Isaac Turns Practice Into Robot Skills

NVIDIA Isaac functions as a development loop for teaching robots physical skills through a structured process. This involves building virtual tasks in Isaac Sim, generating policies with Isaac Lab from experience, and using pretrained models like GR00T. The process connects learned actions to physical machines using Isaac ROS and Isaac OS to maintain a responsive control loop. Physical trials then feed errors back into OSMO-coordinated experiments for iterative improvement. Ultimately, Isaac's value is in simplifying the practice-evaluate-deploy iteration for complex robotic tasks.

Volvo Cars Begins Training Hexagon’s AEON Humanoids at Ghent Plant Ahead of 2027 Production Target

Volvo Cars has introduced Hexagon Robotics' AEON humanoid robots to its Ghent, Belgium factory for initial task training. The robots are currently learning through imitation learning, beginning with precise component placement before scaling to more complex tasks. A broader factory pilot involving additional units is planned for the first half of 2027, aiming for live production deployment by mid-2027. AEON robots feature a wheeled-leg architecture, allowing them to navigate and handle payloads efficiently on factory floors. This deployment expands AEON's presence in the automotive sector, following similar trials by BMW and Schaeffler.

Lute raises $20 million to give robots a sense of touch

Lute, a physical AI startup, raised $20 million to advance foundation models that allow robots to use tactile information alongside visual data. This technology addresses limitations in current physical AI by incorporating tactile sensing for understanding grip force and material properties. The company developed Lute-0, a video-action model that learns scene changes for robotic movements, demonstrating superior performance in manipulation tasks. Lute is deploying its technology in retail and logistics, guiding system integrators in deploying physical AI. The funding will be used to expand data acquisition, improve models, and extend commercial operations.

Researchers develop 3D-printable humanoid robotic hand costing less than $650

Researchers developed the PHD-Hand, a five-fingered humanoid robotic hand costing under $650 to manufacture using 3D printing. This design utilizes a linkage-driven mechanism instead of tendon-based systems to improve dexterity, reliability, and reduced complexity. The hand features 15 independently actuated degrees of freedom and bidirectional movement, based on TPU hinges and spring steel drive rods. Prototypes have successfully demonstrated 33 grasp types and the ability to manipulate various everyday objects. The team plans to release the design files and software as open-source resources to make advanced robotic manipulation more accessible.

China Post tests 45 Robotera humanoids for parcel handling

China Post is testing 45 Robotera humanoids for parcel handling at its Hangzhou Postal District Center. The trial involves tasks such as picking up, orienting, and placing parcels of varying sizes and shapes onto induction platforms. Testing began in April 2026, focusing on parcel recognition and placement amidst the repetitive sorting line work. Robotera reported throughput increases from 500 to 1,200 parcels per hour during test periods with over 90% operational accuracy. Conflicting reports exist regarding the throughput figures and the scope of the reported efficiency.
