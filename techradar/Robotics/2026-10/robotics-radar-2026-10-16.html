The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI bets that robotic intelligence should be transferable across different hardware instead of being rebuilt for every machine. Their research focuses on connecting motor adaptation, video-prompted task learning, and self-play to achieve this goal. The core idea is to separate intelligence from the physical machine, allowing the model to learn how to understand a body rather than requiring precise pre-specification by engineers. The research involves a thought experiment to test if these capabilities can survive real-world deployment constraints like speed and safety. Ultimately, the company is making a bet on the transferability of this intelligence across diverse robotic bodies.

Multi-Dimensional Featurizers Yield Fine-Grained Steering and Data Shaping in Robot Foundation Models

Multi-dimensional featurizers are investigated to improve control over robot behavior within foundation models by offering an interpretable interface. Block-sparse featurizers (BSFs) are shown to recover multi-dimensional task-relevant features like perception and control variables from model computations. Fine-grained steering, manipulating visual perception and semantic representations, causally affects downstream action predictions. Feature-based data attribution helps isolate specific behaviors in training data, identifying a small subset of tokens sufficient for behavior removal. Ultimately, matching featurizers to representation geometry provides more effective tools for understanding and modifying robot behavior.

arXiv - cs.RO

Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle

Reliable heading estimation for road vehicles is achieved through a skylight polarization navigation method. This method globally optimizes a Rayleigh sky model using measurements from a division-of-focal-plane polarimetric camera. The framework integrates a pinhole camera model, a single-scattering Rayleigh polarization model, and a solar position algorithm to synthesize polarization distribution information. A polarization-weighted root mean square error objective function is used to compare measured and simulated patterns, enhancing robustness against occlusions. Experimental results demonstrated sub-degree RMS yaw errors and up to an 87% reduction in heading error compared to other methods.

arXiv - cs.RO

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

The work provides closed-form bounds for the performance of drone-mesh interception before any learning policy is applied. These bounds account for the uncertainty in predicted arrival points by incorporating least-squares variance and time uncertainty. A naive area ratio is shown to miss a coverage ceiling, indicating limits imposed by separation constraints. Optimal packing is achieved through hexagonal arrangements, and Gaussian assumptions are noted as conservative. The authors argue these feasibility ceilings should be calculated prior to any learning experiment and reported with results.

arXiv - cs.RO

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery (PGRR) improves final gait selection following irreversible leg damage. This method predicts multiple outcomes across repeated gait executions and uses uncertainty-aware estimates to guide online probes without resetting the robot. Across various damage scenarios, PGRR yielded a 0.106 improvement in final quality and a 0.044 increase in slot-averaged recovery quality compared to a Gaussian-process baseline. PGRR also reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points across the tested conditions. Conservative uncertainty handling was identified as the key factor contributing to these performance gains.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

The research proposes an adaptive mission planning strategy for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels (ASVs). This strategy maximizes information gathering by focusing on locations of maximum interest identified through in-situ measurements. It employs a clarity-driven ergodic search, linking clarity to differential entropy, to guide the planning. The system continuously adapts a target clarity map and re-plans routes considering the ASV's mobility restrictions. Simulations and experiments conducted at Jordan Lake demonstrated the effectiveness of this approach.

arXiv - cs.RO

Race to Recovery: Designing Advanced Safety Systems for Autonomous Driving via Learning Autonomous Racing

Existing safety systems focus on preventing instability rather than recovering from loss of control during events like icing or hydroplaning. The research trains a racing policy using reinforcement learning to learn drifting as a time-optimal maneuver, which is then adapted for recovery using minimum-time rewards. This approach defines recovery relative to a stable equilibrium and introduces Gym-Khana for controller training and evaluation. The transferred policy significantly improves recovery over baselines, outperforming both nonlinear model predictive controllers and recovery-only policies. Physical deployment on a scale model demonstrated superior recovery, including recovering from states the baseline could not handle.

arXiv - cs.RO

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Sustainably powered mobile robots aim to maximize information quality over time by optimally managing energy allocation. The study focuses on a persistent speed trajectory optimization problem framed as a periodic optimal control problem. It uses information-theoretic clarity as a metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control, including follow-on properties. The research also establishes a high-speed information limit approached as energy reserves become infinite. Simulation results from a real-world salinity characterization in the Chesapeake Bay demonstrate the practical relevance of these findings.

arXiv - cs.RO

BubbleCBF: Constructive Safety for Second-Order Systems and Application to Autonomous Robot Navigation in Unknown Environments

A constructive approach named BubbleCBF is developed to design feasible safety constraints for systems with second-order dynamics. This method constructs collision-free spheres, or "bubbles," from a signed distance function to define a velocity-dependent safety constraint. A quadratic-program safety filter is used to enforce the safe set, which is represented as the union of intersections. The method proves that the resulting controller is well-defined, continuous, and makes the set forward invariant under mild assumptions. The BubbleCBF is successfully applied to a differential-drive robot to achieve safe navigation in unknown environments.
