Automation Created a Training Problem: Upskilling the Industrial Workforce

Automation creates a skills gap because the need for new skills often outpaces training availability. Workers need new competencies, such as operating, maintaining, and programming automated systems, which are not covered by traditional training. Digital e-learning is effective for delivering knowledge across shift-based workforces, though hands-on practice remains essential for practical competence. Successful upskilling requires integrating training planning directly into the automation project timeline. Manufacturers must treat workforce upskilling as an operational priority, budgeted alongside hardware, to realize the full productivity gains of automation.

What robot builders overlook about cooling compact servo drives and controllers

Compact robotic systems face complex thermal management challenges due to the proximity of multiple heat-generating electronic components with restricted airflow. Effective thermal design requires evaluating system-level performance based on actual operating conditions, including peak loads and duty cycles, rather than just component ratings. Heat sink selection should be based on a calculated thermal budget, considering the thermal interface and mounting surface quality. Forced-air cooling effectiveness is limited by internal restrictions, and the mechanical mounting interface significantly impacts heat transfer. Ultimately, reliable thermal management demands early collaboration between thermal, mechanical, and manufacturing teams to ensure practical and reliable solutions.

New ASTM standard aims to test how robots perform in the real world

ASTM International is developing a standard to measure manipulator and mobile manipulator task performance under controlled optical-signal degradation. This proposed method will quantify how degradation in optical information impacts a robot's ability to complete tasks in real-world conditions. Degradation from factors like changing light or interference can cause downstream machine-learning models to receive poorer information, potentially leading to task failure or safety risks. The standard aims to provide a repeatable way to test performance under stress, helping developers identify system limitations before deployment. This research supports responsible real-world deployment and UN Sustainable Development Goal #8.

SPD robot training pairs virtual practice with real demos

SPD robot training utilizes simulated demonstrations to develop a dexterous bimanual policy for real tasks like Jenga. This method combines extensive virtual teleoperation with brief real teleoperated demonstrations to gather training data for two arms and two hands. Five individuals collected 75 hours of demonstrations in VR, involving a 56-DOF simulated system across various scenes and objects. The collected data was used to pretrain a 222 million parameter diffusion transformer policy before adapting it to real data. Simulation pretraining outperformed behavior cloning for these tasks, offering a practical signal for evaluating performance.
