Why a Tiny GPT Can Know Two Facts and Still Not Connect Them

A "Two-Hop Curse" occurs when language models fail to connect two separate facts, even if they are present in the training data. This weakness is demonstrated by training a model on separate one-hop relationships, which causes it to fail when asked to compose the two facts. Introducing an "identity bridge"—an example mapping an entity to itself—helps the model learn to compose the facts. However, the benefit of this fix is temporary, as performance degrades over continued training. The experiment suggests that extended training allows the model to settle into separate representations for the facts, which is the mechanism behind the failure.

In long-horizon agentic RL, tokens/sec is the wrong metric. Your trainer is waiting on stragglers

In long-horizon agentic reinforcement learning, token-per-second is an inadequate metric because training often stalls waiting for slower attempts (stragglers). The crucial metric for tracking should be the time until the next fresh, valid trainable batch is available. Strategies to address this include processing groups in completion order, managing candidate groups to bypass stragglers, and splitting sandbox work across separate CPU sets to prevent starvation. Further optimization involves updating weights incrementally and bounding the age of returned groups on benchmark workloads. These changes led to significant time reductions in batch collection for specific tasks.
