ROS Discourse General: Runtime governability in ROS 2: where should changing operating conditions be handled?

The author seeks architectural experience regarding how to handle changes in external operating conditions while a ROS 2 system is actively performing a task. ROS 2's existing mechanisms for lifecycle management and recovery are considered sufficient, and the focus is on where to represent and evaluate this changing context. Potential locations for this context include the task layer, a supervisory component, or external services. The author asks specific questions about context ownership, handling source unavailability, and introducing higher-level logic without compromising local safety. The discussion centers on the concept of "runtime governability" in autonomous systems.

Why Skydio Built a Lopsided Drone

The Skydio F10 Lightrunner is a fixed-wing camera drone featuring asymmetrical wings to accommodate a wingtip camera payload. This design necessitated uneven motor placement to balance thrust against the added drag from the camera. Engineers used modeling and optimization to achieve a viable design that balanced the camera, lift, and control requirements. The drone utilizes model predictive control to autonomously manage transitions between hovering and high-speed flight. A MegaDock system allows for the autonomous launch, recovery, and recharging of up to five F10 drones.

Robbyant launches LingBot-Touch to give robots a sense of touch

Robbyant released LingBot-Touch, an open-source tactile foundation model enabling robots to perceive touch by unifying data from magnetic, visual, and piezoresistive sensing. This unified architecture translates diverse sensor signals into a shared fingertip coordinate space, allowing different hands and action models to share tactile understanding. Integrating LingBot-Touch resulted in significant performance improvements, with action models achieving 14 to 26 percentage point gains in real-world manipulation tasks. The model is designed to be reusable across different sensors and dexterous hands, accommodating future hardware iterations.

Welcome, GR RL!

GR RL is a robot foundation model developed by ByteDance Seed, building upon the GR 3 vision language action model. It addresses the challenge of noisy human demonstrations by combining demonstration training with reinforcement learning through subsequent robot practice. The training involves three stages: filtering poor demonstrations, mirroring retained examples, and introducing practice guided by results. This research focuses on refining how robot policies are trained rather than producing a physical robot product. The model is assessed as developing, reflecting its focus on the quality of learning methods in robotics.

arXiv - cs.RO

Racing the Clock: Teaching VLNs Budget-Conditioned Object Search

The research investigates budget-conditioned object search for vision-language-navigation (VLN) models, where agents must find target instances within a specific action budget. The study compares supervising VLNs with a privileged Traveling Salesperson Problem (TSP) expert versus a room-level Bellman expert. Despite the TSP expert showing better routing performance, the Bellman-supervised VLN consistently outperforms it in learning exploration under partial observability. This suggests that effective exploration supervision should focus on decisions under partial observability rather than imitating fully known optimal routes. Experiments show the Bellman-supervised VLN finds a higher percentage of reachable targets compared to the other methods.

arXiv - cs.RO

Learning Latent Goal Costs for Visual World-Model Planning with an Expanding Curriculum

Image-goal planning with frozen visual world models benefits from learning latent goal costs. A residual adapter is fitted onto frozen I-JEPA embeddings to improve this latent goal cost signal. Planners collect trajectories from expanding regions to learn from successful and failed rollouts without task-specific labels. Experiments on DotWall and PointMaze showed success rates increasing from 54% to 98% and 82% to 96%, respectively. This demonstrates that planning experience can refine a frozen visual representation's goal cost.

Talk to Act: A Synthetic Multi-Turn Dialogue Benchmark for Human Intent Extraction and Action Planning in Robotics

A synthetic multi-turn dialogue dataset is introduced to benchmark human intent extraction and action planning in human-robot interaction. The work frames intent extraction as a tool-calling problem, testing small language models (270M to 8B parameters) on these tasks without task-specific fine-tuning. Results indicate that these compact models achieve reasonable performance in intent extraction and action planning. This suggests that smaller, locally deployable models offer a useful baseline for conversational robotic systems. Further fine-tuning and more realistic data are anticipated to improve performance for real-world applications.

arXiv - cs.RO

Grounding Object-to-Object Hazard Reasoning into 3D Risk Fields for Task-Conditioned Safety

A framework based on vision-language models infers task- and scene-dependent safety constraints for robot manipulation by analyzing object interactions. It uses RGB-D observations and natural language instructions to determine if a task should be rejected or to identify preventable object-pair hazards. Open-vocabulary segmentation and depth information are then used to map detected objects into 3D and construct continuous risk fields representing hazardous regions. These risk fields are integrated with an obstacle avoidance module to correct robot motions during execution. The system is evaluated on preventable hazards, rejected tasks, and safe variants in both simulation and real-world settings.

arXiv - cs.RO

Natural Swing-Up of an Underpowered Double Pendulum

Robots with weak motors should utilize their natural dynamics instead of resisting them. The research explores swinging up a direct-drive planar two-link robot whose motors provide only 6% of the shoulder gravity torque. Energy injection is analyzed by splitting the input torque into a power-neutral steering component and a scalar pumping input that changes energy. A linear program is used to allocate this torque to maximize pumping subject to torque limits and a control-Lyapunov constraint. Experiments confirmed swing-ups along the natural modes and compared different allocation strategies.

X-Nav Technical Report: Real-World Long-Horizon Indoor-to-Outdoor Navigation on a Humanoid Robot

X-Nav presents a system for long-horizon indoor-to-outdoor navigation for humanoid robots based on language instructions. It utilizes a data engine to collect navigation data and a navigation model, MELIOR, to predict actions or navigation goals from RGB images. The agentic workflow, X-Navarch, translates human instructions into sub-tasks, using MELIOR for target navigation and obstacle avoidance. The system successfully navigated a mission of about a hundred meters by adapting the model with data from digital twins. MELIOR also achieves competitive results on established navigation benchmarks.

arXiv - cs.RO

On the Utility of Pre-Trained Visual Representations for Policy Learning of Contact-Rich Insertion Tasks

Visuomotor imitation learning is data-inefficient and brittle to visual shifts, suggesting pre-trained visual representations could help policy learning. The study investigates this by comparing representations from vision foundation models against explicit contact-centric representations for insertion tasks. Testing across controlled perturbations showed that task dependence is more subtle than previously thought, with different representations performing differently even on nearly identical tasks. Results indicate that spatially-grounded, contact-centric information is distinctly valuable for contact-rich policy learning. This work motivates further research into contact perception for visuomotor policy learning.

arXiv - cs.RO

AI-guided robotic in situ fabrication of curvature- and tissue-adaptive wireless bioelectronics

An AI-guided robotic in situ fabrication platform is presented for creating wireless wound electrotherapy devices directly onto transparent wound dressings. The system employs AI for wound segmentation to determine geometry and tissue curvature, which are then incorporated into the electromagnetic design of the receiving coils. This method accounts for both anatomical curvature and tissue dielectric loading to correct for shifts in resonant behavior. A printable silver ink was used to fabricate these multilayer wireless devices on curved materials. The work establishes a framework for geometry-adaptive wireless bioelectronics through a perception-to-design-to-fabrication process.
