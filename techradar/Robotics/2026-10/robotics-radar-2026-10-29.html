Interview with Faraday Future chairman: Moving from Chinese robot sourcing to US manufacturing

Faraday Future aims to build a US robotics business by establishing a US robot factory by the end of 2026, with the first US-assembled robot scheduled for February 2027. The company seeks to localize production to enhance regulatory compliance, supply chain resilience, and quality control, rather than just moving assembly lines. This transition is supported by leveraging existing EV manufacturing infrastructure to create synergies with robotics development. Faraday Future views vehicles and robotics as complementary parts of a larger physical AI ecosystem, sharing core technologies like AI, perception, and computing. Success is defined by establishing a sustainable US-based physical AI and robotics ecosystem through product launches, manufacturing capacity, and supply chain growth.

Living digital twins combine high-fidelity operational data with control systems to mirror industrial plant dynamism. AGIBOT Introduces Worken, New AI Models and G2 Max at Innovation Day 2026

AGIBOT introduced Worken, a framework to measure robot productivity using the formula: Worken = Active Robots × Autonomous Working Hours × Output Efficiency Ratio. The company also unveiled new hardware like AHA3, which integrates locomotion, interaction, and task execution capabilities. Newer AI models include GO-3 Preview for reasoning and action, and WITA-Omni 1.0 for human-robot interaction. Audi Tests Agile Robots’ Mobile Humanoids for Automotive Parts Sequencing

Audi is testing Agile Robots’ mobile humanoids for sequencing automotive parts at its Ingolstadt Production Lab. The project involves picking components from containers and arranging them for assembly, addressing a material-supply operation scale of one million picks daily. The system utilizes dual-arm manipulation and a mobile base to handle varied, unsorted components in logistics environments. Virtual testing using a digital twin supported by NVIDIA Omniverse and Dassault Systèmes is used to assess feasibility before wider deployment. Success in the final deployment will depend on consistent physical performance metrics like pick rates and operating costs.

A soft robot could help doctors diagnose and treat disorders of the small intestine

MIT engineers developed a robotic enteroscope inspired by snakes to navigate the small intestine for easier diagnosis and treatment. This soft robot uses multifunctional actuators and textured coatings to move through narrow passages by mimicking snake locomotion. It is equipped with tools for imaging, drug delivery, and taking biopsies of polyps. The system allows external controllers to guide the robot through the intestine. Animal studies demonstrated the robot's capability to travel several feet while performing these interventions.

Maxinsights raises $30 million to build training data platform for physical AI

Maxinsights raised $30 million to expand its physical AI data platform, which pays contractors to record real-world activities for robot and AI training data. The company has collected over 2 million hours of human activity, with 1.5 million hours being model-ready, ensuring exposure to real-world variations. They developed MaxLoop for footage quality monitoring and MaxGlove for capturing tactile information like grip force. This data is crucial for training models like Genesis AI, which has used Maxinsights data for its GENE-26.5 model. Maxinsights emphasizes that capturing real-world data, including edge cases, is essential for deploying robots effectively.
