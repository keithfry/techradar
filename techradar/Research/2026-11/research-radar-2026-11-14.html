[2611.05275] Route Anything: A Study of Model and Policy Routing for General-Purpose AI Systems

The work presents Route Anything, a framework for studying model and policy routing in general-purpose AI systems. It develops a theoretical foundation for optimal routing based on quality-cost trade-offs and the information available to the router. The researchers constructed an omni-modal trajectory matrix from 25 benchmarks across various modalities to facilitate this investigation. Experiments show that learned routers and per-input oracle routing offer performance and efficiency improvements over fixed candidates. Overall, routing significantly increases efficiency by an average of 8.82x compared to a reference model.

Zugwise: Domain-Aware Game Reconstruction for Digitizing Handwritten Chess Scoresheets

Zugwise rephrases digitizing handwritten chess scoresheets as a game reconstruction problem rather than just a legality check. It uses computer-chess machinery, like quiescence search, to determine if the scoresheets plausibly represent a human game. The system employs an absurdity detector and backward search to identify and flag silent errors in the scoresheets. By incorporating evidence from second-player sheets and human review, the method improves the accuracy of reconstructing the correct scores. Learned signals enhance the system's ability to fix errors, improving the rate of correct replacement decisions.

Cleaning an Agent's History Hurts When the World Still Holds the Error

Tool-using language-model agents accumulate mistakes in their history, and how this history is managed impacts subsequent performance. When history is cleaned without accounting for the world's state, the resulting information can be detrimental. Experiments show that simply stating a correction can reduce performance when the actual world state contains an error. The effect of history edits depends crucially on the world it describes, suggesting that making history appear clean while errors persist can worsen the agent's state. Therefore, cleaning history must be contextually aware of the existing world state to maintain optimal performance.

Together Compiler: Continual Compilation for AI Inference

Together Compiler (TKCC) is introduced as a novel, continual agent loop designed for optimizing AI inference workloads. It addresses the challenge of continual optimization by tackling the trade-off between measurement throughput and fidelity in existing systems. TKCC employs a Tiered Persistent Engine and a cost model to manage engine launch overhead and parallelism configurations. Performance evaluations show TKCC outperforms existing methods significantly, achieving up to 1.7x and 2.6x higher Tokens Per Second (TPS) on various frontier models. Furthermore, TKCC demonstrates generalization beyond language model inference, serving trillions of tokens across a production fleet.

When Should Semantic Priors Enter Partially-Identified Causal Discovery?

Observational causal discovery often faces unresolved or unstable causal directions. Large language models can offer semantic information to resolve these directions, but incorrect proposals risk invalidating downstream inferences. The study examines decisions regarding where to query, which proposals to admit, and which causal graphs to retain. Semantic information improves recovery over random orientations, but targeting unstable edges is not necessarily advantageous. The value of semantic data depends on calibration and reference adequacy, which are key limitations.

SynthCorp: A Platform for Creating Benchmarks for Data Agents

SynthCorp is a platform designed to create customizable data estates and benchmarks by simulating how business events alter corporate data over time. It achieves this by combining relational databases, document stores, and a data catalog with evaluation tasks and executable gold programs. The platform synthesizes reusable event types, schedules them to maintain data consistency, and executes the simulation to log all operations. Subsequently, it generates analytical tasks and verifies gold programs using causal provenance from the simulation log, exposing reasoning failures in data agents. The work aims to provide more challenging benchmarks for developing enterprise data agents.

Rational Use of Time for Social Coordination in Humans and AI

Human social interactions rely on using implicit temporal cues, such as pauses, to infer mental states. The study investigates how humans and AI use these temporal cues for dynamic social coordination in a card game requiring shared goals without explicit communication. Human behavior is best explained by a Bayesian strategy inferring partner beliefs from timing, mirroring how frontier AI models operate. However, tested AI models failed to efficiently adapt their behaviors to partners' implicit signals during the game. This research highlights a challenge for AI to coordinate effectively by learning to interpret and adapt to implicit temporal signals in dynamic social settings.

Leaderboard Hacking: Preference-Based Model Evaluations are Vulnerable to Manipulation

Arena-style rankings of language models, based on pairwise comparisons, are vulnerable to manipulation in online leaderboards. Unscrupulous providers can employ strategies like strategic voting, strategic prompting, and strategic nomination to unfairly boost a model's rank. These manipulation tactics can result in significant rank inflation, potentially boosting a model by up to 45 places. These vulnerabilities exist for any preference-based evaluation utilizing the Bradley-Terry model with open prompting or voting mechanisms. The authors describe methods to guard against these attacks on leaderboard evaluations.

Robust Task-Aware State Estimation from Pre-trained Perception

Agents using pre-trained perception models face uncertainty in state estimation, which can lead to unsafe decisions if predictions are treated as ground truth. The authors propose a method to construct a $\theta$-confidence Minimal Belief Set ($\text{MBS}_\theta$) by using the perception model's output probabilities. This approach enhances robustness by considering only the most likely states while remaining computationally tractable. A novel three-layered belief system is used to efficiently approximate state probabilities from feature predictions and task constraints. The Probability-Ranked Valid State Search ($\text{PRaVaSS}$) algorithm constructs this $\text{MBS}_\theta$ with correctness guarantees and polynomial complexity.

When Is Proof-Step Verification Worth Its Cost? Operating Requirements of Certify-or-Decline Verification for Open-Weight Mathematical Reasoning

Proof-step verification uses LLM judges to certify or decline solver steps for open-weight mathematical reasoning. Evaluation across GPQA, AIME-2025, and MATH-500 shows certification increases answer-key-match precision significantly. However, gains on MATH-500 are modest, and failure modes like invalid proof construction without judgment are present. Self-consistency selectors and external-LLM acceptance show varied precision results compared to the verifier's performance. The study advocates for separate reporting of execution failures and independently assessed proof validity.

GenPFN: An In-Context Tabular Foundation Model Approach for Augmenting Small Datasets in Engineering

GenPFN is a training-free candidate generator that uses a pretrained tabular Prior-Data Fitted Network (TabPFN) classifier. It interpolates between desired designs and refines candidates by employing gated classifier gradients and Gaussian noise. The process involves evaluating candidates against problem constraints and objective functions to augment small engineering datasets. Comparisons show GenPFN outperforms other generators in class satisfaction and reduces estimation errors significantly. This method effectively augments limited datasets to support more accurate predictive model training in engineering.
