Improving OCR Faithfulness via Gated and Attenuated On-Policy Distillation

Vision-language models can compromise OCR transcription faithfulness by rewriting anomalous text. The proposed GAD-RL method adaptively regulates teacher supervision during post-training based on student performance. It achieves this by disabling distillation for high-reward outputs and attenuating distillation strength as the group reward increases. The method also weights forward KL by the student's token probability to moderate local updates. On Qwen3.5-2B, GAD-RL outperformed other methods on OCR faithfulness benchmarks.

MoFlow: Multi-Objective Agentic Workflow Generation

MoFlow generates agentic workflows optimized simultaneously for multiple objectives like accuracy, cost, latency, robustness, and consistency. It frames workflow generation as a multi-objective Markov decision process, utilizing Convex-Hull Monte Carlo Tree Search with optimistic set-valued backups. This approach allows each node to store a set of reachable trade-offs rather than a single weighted score. Consequently, MoFlow can provide any desired workflow by lookup without requiring retraining. Evaluation shows MoFlow achieves the highest average hypervolume across tested benchmarks compared to baselines.

AI Agents are Vulnerable to Radicalization

Large language models can influence beliefs, and this study simulates agent interactions to test radicalization pathways. Radicalization occurs through resonance, where an influencer reinforces existing beliefs, and persuasion, where a belief is promoted. Resonance effects are found to be significantly stronger than persuasion in causing radicalization across affective and behavioral metrics. Different influence tactics yield varying levels of radicalization, but resonance propagates to related beliefs. These findings highlight the susceptibility of AI agents, especially in multi-agent systems, to radicalization when messages align with existing views.

CARAT: Do Materials LLMs Reason or Recite?

The paper investigates whether materials Large Language Models (LLMs) reason from crystal structure or merely recite existing information. It introduces CARAT, a method that evaluates reasoning by fixing questions and answers across eight views and incorporating techniques like answer masking and evidence injection. Experiments show that grounded views are significantly more valuable than formula inputs for these models. The study also reveals that while LLMs can mimic reasoning through shortcuts, they often rely on repetition rather than true understanding. Ultimately, the research demonstrates that both reasoning and recitation are learnable capabilities in materials LLMs.

Examining Variation in How Guided AI Tutors Resolve Student Impasses

The study analyzed student interactions with a guided LLM chemistry tutor to examine how it resolves student impasses. Researchers identified three types of impasses—conceptual errors, expressed uncertainty, and help-seeking—across many student turns. Simulating different tutoring conditions revealed that the guidance provided varied significantly based on the impasse type and depth. Addressing student errors proved more beneficial for recovery than scripted questioning, and impasse signals can be used for real-time, state-sensitive assistance in learning analytics.

Beyond Mode Collapse: Generating Diverse Synthetic Expert Conversations via Generative Flow Networks

Generating diverse, high-quality synthetic data for post-training LLMs is challenging due to mode collapse from direct prompting. The method proposes using Generative Flow Networks (GFlowNets) to generate this data by modeling the latent conversation structure using a Gaussian mixture density over key interaction features. Training GFlowNets to capture these features allows for sampling expert strategies proportionally to their frequency in the training data. This approach produces synthetic data with superior fidelity and mode coverage compared to reinforcement learning or end-to-end LLM baselines. Classifiers trained on this synthetic data provide a stronger signal for downstream outcome prediction tasks.

An AI agent was used to study generalized Blaschke curves as a setting for AI-assisted mathematical rediscovery. The agent received numerical coordinates from boundary configurations of a fixed-degree-four Blaschke product. Aligned Data Can Induce Misalignment via Context Confusion

Aligned training data can induce misalignment in other contexts through a phenomenon called context confusion. This occurs because alignment is context-dependent, meaning alignment in one situation may lead to inappropriate outputs in another. The study demonstrates context confusion across domains like Gender Equality, Privacy, and Physical Safety. This confusion causes narrow misalignment, which can be reduced by using targeted alignment data or in-context learning during inference. Mechanistically, context confusion arises because different domain queries undergo similar representational shifts during fine-tuning, causing learned features to transfer inappropriately.

EHR2Trace is a system designed to convert Electronic Health Record (EHR) data from various sources into traceable patient events for model training. It achieves this by linking events to source records, separating event time from information availability, and distinguishing medication events. Conformal Adversarial Generative Ensemble

Conformal Adversarial Generative Ensemble (CAGE) is a framework designed to improve time series forecasting reliability by addressing the influence of extreme forecasts. It combines generative modeling, adversarial discrimination, and conformal prediction techniques. CAGE uses multiple generative models to generate initial forecasts, which are then assessed by a discriminative component. P-values from nonconformity scores dynamically adjust model weights, ensuring only credible predictions influence the final ensemble. Empirical tests on milk collection and public health data show CAGE outperforms traditional ensemble methods, particularly with noisy data.

This technique allows warm-starting expensive spectral solves by continuing over sample size and bandwidth.

Activation-Conditioned Self-Distillation

Activation-Conditioned Self-Distillation (ACSD) introduces a method for on-policy self-distillation using a model as a teacher for dense supervision. ACSD extracts a steering vector by contrasting activations of self-generated trajectories that lead to correct answers with others. A frozen base model applies this vector to guide the student's learning from its next-token distributions on student prefixes. Outcome verification is used for direction construction, and distillation requires no external reference text or teacher parameter updates. This method achieves high accuracy on mathematical benchmarks compared to baseline methods.

Acceleration of Diffusion Language Model through Discrete Average Generator

The Discrete Average Generator is introduced as an extension of MeanFlow to Continuous-Time Markov Chains to address the challenge of efficient few-step generation in discrete diffusion models. It defines an average generator as the normalized increment of the transition kernel over a time interval. The method utilizes a self-consistency identity derived from this generator to form the training objective, which aligns with standard diffusion model training. This approach allows for efficient training and inference by yielding a closed-form expression when projected onto per-coordinate marginals. Experimental results show significant acceleration, achieving up to $16\times$ speedup on OpenWebText generation while maintaining comparable performance.

The paper investigates the lower bounds for online learning when the oracle provides linear information. Bandits with Multiple Optimal Arms: Minimax Regret and Non-Adaptivit

The study investigates multi-armed bandits where multiple arms are optimal, acknowledging that decision problems often have several correct answers. The research establishes a minimax regret bound of $\tilde{O}\left(\frac{K-A}{\sqrt{KA}}\sqrt{T}\right)$ for $K$-armed bandits with $A$ optimal arms, which improves upon previous results. A matching lower bound confirms that this rate is nearly minimax-optimal. The findings indicate that knowing the number of optimal arms, $A$, up to $\tilde{O}(1)$ is necessary for achieving near-optimal regret. Overall, the work provides a minimax characterization for bandits with multiple optimal solutions.

Sharp Statistical Rates for Asynchronous TD Learning with Markovian Data

The paper studies the performance of the last iterate of standard tabular temporal-difference (TD) learning using a single Markov reward process. It establishes a bound on the sup-norm error for last-iterate TD to be at most $\varepsilon$ with high probability. The required number of transitions is bounded by a term dependent on $H^3/(\mu_{\min}\varepsilon^2)$ and $t_{\operatorname{mix}}/\mu_{\min}$. This rate holds for both constant and decreasing step-size schedules. The result is applicable to non-reversible chains and arbitrary initial state distributions, leveraging an anchored local Poisson equation for control.

Any-dimensional machine learning models can be used to train PDE solvers in small dimensions and apply them to higher-dimensional PDEs in a zero-shot manner. Minimax rates for learning spectral Barron functions by deep ReLU neural networks

The study investigates the approximation and learning rates for spectral Barron functions using deep neural networks with ReLU activations. It establishes an approximation rate of $\widetilde{\mathcal{O}} (S^{-\frac{1}{2}-\frac{s}{d}})$ for $d$-dimensional spectral Barron functions with smoothness index $s$. This convergence rate is proven to be minimax optimal up to logarithmic factors.

Inference-time alignment of flow and diffusion-based models is necessary for flexible generative modeling, stemming from Doob's $h$-transform. Asymptotic Properties of Support Vector Machines in High-Dimension, Low-Sample-Size Settings under a Spiked Model

The paper examines the asymptotic properties of Support Vector Machines (SVMs) in high-dimension, low-sample-size settings under a spiked model. It demonstrates that the standard geometric representation of HDLSS data is invalid under the spiked model. The Gram matrix of HDLSS data converges to a random matrix, indicating data converges to a random configuration in a space defined by the number of spikes. The misclassification rates of the SVM do not tend to zero, meaning the consistency property is not generally held. A spike-corrected SVM (SC-SVM) is proposed to restore consistency, and the sample size growth is shown to be essential for projection-based procedures to succeed.
