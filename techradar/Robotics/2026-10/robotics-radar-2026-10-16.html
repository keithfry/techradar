The Sequence Robotics - Issue #952: One Brain, Many Bodies: Inside Skild AI's Robotics Bet

Skild AI bets that robotic intelligence should be transferable across different hardware instead of being rebuilt for every machine. Their research focuses on motor adaptation, video-prompted task learning, and self-play to achieve this. The core idea is to separate intelligence from the physical machine, allowing the model to learn how to understand different bodies. This approach aims to let the model learn what a body is, rather than requiring engineers to specify every detail perfectly beforehand. The ultimate test is whether these learned capabilities can function reliably within real-world deployment constraints.

Multi-Dimensional Featurizers Yield Fine-Grained Steering and Data Shaping in Robot Foundation Models

Multi-dimensional featurizers, such as block-sparse featurizers, are investigated to recover interpretable structure in robot foundation models. These featurizers successfully capture multi-dimensional task-relevant features like perception, language, and control variables underlying the model's computation. Fine-grained steering experiments demonstrate that manipulating these features causally alters downstream action predictions. Furthermore, feature-based data attribution allows for isolating specific behaviors, such as failure recovery, within the training data. Ultimately, matching featurizers to representation geometry offers a more effective method for understanding and controlling robot behavior.

arXiv - cs.RO

Navigation by Skylight Polarization via Rayleigh Model Optimization on a Road Vehicle

A skylight polarization navigation method estimates vehicle heading by optimizing a Rayleigh sky model against polarimetric camera measurements. This framework integrates a pinhole camera model, a single-scattering Rayleigh polarization model, and a solar position algorithm to synthesize expected polarization images. A polarization-weighted root mean square error objective function is used to compare measured and simulated patterns while mitigating occlusions. Experiments on urban road trajectories yielded sub-degree RMS yaw errors, ranging from $0.718^{\circ}$ to $0.889^{\circ}$. This approach improves upon other methods by reducing RMS heading error by up to 87% and enhancing robustness to sky occlusion.

Feasibility Ceilings Before Learning: Sizing and Timing Bounds for Drone-Mesh Interception

The paper establishes feasibility ceilings for drone-mesh interception before learning begins, focusing on sizing and timing bounds. It provides closed-form expressions and Monte Carlo validation for coverage and uncertainty based on predicted arrival points. The analysis reveals that naive area ratios underestimate coverage, and optimal packing arrangements are hexagonal. Analytic placement achieves 85% of the configuration's potential, while learning methods achieve significantly less. The authors argue these ceilings should be computed and reported alongside all learning results.

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery Improves Final Gait Selection after Irreversible Leg Damage

Persistent Profile-Guided Reset-Free Recovery (PGRR) is introduced to improve gait selection after irreversible leg damage in robotics. PGRR predicts multiple outcomes over repeated gait executions and uses uncertainty-aware estimates to guide online probes without resetting the robot. Across 108 matched MuJoCo tasks involving various leg damage scenarios, PGRR resulted in a 0.106 improvement in final quality and a 0.044 improvement in slot-averaged recovery quality. This method also reduced low-quality failures by 27.8 percentage points, with the gains observed in all three damage classes. Conservative uncertainty handling was identified as the key factor contributing to these performance improvements.

arXiv - cs.RO

Clarity-Driven Adaptive Mission Planning for a Solar-Powered Autonomous Surface Vessel

A methodology is presented for adaptive mission planning using a clarity-driven approach for solar-powered autonomous surface vessels (ASVs). This strategy maximizes information gathering while respecting mobility constraints imposed by limited renewable power. It employs a clarity-driven ergodic search, where clarity is mathematically linked to differential entropy. An online target clarity map is adapted based on in-situ measurements, guiding a path planner that continuously re-plans based on ASV speed limitations. Simulations and experiments on a solar-powered ASV demonstrated the effectiveness of this adaptive planning approach.

Optimally Trading Energy for Information through Speed Trajectory Optimization of Sustainably Powered Mobile Robots

Mobile robots powered sustainably can optimize energy allocation to maximize information quality over time. The research frames this as a persistent speed trajectory optimization problem involving a super-linear propulsion cost and dynamic information gain. The study uses clarity as an information metric to derive necessary conditions for optimal control. It establishes follow-on properties characterizing the optimal control behavior and a high-speed information limit. Simulations demonstrate the practical relevance of these results by applying them to real-world environmental data.

arXiv - cs.RO

BubbleCBF: Constructive Safety for Second-Order Systems and Application to Autonomous Robot Navigation in Unknown Environments

The research develops a constructive approach to design feasible safety constraints for systems with second-order dynamics, addressing challenges where simple position constraints lead to infeasible decelerations. This is achieved by constructing collision-free spheres, or "bubbles," from a signed distance function to define velocity-dependent safety constraints. These constraints are enforced using a quadratic-program safety filter, which is proven to be well-defined and forward invariant under mild assumptions. The method is demonstrated by applying BubbleCBF to a differential-drive robot to achieve safe autonomous navigation in unknown environments. Sufficient conditions for feasibility under bounded inputs are also derived for this approach.
