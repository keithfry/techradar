The Sequence Robotics - Issue #971: Robots Should Learn on the Job

Tutor Intelligence is developing a method for robots to learn through on-the-job experience guided by remote human tutors. This process uses real-world work interventions as training data to improve robot intelligence. A system involving a 100-robot research fleet and a real-to-sim-to-real loop called TRACE provides additional practice. The company's goal is to create an apprenticeship model where the work itself supplies part of the learning curriculum. The core questions involve the efficacy of real-world practice and the reliability of simulated learning results.

Viam Launchpad aims to accelerate robotics deployments

Viam offers a software platform that acts as an abstraction layer for building and deploying hardware systems, enabling robotics startups to move quickly from prototypes to market. This platform helps founders overcome obstacles in building and deploying physical AI by providing the necessary infrastructure. Viam Launchpad provides an intensive program offering founders technical support, resources, and access to hardware labs to facilitate commercial implementation. The platform has already helped startups like Gambit achieve real-world deployments by streamlining the process of connecting hardware with software. Ultimately, Viam aims to accelerate the deployment of physical AI by lowering the barriers for robotics entrepreneurs.

Helping robots fit into the human workplace

Robust AI develops robots designed to complement human capabilities in factory and warehouse settings. Their mobile robot, Carter, is designed to fit alongside humans, featuring force-sensitive handlebars and AI vision for intuitive human control. The company prioritizes systems where humans have agency and input to guide the overall process. Robots are deployed to assist with tasks like picking, stocking, and moving items in logistics and manufacturing. The development philosophy focuses on creating systems that accelerate human tasks rather than simply replacing them.

Welcome, LingBot-Touch!

LingBot Touch is a software foundation model from Robbyant, focused specifically on touch, positioning it separately from physical robot hands or humanoid platforms. It was trained on nearly 5,000 hours of touch recordings from gloves and robot hands, making tactile data central to its identity. The model is intended to complement other robot software rather than function as an independent controller, scoring a Humanoid Guide Brain Score of 5 out of 10. It is best understood as a specialized component within a larger embodied AI system, distinguished from the surrounding hardware and control software. The project highlights growing research attention to tactile data in embodied AI.

Agile Robots raised $500 million in a Series F round, achieving an $8.5 billion valuation. GXO targets 1,000 Apptronik Apollo robots, Koetsier reports

GXO Logistics has a letter of intent with Apptronik to deploy up to 1,000 Apollo humanoids starting in 2027. This four-year plan targets a cost of $15 an hour for the robots. The planned work involves tasks such as picking, packing, and sorting goods within warehouses. The deployment emphasizes the need for dexterity, reliable manipulation, speed, uptime, and autonomy. GXO has previously conducted over 45 humanoid pilots across various vendors.
